# Microbiology: the organisms behind infectious disease

This branch introduces the living things (and not-quite-living things) that cause infectious disease: bacteria, viruses, toxins, parasites and fungi, and how they spread. It is the first of three foundation branches for the [vaccines course](../immunology/vaccines/README.md). It covers only what is needed to understand vaccines, not the whole of microbiology.

**Prerequisites:** none. Start here if you are new to biology.

## Suggested reading order

Read the lessons in order. Lessons 02 (bacteria) and 03 (viruses) can be read in either order. Lesson 06 (infection and transmission) leads into [immunology](../immunology/README.md) and then [epidemiology](../epidemiology/README.md).

## Key studies & sources

Every source below was checked to exist (DOI, court record or official page). Notes summarise what each found and its main strengths or limitations.

### Peer-reviewed studies

- [RTS,S Clinical Trials Partnership, *Lancet* 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)60721-8): Phase 3 trial of the RTS,S malaria vaccine in seven African countries; partial protection that waned, improved by a booster. Large, multi-site; industry (GSK) and PATH funded.
- [Datoo et al., *Lancet* 2024 (R21 trial)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)02511-4): Phase 3 trial of the R21/Matrix-M malaria vaccine; around 75% efficacy over 12 months in seasonal sites. Strength: large randomised trial; limitation: shorter follow-up than RTS,S; co-developed by Oxford and Serum Institute of India.

### Official reports and guidance

- [CDC, *Epidemiology and Prevention of Vaccine-Preventable Diseases* (the "Pink Book")](https://www.cdc.gov/pinkbook/hcp/table-of-contents/index.html): Free reference with a chapter on each pathogen and vaccine; US perspective.
- [WHO fact sheets on infectious diseases](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets): Short, regularly updated global summaries of each disease's burden and transmission.

## References & further reading

- **Beginner.** Paul de Kruif, *Microbe Hunters* (1926). Classic, lively (if dated) stories of Leeuwenhoek, Pasteur, Koch and others.
- **Beginner.** Carl Zimmer, *A Planet of Viruses* (2011; 3rd ed. 2021). Short essays on what viruses are and how they shape life.
- **Beginner.** Dorothy H. Crawford, *Viruses: A Very Short Introduction* (Oxford University Press). Compact and reliable.
- **Beginner.** Ed Yong, *I Contain Multitudes* (2016). The microbiome: why most microbes are not enemies.
- **Beginner.** Steven Johnson, *The Ghost Map* (2006). John Snow and the 1854 cholera outbreak.
- **Advanced.** Patrick R. Murray, Ken S. Rosenthal and Michael A. Pfaller, *Medical Microbiology*. Standard medical-school textbook.
- **Advanced.** Madigan et al., *Brock Biology of Microorganisms*. Comprehensive general microbiology textbook.
