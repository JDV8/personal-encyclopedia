# Immunology: how the body defends itself

This branch explains the immune system from first principles: barriers and innate immunity, inflammation and fever, antigens and their presentation, B cells and antibodies, T cells, memory, passive and active immunity, and what happens when immunity goes wrong. It ends by opening the main course on [vaccines](vaccines/README.md), which sits inside this folder because vaccination is applied immunology.

**Prerequisites:** [Microbiology](../microbiology/README.md), especially [cells](../microbiology/01-cells-and-the-chemistry-of-life.md) and [infection and transmission](../microbiology/06-infection-and-transmission.md).

## Suggested reading order

Read lessons 01 to 09 in order, then read the [epidemiology](../epidemiology/README.md) branch, then enter the [vaccines](vaccines/README.md) course. If you are short of time, lessons 01, 05, 07 and 09 are the essentials.

## Key studies & sources

Every source below was checked to exist (DOI, court record or official page). Notes summarise what each found and its main strengths or limitations.

### Peer-reviewed studies

- [Offit et al., *Pediatrics* 2002](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.109.1.124): Argued that infants have a very large theoretical capacity to respond to vaccine antigens. Influential in the "too many too soon" debate; a theoretical calculation, and the first author co-invented a rotavirus vaccine.
- [Prymula et al., *Lancet* 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(09)61208-3): Randomised trial: preventive paracetamol at vaccination reduced fever but also lowered antibody responses to several vaccines. Industry (GSK) funded; findings shaped advice against routine preventive antipyretics.
- [Lal et al., *NEJM* 2015 (ZOE-50)](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1501184): ZOE-50 trial of the recombinant shingles vaccine: about 97% efficacy in adults 50+. Large and randomised; GSK funded.
- [Hammitt et al., *NEJM* 2022](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2110275): Trial of nirsevimab, a long-acting monoclonal antibody for infants against RSV; 74.5% efficacy against medically attended RSV infection. Manufacturer funded.
- [McNeil et al., *J Allergy Clin Immunol* 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaci.2015.07.048): Vaccine Safety Datalink estimate of anaphylaxis after vaccination: about 1.3 per million doses. Large active-surveillance data; may miss cases not coded in records.

### Systematic reviews

- [Higgins et al., *BMJ* 2016 (WHO-commissioned review)](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.i5170): WHO-commissioned review of non-specific effects of BCG, DTP and measles vaccines on child mortality; found possible beneficial effects of BCG and measles vaccines and inconclusive evidence on DTP. Mostly observational studies with high risk of bias.

### Notable critical or minority sources

- [Mogensen et al., *EBioMedicine* 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2017.01.041): Observational study from Guinea-Bissau reporting higher mortality among infants receiving early DTP. Part of Aaby and Benn's non-specific-effects research; natural-experiment design but small numbers and potential confounding; disputed but taken seriously by WHO's advisers.

## References & further reading

- **Beginner.** Philipp Dettmer, *Immune: A Journey into the Mysterious System That Keeps You Alive* (2021). Vivid, illustrated, accurate introduction by the creator of Kurzgesagt.
- **Beginner.** Daniel M. Davis, *The Beautiful Cure* (2018). The discoveries behind modern immunology.
- **Beginner.** Paul Klenerman, *The Immune System: A Very Short Introduction* (Oxford University Press, 2017).
- **Intermediate.** Lauren Sompayrac, *How the Immune System Works* (many editions). Short, conversational overview used by students.
- **Intermediate.** Abul K. Abbas, Andrew H. Lichtman and Shiv Pillai, *Basic Immunology*. Concise textbook.
- **Advanced.** Kenneth Murphy and Casey Weaver, *Janeway's Immunobiology*. The standard advanced textbook.
