# How vaccines work

This section explains what vaccines are and how they are built: the core idea, the main platforms (live attenuated, inactivated, toxoid, subunit and recombinant, conjugate, viral vector and mRNA), adjuvants, the other ingredients in the vial, manufacturing, the cold chain, and how routes, doses and combinations are chosen. It assumes the [immunology](../../README.md) lessons.

**Prerequisites:** [Immunological memory](../../07-immunological-memory.md); [Antigens and antigen presentation](../../04-antigens-and-antigen-presentation.md); [Herd immunity](../../../epidemiology/04-herd-immunity.md).

## Suggested reading order

Read 01–02 first. Lessons 03–09 cover one platform each and can be read in any order. Then read 10 (adjuvants) and 11 (ingredients), which underpin several controversies, and 12–14 for the practical side.

## Key studies & sources

Every source below was checked to exist (DOI, court record or official page). Notes summarise what each found and its main strengths or limitations.

### Peer-reviewed studies

- [[c:polack2020]]: Phase 3 trial of the Pfizer–BioNTech mRNA vaccine; about 95% efficacy against symptomatic COVID-19 over a median two months. Randomised and large; manufacturer funded; short follow-up.
- [[c:baden2021]]: Phase 3 trial of the Moderna mRNA vaccine; 94.1% efficacy. Co-funded by the US government (BARDA, NIAID) and Moderna.
- [[c:henaorestrepo2017]]: Ring-vaccination trial of the rVSV-ZEBOV Ebola vaccine in Guinea; very high efficacy. Innovative design under outbreak conditions; randomisation of delayed vaccination was dropped partway, which critics noted.
- [[c:greinacher2021]]: First description of VITT after the AstraZeneca vaccine, with anti-PF4 antibodies. Small case series, quickly confirmed elsewhere.
- [[c:whitney2003]]: US surveillance showing falls in invasive pneumococcal disease after the 7-valent conjugate vaccine, including herd effects in adults. Large active surveillance; observational.
- [[c:klein2010]]: Vaccine Safety Datalink study showing MMRV doubled febrile seizure risk versus separate MMR + varicella in toddlers. Large; confirmed a signal from rapid-cycle monitoring.
- [[c:hammarlund2016]]: Argued tetanus and diphtheria immunity lasts decades, so adult boosters could be spaced 20–30 years apart. Cross-sectional antibody data; supports the UK-style approach.

### Official reports and guidance

- [[c:fda-ingredients]]: FDA explanation of adjuvants, preservatives, residual formaldehyde and other ingredients. Official US view; brief.
- [[c:hhs-thimerosal-2025]]: HHS adoption (July 2025) of ACIP's vote to remove thimerosal from all US flu vaccines; describes the decision as precautionary.
- [[c:hhs-mrna-2025]]: HHS announcement winding down 22 BARDA mRNA projects (August 2025); states the department's rationale, disputed by many scientists.
- [[c:iom2002sv40]]: IOM review: evidence inadequate to accept or reject a causal link between SV40-contaminated polio vaccine and cancer.
- [[c:pinkbook]]: CDC textbook chapters on vaccine principles, storage and handling, and administration.

### Historical and critical sources

- [[c:offit2005]]: Account of the 1955 Cutter incident and its legacy for regulation and liability, by a vaccine scientist and advocate.

## References & further reading

- **Beginner.** Meredith Wadman, *The Vaccine Race: Science, Politics, and the Human Costs of Defeating Disease* (2017). WI-38, rubella vaccine and the fetal cell line story; balanced on ethics.
- **Beginner.** Paul A. Offit, *Vaccinated: One Man's Quest to Defeat the World's Deadliest Diseases* (2007). Maurice Hilleman and how many vaccines were made; the author is a vaccine developer and advocate.
- **Beginner.** Gregory Zuckerman, *A Shot to Save the World* (2021). The race to make COVID-19 vaccines, including the mRNA pioneers.
- **Intermediate.** Stanley A. Plotkin (ed.), *History of Vaccine Development* (Springer, 2011). Chapters by the developers of many vaccines.
- **Advanced.** Stanley A. Plotkin, Walter A. Orenstein, Paul A. Offit and Kathryn M. Edwards (eds), *Plotkin's Vaccines* (8th ed., 2023). Chapters on every platform and vaccine; the professional standard.
- **Advanced.** CDC, *Epidemiology and Prevention of Vaccine-Preventable Diseases* ("Pink Book", 14th ed., 2021). Free online, chapter per vaccine.
