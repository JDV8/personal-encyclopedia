# Development, trials and regulation

How a vaccine gets from the laboratory to an arm and what happens afterwards: the development pipeline, clinical trial phases, the debate over placebo controls, research ethics and challenge studies, licensing by the FDA, Health Canada, EMA and WHO, the advisory committees (ACIP, NACI, JCVI) that decide who gets which vaccine, including the 2025–26 upheaval at ACIP, and the surveillance systems that look for harms after licensing: VAERS, the Vaccine Safety Datalink, CISA, v-safe, CAEFISS, IMPACT, Yellow Card, EudraVigilance and VigiBase. It ends with the expert reviews (National Academies and Cochrane) that weigh the evidence.

**Prerequisites:** [How vaccines work](../01-how-vaccines-work/01-how-vaccines-work.md); [Randomised controlled trials](../../../epidemiology/07-randomised-controlled-trials.md); [Surveillance: passive and active](../../../epidemiology/12-surveillance-passive-and-active.md).

## Key studies & sources

Every source below was checked to exist (DOI, court record or official page). Notes summarise what each found and its main strengths or limitations.

### Peer-reviewed studies

- [Vesikari et al., *NEJM* 2006 (RotaTeq trial)](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa052664): REST trial of RotaTeq in about 70,000 infants, sized to exclude the intussusception risk seen with RotaShield. Manufacturer (Merck) funded.
- [Fraiman et al., *Vaccine* 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vaccine.2022.08.036): Independent reanalysis of serious adverse events in the Pfizer and Moderna trials, reporting an excess of serious adverse events of special interest. Uses only published summary data; method disputed.
- [Lazarus et al., *Electronic Support for Public Health–VAERS* final report to AHRQ (2010)](https://digital.ahrq.gov/sites/default/files/docs/publication/r18hs017045-lazarus-final-report-2011.pdf): Harvard Pilgrim/AHRQ project estimating that fewer than 1% of vaccine adverse events reach VAERS. A grant report, not peer-reviewed; often quoted by critics and defenders alike.
- [Goodman & Nordin, *Pediatrics* 2006](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2004-2687): Showed many VAERS reports of autism and thimerosal-related problems in the early 2000s were linked to litigation. Illustrates stimulated reporting.
- [Klein et al., *JAMA* 2021](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2021.15072): VSD rapid-cycle surveillance of 23 outcomes after mRNA COVID-19 vaccines; confirmed myocarditis in young males. Large, near-real-time; observational.
- [Xu et al., *MMWR* 2021](https://doi.org/10.15585/mmwr.mm7043e2): VSD study finding lower non-COVID mortality in vaccinated people, attributed by the authors to a healthy vaccinee effect. A teaching example of bias.
- [Rosenblum et al., *Lancet Infect Dis* 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1473-3099(22)00054-8): Six months of VAERS and v-safe data after mRNA vaccines. CDC authors; descriptive, no comparison group.
- [Murphy et al., *NEJM* 2001](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM200102223440804): Case-control confirmation of intussusception after RotaShield, leading to withdrawal. Shows surveillance working.

### Systematic reviews and methods

- [Jefferson et al., *BMJ* 2009](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.b354): Industry-funded influenza vaccine studies were published in better journals and cited more, regardless of quality. Key evidence on funding bias.
- [Bonhoeffer et al., *Vaccine* 2002 (Brighton Collaboration)](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0264-410x(02)00449-8): Founding paper of the Brighton Collaboration, which sets standard case definitions for adverse events.
- [Tozzi et al., *Vaccine* 2013 (WHO causality tool)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vaccine.2013.08.087): WHO tool for assessing whether a vaccine caused an individual adverse event.
- [Puliyel & Naik, *F1000Research* 2018](https://doi.org/10.12688/f1000research.13694.2): Critique of the WHO causality method, arguing that it discounts new reactions. A notable critical voice from India.
- [McNeil et al., *Vaccine* 2014 (VSD)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vaccine.2014.07.073): Overview of the Vaccine Safety Datalink by its CDC managers; describes its methods and limits.
- [Bettinger et al., *CCDR* 2014 (IMPACT)](https://doi.org/10.14745/ccdr.v40is3a06): Description of Canada's IMPACT hospital-based active surveillance network.
- [El Jaouhari et al., *CCDR* 2024 (CAEFISS 2018–2019)](https://doi.org/10.14745/ccdr.v50i12a06): PHAC national CAEFISS report for 2018–2019, showing how Canadian AEFI data are summarised.

### Official reports

- [Institute of Medicine, *Adverse Effects of Vaccines: Evidence and Causality* (2011)](https://doi.org/10.17226/13164): 158 vaccine–event pairs: 14 causal, 4 favour acceptance, 5 favour rejection, 135 inadequate evidence.
- [Institute of Medicine, *The Childhood Immunization Schedule and Safety* (2013)](https://doi.org/10.17226/13563): The childhood schedule: no evidence of harm, but limited direct study; recommends using the VSD.
- [NASEM, *Evidence Review of the Adverse Effects of COVID-19 Vaccination* (2024)](https://doi.org/10.17226/27746): COVID-19 vaccines: myocarditis with mRNA established; TTS and GBS with Janssen favoured; many outcomes inadequate.
- [Prasad & Makary, *NEJM* 2025, "An Evidence-Based Approach to Covid-19 Vaccination"](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsb2506929): FDA's 2025 COVID-19 vaccine framework, requiring placebo-controlled trials for healthy people under 65.
- [CDC newsroom, ACIP June 2025 meeting votes](https://www.cdc.gov/media/releases/2025/2025-cdcs-advisory-committee-on-immunization-concludes-meeting-with-joint-statement.html): CDC page on the reconstituted ACIP after all 17 members were removed in June 2025.
- [CDC, "ACIP Committee Members"](https://www.cdc.gov/acip/membership/index.html): Current ACIP membership list.
- [Shimabukuro et al., *Vaccine* 2015 (VAERS)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vaccine.2015.07.035): CDC/FDA description of how VAERS works and how it is analysed.

### Court rulings

- [*American Academy of Pediatrics v. Kennedy*, First Circuit docket 26-1503 (CourtListener)](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/73286021/american-academy-of-pediatrics-v-kennedy/): *AAP v. Kennedy*: federal court preliminarily blocked the 2026 schedule overhaul and the reconstituted ACIP (March 2026), under appeal.

### Notable critical sources

- [Jørgensen, Gøtzsche & Jefferson, *BMJ Evidence-Based Medicine* 2018](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjebm-2018-111012): Critique of the Cochrane HPV review for adjuvant comparators and missing trials, by Cochrane insiders who were then in conflict with the organisation.
- [NBC News, "The little-known database at the heart of Kennedy's vaccine conspiracy theory" (2025)](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/kennedy-vaccine-safety-datalink-conspiracy-theory-rcna203411): Reporting on David Geier's 2025 re-examination of VSD data for HHS and the history of disputes over VSD access.

## References & further reading

- **Beginner.** Paul A. Offit, *The Cutter Incident: How America's First Polio Vaccine Led to the Growing Vaccine Crisis* (2005). How a manufacturing failure shaped regulation and liability; the author is a vaccine developer.
- **Beginner.** Arthur Allen, *Vaccine: The Controversial Story of Medicine's Greatest Lifesaver* (2007). Journalistic history including regulation and safety disputes.
- **Intermediate.** Ben Goldacre, *Bad Pharma: How Drug Companies Mislead Doctors and Harm Patients* (2012). Not vaccine-specific; trial design, missing data, regulators and conflicts of interest.
- **Intermediate.** Elena Conis, *Vaccine Nation: America's Changing Relationship with Immunization* (2015). How US vaccine policy and ACIP-era schedules developed.
- **Intermediate.** Anonymous authors, *Turtles All the Way Down: Vaccine Science and Myth* (Hebrew 2019; English edition edited by Zoey O'Toole and Mary Holland, 2022). A critical book arguing vaccine trials lack proper placebo controls; useful for understanding the case critics make, though its conclusions are rejected by mainstream reviews.
- **Advanced.** Daniel Carpenter, *Reputation and Power: Organizational Image and Pharmaceutical Regulation at the FDA* (2010). Scholarly history of FDA power and its relationship with industry.
- **Advanced.** Stanley A. Plotkin et al. (eds), *Plotkin's Vaccines* (8th ed., 2023). Chapters on regulation, safety surveillance and the US, European and WHO systems.
