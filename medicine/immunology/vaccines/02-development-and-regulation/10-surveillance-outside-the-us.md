## Canada: CAEFISS

The **Canadian Adverse Events Following Immunization Surveillance System (CAEFISS)** is run by the Public Health Agency of Canada with the provinces and territories. Unlike VAERS, most reports come through public health units: in most provinces, health professionals are legally required to report adverse events following immunisation to local public health, which review them and pass them to PHAC. Manufacturers report to Health Canada. Reports are summarised in national reports such as the one covering 2018–2019 ([El Jaouhari et al., *CCDR* 2024 (CAEFISS 2018–2019)](https://doi.org/10.14745/ccdr.v50i12a06)). Combined with IMPACT's hospital-based active surveillance ([Bettinger et al., *CCDR* 2014 (IMPACT)](https://doi.org/10.14745/ccdr.v40is3a06)), this makes Canada's system a mix of passive and active elements. Serious injuries may lead to claims under the federal no-fault scheme (now the Vaccine Impact Assistance Program) or Quebec's program; see [[canadas-compensation-programs|Canada's compensation programs]].

## United Kingdom: the Yellow Card scheme

The **Yellow Card scheme**, run by the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA), was set up in 1964 after the thalidomide disaster, originally for doctors to report suspected drug reactions on yellow cards. Patients have been able to report since 2005, and reporting is now mostly online. It covers all medicines, including vaccines. The MHRA analyses reports for signals, and the UK's linked GP records (such as the Clinical Practice Research Datalink) allow controlled studies. UK studies using linked hospital records, notably self-controlled case series, have been important in vaccine safety, for example on myocarditis after COVID-19 vaccines ([Patone et al., *Circulation* 2022](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.122.059970)) and in the original development of the method for vaccine questions ([Farrington, *Biometrics* 1995](https://doi.org/10.2307/2533328)).

## European Union: EudraVigilance and PRAC

**EudraVigilance**, run by the European Medicines Agency, collects reports of suspected adverse reactions from national authorities and manufacturers across the European Economic Area. Its Pharmacovigilance Risk Assessment Committee (PRAC) assesses signals and recommends regulatory action. During COVID-19, PRAC reviews led to warnings about VITT with the AstraZeneca and Janssen vaccines and myocarditis with mRNA vaccines. Public reports are available through a public portal. Several European countries, particularly the Nordic countries, have national registries linking every resident's vaccinations and health records, allowing very large population studies; see [[the-danish-and-nordic-schedules|the Danish and Nordic schedules]].

## WHO: VigiBase and global safety

The WHO Programme for International Drug Monitoring, started in 1968, pools reports from member countries in **VigiBase**, maintained by the Uppsala Monitoring Centre in Sweden. WHO's Global Advisory Committee on Vaccine Safety (GACVS), founded in 1999, reviews safety questions and issues statements. WHO also publishes a standard method for assessing whether a vaccine caused an individual case ([Tozzi et al., *Vaccine* 2013 (WHO causality tool)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vaccine.2013.08.087)); see [[causality-assessment|causality assessment]].

Many low- and middle-income countries have weaker systems, so safety evidence for vaccines used mainly there (such as malaria or newer typhoid conjugate vaccines) sometimes depends on targeted studies.

## Comparing the systems

| System | Country | Type | Who reports | Public data |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VAERS | US | Passive | Anyone | Yes |
| CAEFISS | Canada | Mostly passive, through public health | Health professionals (mandatory in most provinces), public | Summary reports |
| Yellow Card | UK | Passive | Professionals and public | Summaries (interactive drug analysis profiles) |
| EudraVigilance | EU/EEA | Passive | National authorities, companies | Public portal |
| VigiBase | Global | Passive, pooled | National centres | Limited public access (VigiAccess) |

All passive systems share VAERS's limits: reports are not proof of cause, there is no denominator and reporting is affected by publicity.

## Key ideas

- Canada's CAEFISS routes reports through public health units; IMPACT adds active hospital surveillance.
- The UK's Yellow Card scheme dates from 1964, after thalidomide; linked UK records support controlled studies.
- The EU pools reports in EudraVigilance; WHO pools global reports in VigiBase.
- Nordic population registries allow some of the largest vaccine-safety studies.
