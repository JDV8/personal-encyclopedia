## Cost-effectiveness and the JCVI

In the UK, the **Joint Committee on Vaccination and Immunisation (JCVI)** advises the health departments of England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. In England the government is legally required to implement JCVI recommendations that are cost-effective, which makes the UK schedule unusually shaped by economic analysis. Vaccination is voluntary: there are no school requirements. Vaccines are delivered through GP practices (infants) and school nursing teams (adolescents).

## The schedule in 2026

From September 2026 ([UKHSA, "Complete routine immunisation schedule from 1 September 2026"](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-complete-routine-immunisation-schedule/complete-routine-immunisation-schedule-from-1-july-2026)):

| Age | Vaccines |
|---|---|
| 8 weeks | 6-in-1 (DTaP/IPV/Hib/HepB), MenB, rotavirus |
| 12 weeks | 6-in-1, MenB, rotavirus |
| 16 weeks | 6-in-1, pneumococcal (PCV13) |
| 1 year | PCV, MenB, MMRV |
| 18 months | 6-in-1, MMRV |
| 3 years 4 months | dTaP/IPV |
| 12–13 years | HPV (one dose) |
| 14 years | Td/IPV, MenACWY |
| Children 2 years to school age and schoolchildren | Nasal flu vaccine yearly |
| 65 and over | Pneumococcal, yearly flu, shingles (at 65 and 70–79) |
| 75 and over | RSV |

Pregnant women are offered pertussis, flu, RSV and (in some seasons) COVID-19 vaccines.

## Distinctive choices

- **MenB in infants**: in 2015 the UK became the first country to add MenB vaccine to its national infant programme.
- **Hepatitis B** was added to infant vaccines only in 2017, in the 6-in-1; there is no universal birth dose, though babies of infected mothers get one.
- **Chickenpox**: the UK long declined routine varicella vaccination over concern that it could increase shingles in adults; it introduced MMRV for all children in January 2026 ([UKHSA, "MMRV vaccination: information for healthcare professionals"](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/mmrv-programme-information-for-healthcare-practitioners/mmrv-vaccination-information-for-healthcare-professionals)). See [chickenpox and shingles](../03-diseases/shingles-and-varicella.md).
- **No routine hepatitis A** or adolescent MenB, and adults do not get routine ten-yearly tetanus boosters after five childhood doses.
- **A new 18-month appointment** was created in 2025–26, partly to improve MMR coverage.
- **Nasal flu for children**, partly to reduce transmission to older people.
- **One-dose HPV**: following JCVI advice based on evidence that a single dose gives durable protection, England moved to one dose of HPV vaccine from September 2023. Boys have been included since 2019.

## Delivery without mandates

The UK relies on a call-and-recall system: GP practices are paid partly according to their vaccination coverage, child health records track each child, and parents receive reminders. Unlike the US, there is no school-entry requirement and no religious or philosophical exemption process, because nothing is compulsory. Debates about compulsion recur after outbreaks; successive governments have rejected mandates for children, though a requirement for COVID-19 vaccination of care home staff in England was introduced in 2021 and revoked in 2022.

## Coverage and the MMR legacy

After the 1998 Wakefield paper, MMR coverage in England fell to around 80%, and measles returned. Coverage recovered but has slipped again; England's MMR coverage is well below the 95% target, and measles outbreaks and a child death in Liverpool in 2025 renewed concern. See [the MMR era](../04-history/10-the-mmr-era.md) and [[mmr-and-autism|MMR and autism]].

## Key ideas

- The JCVI's advice, tied to cost-effectiveness, shapes a voluntary schedule with no school mandates.
- The UK pioneered infant MenB vaccination (2015) but added hepatitis B (2017) and varicella (2026) later than the US.
- MMR coverage has never fully recovered from the Wakefield episode.
