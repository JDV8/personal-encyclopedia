# History

How vaccination developed, from variolation and Jenner's cowpox through compulsory vaccination and the first anti-vaccination movements, Pasteur and the laboratory vaccines, toxoids and BCG, the polio vaccines and the Cutter incident, the eradication of smallpox and the unfinished polio campaign, the 1976 swine flu affair, the MMR era, conjugate vaccines, HPV, and COVID-19 and Operation Warp Speed. The history includes failures and abuses as well as successes, because both shape today's debates.

**Prerequisites:** [Smallpox](../03-diseases/smallpox.md) and [Polio](../03-diseases/polio.md) overviews help; nothing else is required.

## Suggested reading order

Read in order; the lessons are chronological. 03 (compulsion and early opposition) and 09 (swine flu) are the most useful background for the controversies section.

## Key studies & sources

Every source below was checked to exist (DOI, court record or official page). Notes summarise what each found and its main strengths or limitations.

### Peer-reviewed studies and primary reports

- [Francis et al., *JAMA* 1955 (1954 polio field trial)](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.1955.02960140028004): Evaluation of the 1954 Salk field trial: 80–90% efficacy against paralytic polio. NFIP-funded (March of Dimes); independent evaluation centre at Michigan.
- [Nathanson & Langmuir, *American Journal of Hygiene* 1963](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordjournals.aje.a120328): CDC's account of the Cutter incident and its spread to contacts.
- [Schonberger et al., *American Journal of Epidemiology* 1979](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordjournals.aje.a112795): Excess Guillain-Barré syndrome after the 1976 swine flu vaccine, about 1 per 100,000. CDC authors.
- [Schneerson et al., *Journal of Experimental Medicine* 1980](https://doi.org/10.1084/jem.152.2.361): NIH paper describing Hib polysaccharide–protein conjugates, the basis of all conjugate vaccines.
- [Kirnbauer et al., *PNAS* 1992](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.89.24.12180): NCI paper showing HPV L1 virus-like particles are highly immunogenic.
- [Karikó et al., *Immunity* 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.immuni.2005.06.008): Nucleoside-modified mRNA avoids innate immune activation; foundation of mRNA vaccines.
- [Polack et al., *NEJM* 2020 (BNT162b2 trial)](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2034577): Phase 3 Pfizer–BioNTech trial; about 95% efficacy. Manufacturer funded.
- [Wakefield et al., *Lancet* 1998 (retracted)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(97)11096-0): The retracted *Lancet* paper that started the MMR–autism scare (see controversies).

### Historical reviews

- [Riedel, *Baylor University Medical Center Proceedings* 2005](https://doi.org/10.1080/08998280.2005.11928028): Short history of Jenner and smallpox vaccination.
- [Fraser, *Medical History* 1980](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0025727300040345): The Leicester method of isolation and quarantine as an alternative to compulsory vaccination.
- [Offit, *NEJM* 2005, "The Cutter incident, 50 years later"](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMp048180): The Cutter incident 50 years on, arguing it set the pattern for liability without fault. The author co-invented RotaTeq.
- [Fenner et al., *Smallpox and Its Eradication* (WHO, 1988)](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/39485): WHO's official history of smallpox eradication (free online).
- [Jegede, *PLoS Medicine* 2007](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.0040073): Why northern Nigerian leaders boycotted polio vaccination in 2003–04.
- [Martinez-Bravo & Stegmann, *Journal of the European Economic Association* 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/jeea/jvab018): Vaccination fell in Pakistan after the CIA's fake vaccination campaign was revealed.

### Official reports

- [Slaoui & Hepburn, *NEJM* 2020](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmp2027405): Operation Warp Speed's leaders describe its strategy.
- [Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine 2023, press release](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2023/press-release/): Nobel Assembly's explanation of Karikó and Weissman's work.
- [WHO smallpox health topic](https://www.who.int/health-topics/smallpox): WHO summary of smallpox and its eradication.

### Court rulings

- [*Jacobson v. Massachusetts*, 197 U.S. 11 (1905)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/197/11): US Supreme Court upholds compulsory smallpox vaccination during an epidemic, with limits.
- [*Zucht v. King*, 260 U.S. 174 (1922)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/260/174): US Supreme Court upholds school vaccination requirements.
- [*United States ex rel. Jackson v. Ventavia Research Group*, No. 24-40564 (5th Cir. 13 November 2026)](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/24/24-40564-CV0.pdf): Fifth Circuit affirms dismissal of the Ventavia whistleblower's False Claims Act suit (2026).

### Notable critical sources

- [Thacker, *BMJ* 2021](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.n2635): *BMJ* investigation of alleged data-integrity problems at Pfizer trial sites run by Ventavia.

## References & further reading

- **Beginner.** Arthur Allen, *Vaccine: The Controversial Story of Medicine's Greatest Lifesaver* (2007). Readable history from Jenner to the autism wars.
- **Beginner.** David M. Oshinsky, *Polio: An American Story* (2005). Salk, Sabin, the field trial and Cutter.
- **Beginner.** Gareth Williams, *Angel of Death: The Story of Smallpox* (2010). Variolation, Jenner and eradication.
- **Intermediate.** Nadja Durbach, *Bodily Matters: The Anti-Vaccination Movement in England, 1853–1907* (2005). Why working-class Britons resisted compulsion.
- **Intermediate.** Michael Willrich, *Pox: An American History* (2011). Smallpox, compulsion and civil liberties around 1900, including *Jacobson*.
- **Intermediate.** Richard E. Neustadt and Harvey V. Fineberg, *The Epidemic That Never Was: Policy-Making and the Swine Flu Scare* (1983; first published 1978 as *The Swine Flu Affair*).
- **Intermediate.** Suzanne Humphries and Roman Bystrianyk, *Dissolving Illusions: Disease, Vaccines, and the Forgotten History* (2013). A critical history arguing that sanitation and nutrition, not vaccines, drove the fall in deaths; useful for understanding that argument, which mainstream historians reject for measles, Hib and others (see the historical mortality decline lesson).
- **Advanced.** Gerald L. Geison, *The Private Science of Louis Pasteur* (1995). Pasteur's notebooks versus his public accounts.
- **Advanced.** Stanley A. Plotkin (ed.), *History of Vaccine Development* (2011). Chapters by the scientists involved.
