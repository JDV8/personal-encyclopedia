# Adverse events

What vaccines can do wrong, how often, and how we know. The section moves from common, short-lived reactions through febrile seizures, anaphylaxis, fainting and shoulder injuries, to the rare serious harms that have changed vaccine policy: intussusception after rotavirus vaccines, Guillain-Barré syndrome, narcolepsy after Pandemrix, myocarditis after mRNA vaccines, VITT after adenovirus vector vaccines, vaccine-derived polio, ITP, and reactions to live vaccines. It ends with how causality is assessed and how risks are weighed against benefits. Claimed harms that have not been confirmed, such as autism, are covered in the controversies section.

**Prerequisites:** [Development and regulation](../02-development-and-regulation/), especially post-market surveillance and reading VAERS, and the epidemiology lessons on [causation](../../../epidemiology/06-causation-and-association.md) and [self-controlled designs](../../../epidemiology/09-self-controlled-designs.md).

## Suggested reading order

Read 01–04 first for the common and injection-related reactions, then any of 05–12, each a case study of a specific harm. Finish with 13 and 14, which tie the cases together.

## Key studies & sources

Every source below was checked to exist (DOI, court record or official page). Notes summarise what each found and its main strengths or limitations.

### Peer-reviewed studies

- [McNeil et al., *J Allergy Clin Immunol* 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaci.2015.07.048): VSD: anaphylaxis 1.3 per million doses (33 cases in 25 million). CDC-funded.
- [Shimabukuro & Nair, *JAMA* 2021](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2021.0600): Early mRNA COVID-19 anaphylaxis rates (4.7 and 2.5 per million). CDC authors; early passive data.
- [Haas et al., *JAMA Network Open* 2022 (adverse events in placebo arms)](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.43955): Meta-analysis: most systemic adverse events after first COVID-19 dose also occurred with placebo (nocebo).
- [Barlow et al., *NEJM* 2001](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa003077): DTP and MMR raise febrile-seizure risk briefly, without long-term neurological harm. CDC-funded VSD.
- [Vestergaard et al., *JAMA* 2004](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.292.3.351): Danish cohort of 537,171: MMR febrile seizures small absolute risk, no excess epilepsy.
- [Klein et al., *Pediatrics* 2010](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2010-0665): MMRV doubles febrile-seizure risk vs separate MMR + varicella. Some authors had manufacturer research funding.
- [Berkovic et al., *Lancet Neurology* 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(06)70446-X): 11 of 14 'vaccine encephalopathy' cases had SCN1A mutations (Dravet syndrome).
- [McIntosh et al., *Lancet Neurology* 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(10)70107-1): Vaccination may bring forward, but does not worsen, Dravet syndrome.
- [Murphy et al., *NEJM* 2001](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM200102223440804): RotaShield and intussusception case-control study. CDC.
- [Yih et al., *NEJM* 2014 (intussusception after rotavirus vaccine)](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1303164): FDA Mini-Sentinel: 1.1–1.5 excess intussusceptions per 100,000 first doses of RotaTeq.
- [Schonberger et al., *American Journal of Epidemiology* 1979](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordjournals.aje.a112795): 1976 swine flu vaccine: about 1 excess GBS case per 100,000.
- [Salmon et al., *Lancet* 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(12)62189-8): 2009 H1N1 vaccine: 1.6 excess GBS per million across six US systems.
- [Kwong et al., *Lancet Infectious Diseases* 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1473-3099(13)70104-X): Ontario: GBS risk far higher after influenza illness than after flu vaccine.
- [Goud et al., *JAMA Internal Medicine* 2021](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2021.6227): Shingrix: about 3 excess GBS per million doses in Medicare. FDA authors.
- [Nohynek et al., *PLoS One* 2012](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0033536): Finland: Pandemrix narcolepsy risk 1 in 16,000 children. Some authors had GSK funding, yet reported the harm.
- [Ahmed et al., *Science Translational Medicine* 2015](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aab2354): Proposed mechanism for Pandemrix narcolepsy (nucleoprotein mimicry). Found less nucleoprotein in Novartis's Focetria, which was not linked to narcolepsy; mechanism still debated.
- [Oster et al., *JAMA* 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2021.24110): US myocarditis after mRNA vaccines, highest in males 16–17 after dose 2 (about 106 per million). CDC/FDA.
- [Mevorach et al., *NEJM* 2021](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2109730): Israel national data: myocarditis mostly mild, highest in young males.
- [Patone et al., *Circulation* 2022](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.122.059970): England, 42 million: myocarditis after vaccines and infection compared by age and sex.
- [Greinacher et al., *NEJM* 2021](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2104840): Description of VITT and anti-PF4 antibodies.
- [Schultz et al., *NEJM* 2021](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2104882): Norway: five VITT cases among 130,000 vaccinated.
- [Pavord et al., *NEJM* 2021 (VITT clinical features, UK)](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2109908): UK VITT series: 220 cases, 22% mortality.
- [See et al., *Annals of Internal Medicine* 2022 (TTS after Ad26.COV2.S, US)](https://doi.org/10.7326/m21-4502): US TTS reporting rates: 3.83 per million J&J doses vs 0.0086 for mRNA.
- [Simpson et al., *Nature Medicine* 2021 (ChAdOx1 and ITP, Scotland)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-021-01408-4): Scotland: ITP after AstraZeneca at about 1.1 per 100,000 doses.
- [France et al., *Pediatrics* 2008](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2007-1578): VSD: MMR causes ITP, highest risk at 12–15 months.
- [Atanasoff et al., *Vaccine* 2010 (SIRVA)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vaccine.2010.10.005): First description of SIRVA, by VICP physicians (13 cases).

### Systematic and expert reviews

- [Institute of Medicine, *Adverse Effects of Vaccines: Evidence and Causality* (2011)](https://doi.org/10.17226/13164): IOM review of 158 vaccine–event pairs: 14 convincingly causal, 5 favouring rejection, most inadequate. Commissioned by HRSA, which runs the VICP.
- [Institute of Medicine, *Adverse Effects of Vaccines*, report brief (2011)](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/resource/13164/Adverseeffectsofvaccinesreportbrief.pdf): Report brief listing the causal relationships.
- [NASEM, *Evidence Review of the Adverse Effects of COVID-19 Vaccination* (2024)](https://doi.org/10.17226/27746): NASEM review of COVID-19 vaccine adverse effects (2024).
- [Bigouette et al., *MMWR* 2023 (vaccine-derived poliovirus outbreaks 2021–2022)](https://doi.org/10.15585/mmwr.mm7214a3): CDC/WHO update on vaccine-derived poliovirus outbreaks 2021–22.

### Official reports and guidance

- [GPEI, "Oral polio vaccine (OPV)"](https://polioeradication.org/about-polio/the-vaccines/opv/): GPEI on OPV advantages and VAPP (1 per 2.7 million doses).
- [GPEI press release on nOPV2 prequalification (December 2023)](https://polioeradication.org/news/gpei-press-release-on-nopv2-prequalification/): nOPV2 prequalification, December 2023.
- [FDA, "FDA requires Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS) warning in the prescribing information for RSV vaccines Abrysvo and Arexvy" (January 2025)](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/safety-availability-biologics/fda-requires-guillain-barre-syndrome-gbs-warning-prescribing-information-rsv-vaccines-abrysvo-and): FDA GBS warning for older-adult RSV vaccines.
- [CDC Yellow Book 2026, "Yellow fever" (NCBI Bookshelf)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK620866/): CDC Yellow Book rates of yellow fever vaccine reactions.
- [CDC, *General Best Practice Guidelines for Immunization*: contraindications and precautions](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/imz-best-practices/contraindications-precautions.html): CDC contraindications and precautions.
- [Tozzi et al., *Vaccine* 2013 (WHO causality tool)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vaccine.2013.08.087): WHO individual causality assessment algorithm.
- [Bonhoeffer et al., *Vaccine* 2002 (Brighton Collaboration)](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0264-410x(02)00449-8): Brighton Collaboration case definitions.

### Notable critical sources

- [Puliyel & Naik, *F1000Research* 2018](https://doi.org/10.12688/f1000research.13694.2): Critique arguing the revised WHO causality algorithm makes new harms hard to recognise.
- [Hill, *Proceedings of the Royal Society of Medicine* 1965](https://doi.org/10.1177/003591576505800503): Bradford Hill's criteria, the basis for causal reasoning on both sides.

## References & further reading

- **Beginner.** Paul A. Offit and Charlotte A. Moser, *Vaccines and Your Child: Separating Fact from Fiction* (2011). Side effects vaccine by vaccine; Offit co-invented RotaTeq.
- **Beginner.** Institute of Medicine, *Adverse Effects of Vaccines: Evidence and Causality* (2011), report brief. Free; the most thorough independent review of pre-COVID evidence.
- **Intermediate.** Heidi J. Larson, *Stuck: How Vaccine Rumors Start and Why They Don't Go Away* (2020). How real and perceived harms shape trust.
- **Intermediate.** Robert F. Kennedy Jr., *The Real Anthony Fauci: Bill Gates, Big Pharma, and the Global War on Democracy and Public Health* (2021). A widely read critical account alleging regulatory capture and hidden harms; read critically, many of its claims are disputed by the studies cited here.
- **Advanced.** Stanley A. Plotkin et al. (eds), *Plotkin's Vaccines* (8th ed., 2023). Chapters on vaccine safety and each vaccine's adverse events.
- **Advanced.** World Health Organization, *Causality Assessment of an Adverse Event Following Immunization (AEFI): User Manual for the Revised WHO Classification* (2nd ed., 2018). Free.
