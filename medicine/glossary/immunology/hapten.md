# Hapten

A small molecule that can be recognised by antibodies but cannot provoke a response unless it is attached to a larger carrier protein. The concept, developed by Karl Landsteiner, explains some drug allergies, such as reactions to penicillin, which binds body proteins. It also underlies the logic of conjugate vaccines, where weakly immunogenic sugars are linked to proteins to recruit T-cell help.
