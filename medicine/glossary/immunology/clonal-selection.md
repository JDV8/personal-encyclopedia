# Clonal selection

The theory, developed by Frank Macfarlane Burnet and others in the 1950s, that each lymphocyte carries a receptor for a single antigen and that antigen selects and expands the matching cells. It explains specificity, memory and tolerance of self, and is the framework for understanding how vaccines work: they selectively expand a few clones among millions.
