# Adaptive immunity

The slower, specific arm of the immune system, made up of B and T cells, that recognises particular antigens, takes days to weeks to develop on first exposure, and leaves immunological memory. Its specificity and memory are what vaccines exploit: a second encounter with the same antigen produces a faster, stronger response, which is the basis of booster doses.
