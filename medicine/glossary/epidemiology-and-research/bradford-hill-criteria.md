# Bradford Hill criteria

Nine considerations proposed by Austin Bradford Hill in 1965 to help judge whether an association is causal: strength, consistency, specificity, temporality, biological gradient, plausibility, coherence, experiment and analogy. Hill stressed that they are not a checklist; only temporality, the cause preceding the effect, is strictly necessary. They remain the standard framework in epidemiology and vaccine-safety reviews.
