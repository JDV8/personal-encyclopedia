# Live attenuated vaccine

A vaccine containing a living pathogen weakened so that it replicates enough to stimulate strong immunity but rarely causes disease. Examples include MMR, varicella, yellow fever, oral polio and rotavirus vaccines. They often give lifelong protection after one or two doses but can, rarely, revert or harm people with weakened immune systems, so they are usually avoided in pregnancy and immunodeficiency.
