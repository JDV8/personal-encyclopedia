# Correlate of protection

A measurable immune marker, usually an antibody level, that reliably predicts protection against disease. For example, a tetanus antitoxin level of 0.01 IU/mL is considered protective. Established correlates allow new vaccines to be licensed on immune response data rather than large efficacy trials. Many diseases, such as pertussis and tuberculosis, lack clear correlates.
