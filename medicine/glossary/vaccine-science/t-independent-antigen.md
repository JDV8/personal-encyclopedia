# T-independent antigen

An antigen, typically a repeating polysaccharide, that can activate B cells without help from T cells. Responses are mostly IgM, short-lived and without memory, and the immune system of children under about two years responds poorly to these antigens. This explains why older polysaccharide vaccines failed in infants and why conjugate vaccines were needed.
