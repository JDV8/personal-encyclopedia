# Inactivated vaccine

A vaccine containing whole pathogens killed by heat, formaldehyde or other chemicals, so they cannot replicate. Examples include the Salk polio vaccine, hepatitis A and most traditional influenza vaccines. They are safe in immunodeficiency but generally need several doses and boosters, and often an adjuvant, because a killed microbe stimulates weaker responses than a live one.
