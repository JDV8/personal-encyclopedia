# Theory of the second best

Lipsey and Lancaster's 1956 result that if one condition for efficiency cannot be met, satisfying the other conditions is not necessarily desirable. Removing one distortion while others remain may make things worse. It cautions against piecemeal policy based on first-best reasoning.

In this course the term is introduced in [The welfare theorems](../08-welfare-economics/03-the-welfare-theorems.md), part of the section [Welfare economics](../08-welfare-economics/README.md).
