# Cobb–Douglas function

A functional form, Y = A·K^α·L^β for production or U = x^a·y^b for utility, named after Charles Cobb and Paul Douglas, who fitted it to US manufacturing data in 1928. Its convenient properties include constant shares of spending on each good (or of income paid to each factor) and an elasticity of substitution of one. If α + β = 1 the production function has constant returns to scale.
