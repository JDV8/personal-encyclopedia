# Ambiguity aversion

A preference for known risks over unknown ones. In Daniel Ellsberg's 1961 experiment, people prefer to bet on an urn with a known 50-50 mix of colours rather than one with an unknown mix, even when the bets are equivalent under any consistent probability belief. Ambiguity aversion cannot be explained by standard expected utility and may help explain home bias in investing and reluctance to adopt unfamiliar technologies.
