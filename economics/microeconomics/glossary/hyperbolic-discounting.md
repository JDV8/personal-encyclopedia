# Hyperbolic discounting

A pattern of time preference in which people discount the near future much more steeply than the distant future, so they prefer smaller sooner rewards when the choice is immediate but larger later rewards when both are far away. It produces time-inconsistent plans and procrastination. The quasi-hyperbolic β–δ model is a simple version used in economics.
