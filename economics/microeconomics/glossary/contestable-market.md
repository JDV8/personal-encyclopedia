# Contestable market

A market in which entry and exit are free and costless, so that even a single incumbent firm must keep prices close to average cost or be undercut by "hit-and-run" entrants. Developed by Baumol, Panzar and Willig in 1982, the theory implies that market structure alone may not indicate market power. Few markets are perfectly contestable, because entry usually involves sunk costs.
