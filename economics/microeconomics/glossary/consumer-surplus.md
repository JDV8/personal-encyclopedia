# Consumer surplus

The difference between what consumers are willing to pay for a good and what they actually pay, summed over all units bought. On a supply-and-demand diagram, it is the area below the demand curve and above the price. Jules Dupuit introduced the idea in 1844 and Alfred Marshall popularised it. In the coffee example, consumer surplus at the equilibrium price of 3 is 625.
