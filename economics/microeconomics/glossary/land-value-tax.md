# Land value tax

A tax on the unimproved value of land, excluding buildings and other improvements. Because the supply of land is fixed, the tax cannot reduce the amount of land and so causes no deadweight loss; its burden falls on landowners. Henry George advocated it as a "single tax" in Progress and Poverty (1879), and economists of many persuasions have praised its efficiency.
