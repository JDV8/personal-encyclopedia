# Expected utility

The probability-weighted average of the utilities of possible outcomes of a risky choice. Expected utility theory, formalised by von Neumann and Morgenstern in 1944, holds that rational people choose the option with the highest expected utility. With a concave utility function, it explains risk aversion and insurance. Paradoxes such as Allais's motivated alternatives like prospect theory.
