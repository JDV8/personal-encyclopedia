# Classical economics

The school of economic thought from Adam Smith's Wealth of Nations (1776) to John Stuart Mill's Principles (1848), including Malthus, Ricardo and Say. Classical economists focused on growth, capital accumulation and the distribution of income among workers, landlords and capitalists, and mostly held cost-of-production or labour theories of value. Their theory of value was replaced by marginalism in the 1870s, but many of their insights, such as comparative advantage, endure.
