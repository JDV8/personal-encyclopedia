# Veblen good

A good for which demand rises with price because a high price makes it a status symbol, named after Thorstein Veblen, who described "conspicuous consumption" in The Theory of the Leisure Class (1899). Luxury handbags and watches are sometimes cited. Unlike Giffen goods, Veblen goods reflect preferences about price itself, not income effects.

In this course the term is introduced in [Shifts in demand](../02-supply-and-demand/02-shifts-in-demand.md), part of the section [Supply and demand](../02-supply-and-demand/README.md).
