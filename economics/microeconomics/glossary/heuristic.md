# Heuristic

A mental shortcut or rule of thumb for making judgements and decisions quickly without full analysis. Heuristics such as representativeness, availability and anchoring are usually useful but can produce systematic errors, or biases, in predictable situations. Debate continues over how often heuristics mislead and how often they are well adapted to real environments.

In this course the term is introduced in [Heuristics and biases](../09-behavioural-economics/03-heuristics-and-biases.md), part of the section [Behavioural economics](../09-behavioural-economics/README.md). Related terms from the same lesson: [Anchoring](anchoring.md), [Availability heuristic](availability-heuristic.md), [Framing effect](framing-effect.md), [Representativeness heuristic](representativeness-heuristic.md).
