# Bounded rationality

Herbert Simon's term for decision-making by people who are "intendedly rational, but only limitedly so", because of limited information, attention, computing power and time. Rather than optimise, boundedly rational people use rules of thumb and satisfice, accepting good-enough options. Bounded rationality explains the importance of defaults and simplicity, and why organisations rely on routines.
