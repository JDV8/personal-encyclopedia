# Impartial spectator

Adam Smith's idea, in The Theory of Moral Sentiments (1759), that we judge our own conduct by imagining how a fair and well-informed observer would see it. The impartial spectator is the voice of conscience, formed through sympathy with others. It shows that Smith's view of human nature included moral sentiments alongside self-interest.

In this course the term is introduced in [Adam Smith (1723–1790)](../10-history-of-thought/thinkers/01-adam-smith.md), part of the section [Thinkers](../10-history-of-thought/thinkers/README.md).
