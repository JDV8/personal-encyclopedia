# Austrian school

A tradition of economic thought founded by Carl Menger in 1871 and developed by Böhm-Bawerk, Wieser, Mises, Hayek and Kirzner. It emphasises subjective value, the market as a process of discovery rather than a static equilibrium, entrepreneurship, dispersed knowledge and the time structure of production. Austrians are generally sceptical of mathematical modelling and of government intervention, and their critique of central planning is widely respected even outside the school.
