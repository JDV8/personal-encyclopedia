# Indifference curve

A curve showing all combinations of two goods that give a consumer the same level of satisfaction. Indifference curves are usually downward sloping and convex to the origin, and higher curves represent greater utility. Their slope is the marginal rate of substitution. Introduced by Edgeworth and developed by Pareto, Hicks and Allen, they underpin ordinal consumer theory.
