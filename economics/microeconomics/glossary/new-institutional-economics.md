# New institutional economics

An approach that uses largely mainstream economic tools to study how institutions such as property rights, contracts, firms, legal systems and governance arrangements affect economic performance. Leading figures include Ronald Coase, Oliver Williamson, Douglass North and Elinor Ostrom. It emphasises transaction costs and the choice between markets, hierarchies and other forms of organisation.

In this course the term is introduced in [Twentieth-century microeconomics](../10-history-of-thought/04-twentieth-century-microeconomics.md), part of the section [History of economic thought](../10-history-of-thought/README.md).
