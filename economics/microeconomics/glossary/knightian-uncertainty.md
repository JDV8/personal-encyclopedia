# Knightian uncertainty

Frank Knight's distinction, in Risk, Uncertainty and Profit (1921), between measurable risk, where probabilities are known or can be estimated, and true uncertainty, where they cannot. Knight argued that profit is the reward for bearing uncertainty, which cannot be insured. The idea matters for entrepreneurship, financial crises and climate policy.
