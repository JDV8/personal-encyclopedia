# Kaldor–Hicks efficiency

A criterion, proposed by Nicholas Kaldor and John Hicks in 1939, under which a change is an improvement if the gainers gain enough that they could in principle compensate the losers and still be better off, whether or not compensation is actually paid. It allows ranking policies that create winners and losers, and underlies cost-benefit analysis, but is criticised because the losers really do lose.
