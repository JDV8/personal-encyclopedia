# Microeconomics glossary

Paragraph-length definitions of 345 terms used in the microeconomics course. Each entry explains the idea in a few sentences, names the lesson where it is introduced, and links to related terms from that lesson. Use it while reading; the lessons are where each idea is built up with examples.

**Prerequisites:** none; this is a lookup list. For reading order see the [microeconomics course](../README.md).

## Suggested reading order

Look terms up as they appear in the lessons. For a quick first pass, read these core entries: [scarcity](scarcity.md), [opportunity cost](opportunity-cost.md), [marginal analysis](marginal-analysis.md), [equilibrium](equilibrium.md), [price elasticity of demand](price-elasticity-of-demand.md), [deadweight loss](deadweight-loss.md), [Nash equilibrium](nash-equilibrium.md), [externality](externality.md) and [Pareto efficiency](pareto-efficiency.md).

## References & further reading

- John Black, Nigar Hashimzade and Gareth Myles, *A Dictionary of Economics* (Oxford) (Beginner). Concise definitions of several thousand economic terms.
- David W. Pearce (ed.), *The MIT Dictionary of Modern Economics* (Intermediate). Longer entries on concepts and schools of thought.
- Steven N. Durlauf and Lawrence E. Blume (eds.), *The New Palgrave Dictionary of Economics* (Advanced). The authoritative encyclopedia of economics, with essay-length entries.
- N. Gregory Mankiw, *Principles of Microeconomics* (Beginner). Its end-of-book glossary matches the vocabulary of introductory courses.
