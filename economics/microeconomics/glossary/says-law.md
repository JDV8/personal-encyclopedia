# Say's law

The principle, associated with Jean-Baptiste Say (1803), that production creates the income needed to purchase output, so that a general glut of all goods at once is impossible. Often summarised as "supply creates its own demand", a phrase from Keynes, who attacked the principle in 1936. It remains a point of debate in macroeconomics.
