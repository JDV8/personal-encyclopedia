# Asymmetric information

A situation in which one party to a transaction knows something relevant that the other does not, such as a seller who knows a car's history or a borrower who knows the riskiness of a project. Hidden information before a deal leads to adverse selection; hidden action after it leads to moral hazard. Akerlof, Spence and Stiglitz shared the 2001 Nobel prize for showing how such asymmetries can cause markets to fail.
