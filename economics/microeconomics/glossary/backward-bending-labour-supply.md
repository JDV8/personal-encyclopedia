# Backward-bending labour supply

A labour supply curve that slopes upward at low wages but bends back at high wages, so that further wage rises reduce hours worked. It arises when the income effect of a higher wage (richer people want more leisure) outweighs the substitution effect (leisure becomes more expensive). Historical declines in working hours as wages rose are consistent with it, though for most workers the curve appears fairly steep rather than backward bending.
