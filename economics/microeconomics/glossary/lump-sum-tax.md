# Lump-sum tax

A tax of a fixed amount that does not depend on anything the taxpayer can change, such as income, spending or work. Because it does not alter incentives at the margin, it causes no deadweight loss. The second welfare theorem assumes redistribution through lump-sum taxes and transfers; in practice such taxes are rare and seen as unfair, as the UK poll tax of 1990 showed.
