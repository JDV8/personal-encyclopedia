# Marginal productivity theory

The theory, developed by John Bates Clark and others around 1900, that in competitive markets each factor of production is paid the value of its marginal product. Workers earn what their last hour of work adds to output. It replaced classical class-based theories of distribution, though critics argue that market power and institutions also shape pay.
