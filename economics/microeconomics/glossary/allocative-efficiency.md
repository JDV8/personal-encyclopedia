# Allocative efficiency

Producing the combination of goods that consumers value most, given costs. In a single market it means producing up to the point where price (marginal benefit) equals marginal cost; for the whole economy, the rate at which consumers would trade goods equals the rate at which the economy can transform one into another. Perfect competition achieves it in theory; monopoly does not, because price exceeds marginal cost.
