# Job polarisation

The growth of employment in both high-skill, high-wage jobs and low-skill, low-wage service jobs, with a decline in middle-skill routine jobs such as clerical and manufacturing work. David Autor and others link it to automation of routine tasks and to trade. It has been documented in the United States, Canada and many European countries.

In this course the term is introduced in [Labour demand](../06-factor-markets/02-labour-demand.md), part of the section [Factor markets and the distribution of income](../06-factor-markets/README.md). Related terms from the same lesson: [Labour demand](labour-demand.md).
