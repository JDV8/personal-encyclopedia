# Framing effect

A change in choices caused by how options are described rather than by their substance. In Tversky and Kahneman's Asian disease problem, people preferred a sure option when outcomes were framed as lives saved but a gamble when the same outcomes were framed as deaths. Framing effects violate the standard assumption that preferences depend only on outcomes.
