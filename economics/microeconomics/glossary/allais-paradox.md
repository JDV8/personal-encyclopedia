# Allais paradox

A pair of choices, devised by Maurice Allais in 1953, in which most people make decisions that cannot both be consistent with expected utility theory. People prefer a certain million to a gamble with a small chance of nothing, yet in a second choice where the certainty is removed they prefer the riskier option. The pattern reveals a certainty effect, the overweighting of sure outcomes, and later motivated prospect theory's probability weighting.
