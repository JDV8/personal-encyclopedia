# Nash equilibrium

A set of strategies, one per player, such that no player can improve their payoff by changing strategy alone, given the others' strategies. Defined by John Nash in 1950, who proved that every finite game has at least one, possibly in mixed strategies. It is the central solution concept in game theory, though equilibria need not be efficient, as the prisoners' dilemma shows.
