# Marginal revolution

The transformation of economics in the early 1870s, when Jevons, Menger and Walras independently argued that value depends on marginal utility rather than labour or cost of production. It resolved the diamond–water paradox and marks the shift from classical to neoclassical economics, with greater use of mathematics and a focus on allocation of scarce resources.
