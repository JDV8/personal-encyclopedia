# Consumer theory

Where does the demand curve come from? This section models a consumer with preferences, represented by utility and indifference curves, facing a budget constraint. The optimal choice equates the marginal rate of substitution with the price ratio. Changing income and prices traces out Engel curves and demand curves, and the Slutsky decomposition separates income and substitution effects. The last lessons extend the model to choices under uncertainty and over time.

**Prerequisites:** [Supply and demand](../02-supply-and-demand/README.md). Comfort with reading graphs helps.

## References & further reading

- N. Gregory Mankiw, *Principles of Microeconomics* (Beginner). The most widely used introductory textbook; clear on supply and demand, elasticity, taxes and market structures.
- Hal R. Varian, *Intermediate Microeconomics: A Modern Approach* (Intermediate). The standard intermediate text, with careful treatment of consumer and producer theory.
- Robert S. Pindyck and Daniel L. Rubinfeld, *Microeconomics* (Intermediate). An intermediate textbook strong on applications, market power and information.
- Walter Nicholson and Christopher Snyder, *Microeconomic Theory: Basic Principles and Extensions* (Intermediate). A calculus-based treatment of utility maximisation and demand.
- Angus Deaton and John Muellbauer, *Economics and Consumer Behavior* (Advanced). The classic text on demand analysis.
- Andreu Mas-Colell, Michael D. Whinston and Jerry R. Green, *Microeconomic Theory* (Advanced). The graduate-level reference for consumer, producer, general equilibrium and information theory.
