# Market failure

Markets are efficient only under certain conditions. This section studies what happens when they fail: externalities and the remedies of Pigouvian taxes, Coasean bargaining, cap and trade and regulation; public goods and free riding; common resources and the tragedy of the commons; and asymmetric information, adverse selection, moral hazard, principal–agent problems, signalling and screening. It ends with government failure, the reminder that interventions can fail too.

**Prerequisites:** [Supply and demand](../02-supply-and-demand/README.md) (surplus and deadweight loss) and [Market structures](../05-market-structures/README.md).

## Suggested reading order

Read 01 first. Lessons 02 to 07 form a unit on externalities; 08 and 09 on public goods and commons; 10 to 14 on information; 15 closes the section.

## References & further reading

- Tim Harford, *The Undercover Economist* (Beginner). Scarcity, pricing, externalities and information explained through everyday examples.
- Elinor Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (Intermediate). Field evidence on how communities manage shared resources.
- Ronald H. Coase, *The Firm, the Market, and the Law* (Intermediate). Includes "The Problem of Social Cost".
- Arthur Cecil Pigou, *The Economics of Welfare* (Advanced). The 1920 founding text of externality analysis.
- Joseph E. Stiglitz and Jay K. Rosengard, *Economics of the Public Sector* (Intermediate). Market failure and public policy.
- Tom Tietenberg and Lynne Lewis, *Environmental and Natural Resource Economics* (Intermediate). Pollution policy, permits and resource management.
- James M. Buchanan and Gordon Tullock, *The Calculus of Consent* (Advanced). The founding text of public choice theory.
