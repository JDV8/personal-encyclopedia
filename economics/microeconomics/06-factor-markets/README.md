# Factor markets and the distribution of income

Factor markets determine wages, interest and rents, and so how income is distributed. This section derives labour demand from marginal revenue product and labour supply from the work–leisure choice, explains wage differences through compensating differentials, human capital and discrimination, and analyses monopsony and unions. It then covers capital and interest, land and economic rent, and the measurement and causes of income inequality.

**Prerequisites:** [Producer theory](../04-producer-theory/README.md) and [Market structures](../05-market-structures/README.md).

## Suggested reading order

Read 01 to 06 on labour, then 07 and 08 on capital and land, then 09.

## References & further reading

- N. Gregory Mankiw, *Principles of Microeconomics* (Beginner). The most widely used introductory textbook; clear on supply and demand, elasticity, taxes and market structures.
- George J. Borjas, *Labor Economics* (Intermediate). The standard labour economics textbook.
- Claudia Goldin and Lawrence F. Katz, *The Race between Education and Technology* (Intermediate). Education, technology and inequality in the US.
- Henry George, *Progress and Poverty* (Beginner). The 1879 case for a land value tax.
- Thomas Piketty, *Capital in the Twenty-First Century* (Intermediate). A long-run history of income and wealth inequality.
- Anthony B. Atkinson, *Inequality: What Can Be Done?* (Beginner). Measurement and policy proposals from a leading scholar.
- Alan Manning, *Monopsony in Motion* (Advanced). Employer power in labour markets.
