# Market structures and game theory

How does the number of competitors change prices and output? This section moves from perfect competition, through monopoly, price discrimination and natural monopoly, to monopolistic competition and oligopoly. Because oligopolists must anticipate one another, the section introduces game theory: dominant strategies, Nash equilibrium, the prisoners' dilemma, Cournot, Bertrand and Stackelberg models, and repeated games. It ends with competition policy.

**Prerequisites:** [Producer theory](../04-producer-theory/README.md).

## Suggested reading order

Read 01 to 08 for market structures, then 09 to 14 for game theory, then 15.

## References & further reading

- N. Gregory Mankiw, *Principles of Microeconomics* (Beginner). The most widely used introductory textbook; clear on supply and demand, elasticity, taxes and market structures.
- Avinash K. Dixit and Barry J. Nalebuff, *The Art of Strategy* (Beginner). Game theory explained with business and everyday examples.
- Thomas C. Schelling, *The Strategy of Conflict* (Intermediate). The classic on commitment, threats and focal points.
- Luís M. B. Cabral, *Introduction to Industrial Organization* (Intermediate). Market power, pricing, entry and competition policy.
- Robert S. Pindyck and Daniel L. Rubinfeld, *Microeconomics* (Intermediate). An intermediate textbook strong on applications, market power and information.
- Jean Tirole, *The Theory of Industrial Organization* (Advanced). The graduate synthesis of game-theoretic industrial organisation.
- Robert Gibbons, *Game Theory for Applied Economists* (Advanced). A concise rigorous introduction to game theory.
