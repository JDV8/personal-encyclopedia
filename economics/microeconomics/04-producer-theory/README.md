# Producer theory

This section explains the supply side. It asks what a firm is, describes production with production functions and isoquants, explains diminishing marginal returns, and derives the family of short-run and long-run cost curves. Economies of scale shape industry structure, and the profit-maximising rule (marginal revenue equals marginal cost) links costs to output decisions. A running brewery example (TC = 100 + 10Q − Q² + 0.1Q³) is used throughout.

**Prerequisites:** [Supply and demand](../02-supply-and-demand/README.md); [Consumer theory](../03-consumer-theory/README.md) helps for isoquants.

## References & further reading

- N. Gregory Mankiw, *Principles of Microeconomics* (Beginner). The most widely used introductory textbook; clear on supply and demand, elasticity, taxes and market structures.
- Hal R. Varian, *Intermediate Microeconomics: A Modern Approach* (Intermediate). The standard intermediate text, with careful treatment of consumer and producer theory.
- Robert S. Pindyck and Daniel L. Rubinfeld, *Microeconomics* (Intermediate). An intermediate textbook strong on applications, market power and information.
- Ronald H. Coase, *The Firm, the Market, and the Law* (Intermediate). Coase's essays, including "The Nature of the Firm".
- Paul Milgrom and John Roberts, *Economics, Organization and Management* (Intermediate). How firms are organised and why.
- Andreu Mas-Colell, Michael D. Whinston and Jerry R. Green, *Microeconomic Theory* (Advanced). The graduate-level reference for consumer, producer, general equilibrium and information theory.
