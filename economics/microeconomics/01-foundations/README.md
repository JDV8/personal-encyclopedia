# Foundations: scarcity, choice and trade

The first section introduces the way economists think. Because resources are scarce, every choice has an opportunity cost; people respond to incentives and decide at the margin; and specialisation according to comparative advantage creates gains from trade. The production possibilities frontier pictures these ideas, and the last two lessons explain how markets, prices and property rights coordinate decisions, and how economists use models.

**Prerequisites:** none.

## Suggested reading order

Read in numerical order. Lessons 01 to 05 are about individual choice; 06 to 09 about specialisation and trade; 10 and 11 about markets and method.

## References & further reading

- N. Gregory Mankiw, *Principles of Microeconomics* (Beginner). The most widely used introductory textbook; clear on supply and demand, elasticity, taxes and market structures.
- Thomas Sowell, *Basic Economics* (Beginner). A non-mathematical explanation of how prices, incentives and markets work, with strong views on price controls.
- Tim Harford, *The Undercover Economist* (Beginner). Scarcity, pricing, externalities and information explained through everyday examples.
- Henry Hazlitt, *Economics in One Lesson* (Beginner). A short classic on looking beyond the immediate effects of policy.
- Russell Roberts, *The Price of Everything* (Beginner). A novel that dramatises how prices coordinate behaviour.
- Paul Krugman and Robin Wells, *Microeconomics* (Beginner). An introductory textbook with many real-world cases.
- Leonard E. Read, *I, Pencil* (Beginner). A famous short essay on the division of labour and spontaneous coordination.
- Hal R. Varian, *Intermediate Microeconomics: A Modern Approach* (Intermediate). The standard intermediate text, with careful treatment of consumer and producer theory.
