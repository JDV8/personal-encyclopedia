# Supply and demand

This section builds the core model of economics. It introduces demand and supply and what shifts them, shows how they determine equilibrium price and quantity, and measures responsiveness with elasticities. Consumer and producer surplus then let us measure welfare, which is used to analyse price ceilings, price floors, taxes, deadweight loss, subsidies, quotas and tariffs. A running coffee-market example (demand Qd = 1100 − 200P, supply Qs = −100 + 200P) ties the lessons together.

**Prerequisites:** [Foundations](../01-foundations/README.md).

## Suggested reading order

Read in order: 01 to 06 build the model, 07 and 08 add elasticity, 09 and 10 introduce surplus, and 11 to 15 apply everything to policy.

## References & further reading

- N. Gregory Mankiw, *Principles of Microeconomics* (Beginner). The most widely used introductory textbook; clear on supply and demand, elasticity, taxes and market structures.
- Paul Krugman and Robin Wells, *Microeconomics* (Beginner). An introductory textbook with many real-world cases.
- Thomas Sowell, *Basic Economics* (Beginner). A non-mathematical explanation of how prices, incentives and markets work, with strong views on price controls.
- Alfred Marshall, *Principles of Economics* (Intermediate). The 1890 classic in which the modern supply-and-demand framework took shape.
- Hal R. Varian, *Intermediate Microeconomics: A Modern Approach* (Intermediate). The standard intermediate text, with careful treatment of consumer and producer theory.
- Robert S. Pindyck and Daniel L. Rubinfeld, *Microeconomics* (Intermediate). An intermediate textbook strong on applications, market power and information.
- Harvey S. Rosen and Ted Gayer, *Public Finance* (Intermediate). Tax incidence and deadweight loss in depth.
