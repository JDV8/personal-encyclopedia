# History of economic thought

This section traces how microeconomic ideas developed: from Greek and medieval thinking about exchange and money, through mercantilism and the Physiocrats, to classical economics, the marginal revolution of the 1870s, and the twentieth-century transformation of the field. A survey of schools of thought compares neoclassical, Marxian, Austrian, institutional and other traditions. The thinkers folder profiles sixteen economists, from Adam Smith to Daniel Kahneman.

**Prerequisites:** none strictly, but the lessons refer often to ideas taught earlier in the course.

## Suggested reading order

Read lessons 01 to 05 in order, then the thinker profiles, which follow chronological order.

## References & further reading

- Robert L. Heilbroner, *The Worldly Philosophers* (Beginner). A classic narrative of the great economists' lives and ideas.
- Todd G. Buchholz, *New Ideas from Dead Economists* (Beginner). An accessible survey from Smith to modern times.
- Sylvia Nasar, *Grand Pursuit: The Story of Economic Genius* (Beginner). A narrative history from Marshall to Keynes and beyond.
- Adam Smith, *The Wealth of Nations* (Intermediate). The 1776 founding text.
- Roger E. Backhouse, *The Ordinary Business of Life* (Intermediate). A history of economics from the ancient world to the present.
- Mark Blaug, *Economic Theory in Retrospect* (Advanced). A detailed analytical history of economic theory.
- Joseph A. Schumpeter, *History of Economic Analysis* (Advanced). The monumental, opinionated history.
