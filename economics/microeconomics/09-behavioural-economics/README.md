# Behavioural economics

Standard economics assumes rational, consistent, self-interested decision-makers. This section examines the evidence that real people depart from those assumptions in systematic ways: bounded rationality, heuristics and biases, prospect theory and loss aversion, present bias, and social preferences. It then looks at nudges and choice architecture and closes with the main critiques and limits of behavioural economics.

**Prerequisites:** [Consumer theory](../03-consumer-theory/README.md), especially choice under uncertainty and over time.

## References & further reading

- Daniel Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (Beginner). The research programme behind heuristics, biases and prospect theory.
- Richard H. Thaler, *Misbehaving: The Making of Behavioral Economics* (Beginner). A memoir-history of the field.
- Richard H. Thaler and Cass R. Sunstein, *Nudge* (Beginner). Choice architecture and libertarian paternalism.
- Michael Lewis, *The Undoing Project* (Beginner). The story of Kahneman and Tversky's collaboration.
- Gerd Gigerenzer, *Gut Feelings* (Beginner). The case for simple heuristics and ecological rationality.
- Nick Wilkinson and Matthias Klaes, *An Introduction to Behavioral Economics* (Intermediate). A textbook survey of the field.
- Colin F. Camerer, George Loewenstein and Matthew Rabin (eds.), *Advances in Behavioral Economics* (Advanced). Key research papers.
