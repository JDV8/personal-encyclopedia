# Behavioural finance: how investors really behave

Classical finance assumes rational investors and efficient prices. This section studies the evidence that people depart from that ideal in systematic ways, and what it means for markets and for your own decisions. It begins with the shift from rational to behavioural models, then covers prospect theory and loss aversion, the biases in how we form beliefs, herding and bubbles, and finally mental accounting, anchoring and framing. Each lesson ends with practical defences, because the most useful application is learning your own predictable mistakes.

**Prerequisites:** [Portfolio theory](../06-portfolio-theory/README.md), particularly the efficient market hypothesis, and [Foundations](../01-foundations/README.md) for probability and expected value.

## Suggested reading order

Read in order. Lesson 02 is the conceptual core. Lessons 03 and 05 are catalogues of biases you can dip into, and lesson 04 links directly to the history section, so you may wish to read it alongside the lessons on tulip mania and the dot-com bubble.

## References & further reading

- Daniel Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (Beginner to Intermediate). The Nobel laureate's account of heuristics, biases and prospect theory.
- Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky, "Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk", *Econometrica* (1979) (Advanced). The original paper.
- Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman, "Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases", *Science* (1974) (Intermediate). The classic short paper on anchoring, availability and representativeness.
- Richard H. Thaler, *Misbehaving: The Making of Behavioral Economics* (Beginner). A memoir-history of the field by a Nobel laureate.
- Richard H. Thaler and Cass R. Sunstein, *Nudge* (Beginner). How choice architecture and defaults shape decisions.
- Robert J. Shiller, *Irrational Exuberance* (Intermediate). Bubbles, psychology and valuation.
- Charles P. Kindleberger and Robert Z. Aliber, *Manias, Panics, and Crashes* (Intermediate). The classic history of financial crises.
- Hyman P. Minsky, *Stabilizing an Unstable Economy* (Advanced). The financial instability hypothesis.
- Brad M. Barber and Terrance Odean, "Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth", *Journal of Finance* (2000) (Intermediate). Evidence on individual investors' trading.
- Hersh Shefrin, *Beyond Greed and Fear* (Intermediate). Behavioural finance for investors and managers.
- Morgan Housel, *The Psychology of Money* (Beginner). Short essays on how behaviour matters more than intelligence in investing.
- Charles Mackay, *Extraordinary Popular Delusions and the Madness of Crowds* (Beginner). The 1841 classic on manias; some of its historical claims have been challenged.
