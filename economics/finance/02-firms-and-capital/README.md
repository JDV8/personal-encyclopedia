# Firms and capital: companies, statements and securities

Securities are claims on companies, so this section explains the thing being claimed on. It covers what a company is and why the corporation with limited liability became the vehicle for raising capital from strangers, the two basic ways of financing a firm (equity and debt), the three financial statements in which firms report themselves to investors, the general definition of a security, and finally how the mix of debt and equity affects risk, value and the cost of capital. The worked examples use one small imaginary company, Maple Bakery Ltd., so that the balance sheet, income statement and cash flow statement can be seen to tie together.

**Prerequisites:** [Foundations](../01-foundations/README.md), especially present value, risk and return.

## Suggested reading order

Read in numerical order. Lessons 01 to 03 are about legal and financial form: companies, limited liability, equity versus debt. Lessons 04 to 06 are the three statements, which build on each other (balance sheet, then income statement, then cash flow). Lesson 07 defines a security and lesson 08 ties together leverage and the cost of capital.

## References & further reading

- Richard A. Brealey, Stewart C. Myers and Franklin Allen, *Principles of Corporate Finance* (Intermediate). The standard textbook on capital structure, cost of capital and valuation.
- Aswath Damodaran, *Applied Corporate Finance* (Intermediate). Practical treatment of financing, WACC and investment decisions, with many real examples.
- Benjamin Graham, *The Intelligent Investor* (Beginner to Intermediate). Chapters on reading company financials from an investor's point of view.
- Thomas R. Ittelson, *Financial Statements: A Step-by-Step Guide to Understanding and Creating Financial Reports* (Beginner). A plain-language guide to the balance sheet, income statement and cash flow statement.
- Karen Berman and Joe Knight, *Financial Intelligence* (Beginner). A manager's-eye introduction to reading financial statements.
- Ronald H. Coase, "The Nature of the Firm", *Economica* (1937) (Advanced). The classic short paper on why firms exist.
- Franco Modigliani and Merton H. Miller, "The Cost of Capital, Corporation Finance and the Theory of Investment", *American Economic Review* (1958) (Advanced). The original capital-structure irrelevance proposition.
- William N. Goetzmann, *Money Changes Everything* (Intermediate). Includes the story of joint-stock companies and early corporate finance.
