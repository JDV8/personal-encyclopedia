# Derivatives

Derivatives are contracts whose value depends on something else. This branch begins with why they exist (hedging, speculation, arbitrage) and the difference between exchange-traded and over-the-counter contracts. It then covers forwards and futures, options and their payoffs, the pricing logic of replication that produced the Black–Scholes model, and finally swaps and credit derivatives, which were central to the 2008 crisis. Each lesson keeps the payoff arithmetic explicit so you can see exactly who wins and loses in each scenario.

**Prerequisites:** [Foundations](../../01-foundations/README.md); [Stocks](../01-stocks/README.md) and [Bonds](../02-bonds/README.md) give the underlying assets.

## Suggested reading order

Read 01 to 03 in order. Lesson 04 is practice with payoff shapes; do the arithmetic by hand once. Lesson 05 is the most mathematical; the one-step binomial example is the core and the Black–Scholes formula can be treated as a result on a first pass. Lesson 06 stands somewhat apart and connects to the history section on 2008.

## References & further reading

- John C. Hull, *Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives* (Intermediate to Advanced). The standard textbook for derivative markets and pricing.
- John C. Hull, *Fundamentals of Futures and Options Markets* (Intermediate). A shorter, less mathematical version.
- Lawrence G. McMillan, *Options as a Strategic Investment* (Intermediate). A practitioner's guide to option strategies.
- Sheldon Natenberg, *Option Volatility and Pricing* (Intermediate). Trading-oriented treatment of volatility and the Greeks.
- Fischer Black and Myron Scholes, "The Pricing of Options and Corporate Liabilities", *Journal of Political Economy* (1973) (Advanced). The original paper.
- Robert C. Merton, "Theory of Rational Option Pricing", *Bell Journal of Economics and Management Science* (1973) (Advanced). Extends the model by continuous replication.
- Peter L. Bernstein, *Against the Gods: The Remarkable Story of Risk* (Beginner). A history of ideas about risk, including options and Black–Scholes.
- Roger Lowenstein, *When Genius Failed* (Beginner). The story of Long-Term Capital Management and derivative-driven leverage.
- Gillian Tett, *Fool's Gold* (Beginner). How credit derivatives and CDOs developed at J.P. Morgan and spread before 2008.
- Donald MacKenzie, *An Engine, Not a Camera* (Advanced). A sociological history of financial models including Black–Scholes.
