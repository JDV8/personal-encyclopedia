# Funds

Pooling money lets small investors own a diversified portfolio. This branch starts with the mutual fund and the idea of net asset value, then covers the exchange-traded fund and how creation and redemption keep its price in line. It explains index funds and the arithmetic case for them, shows with numbers how fees compound against investors, and ends with the more exclusive pooled vehicles: closed-end funds, hedge funds and private equity.

**Prerequisites:** [Foundations](../../01-foundations/README.md), especially correlation and diversification; [What a security is](../../02-firms-and-capital/07-what-is-a-security.md).

## Suggested reading order

Read in order. Lessons 01 to 03 describe the three main retail vehicles, and lesson 04 is the one most worth absorbing: it quantifies the cost of fees. Lesson 05 is context for the high-fee, private end of the spectrum and can be skimmed on a first pass.

## References & further reading

- John C. Bogle, *The Little Book of Common Sense Investing* (Beginner). The founder of Vanguard on why low-cost index funds win.
- Burton G. Malkiel, *A Random Walk Down Wall Street* (Beginner). Includes the case for indexing and a survey of fund performance.
- William J. Bernstein, *The Four Pillars of Investing* (Beginner to Intermediate). Theory, history, psychology and business of investing, with an index-fund approach.
- Larry E. Swedroe, *The Only Guide to a Winning Investment Strategy You'll Ever Need* (Beginner). A plain-language case for evidence-based, low-cost investing.
- Zvi Bodie, Alex Kane and Alan J. Marcus, *Investments* (Intermediate). The chapters on investment companies and performance evaluation.
- Sebastian Mallaby, *More Money Than God* (Intermediate). A history of hedge funds.
- Andrew W. Lo, *Hedge Funds: An Analytic Perspective* (Advanced). A rigorous treatment of hedge fund strategies and risk.
