# Stocks

Shares are claims on the residual profits of a company. This branch starts with what a share is and what rights it carries, then separates the main classes (common and preferred), then follows the money paid out to shareholders (dividends, buybacks, splits). It then moves to valuation: earnings per share, the price-to-earnings ratio and its relatives, and finally the two core valuation models, the dividend discount model and discounted cash flow. By the end you should be able to explain what a share price means and where a valuation actually comes from.

**Prerequisites:** [Foundations](../../01-foundations/README.md) and [Firms and capital](../../02-firms-and-capital/README.md).

## Suggested reading order

Read 01 to 04 for the instrument and what flows to its owner. Read 05 to 07 for the measures investors use to compare prices with earnings and assets. Read 08 and 09 last, because they are the formal models behind the ratios. The bonds branch (next) uses the same discounting machinery with simpler cash flows, so it is fine to read bonds between lessons 04 and 05 if you prefer to see a mechanical example first.

## References & further reading

- Benjamin Graham, *The Intelligent Investor* (Beginner to Intermediate). The classic on value investing, margin of safety and investor temperament; read a revised edition with Jason Zweig's commentary for modern context.
- Benjamin Graham and David Dodd, *Security Analysis* (Advanced). The 1934 foundational text on analysing stocks and bonds.
- Burton G. Malkiel, *A Random Walk Down Wall Street* (Beginner). Valuation, firm foundations and castles in the air, plus a case for index investing.
- Peter Lynch with John Rothchild, *One Up on Wall Street* (Beginner). A fund manager's practical advice on understanding companies.
- Aswath Damodaran, *The Little Book of Valuation* (Beginner). A short, clear guide to DCF and relative valuation.
- Aswath Damodaran, *Investment Valuation* (Advanced). The comprehensive reference for DCF, relative valuation and option-based valuation.
- Zvi Bodie, Alex Kane and Alan J. Marcus, *Investments* (Intermediate). The chapters on equity valuation cover the DDM, P/E and growth opportunities.
- John Burr Williams, *The Theory of Investment Value* (Advanced). The 1938 classic on discounting dividends.
- Robert J. Shiller, *Irrational Exuberance* (Intermediate). On valuation, the CAPE and market psychology.
