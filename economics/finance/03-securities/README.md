# Securities: stocks, bonds, funds, derivatives and alternatives

This section surveys the instruments themselves. Stocks and bonds are the two basic claims on a company (ownership and debt); funds package them; derivatives are contracts built on top of them; and alternatives are assets with different drivers. Every lesson uses the tools from the foundations section, especially present value and risk, so that valuation is derived rather than memorised.

**Prerequisites:** [Foundations](../01-foundations/README.md) and [Firms and capital](../02-firms-and-capital/README.md).

## Suggested reading order

Read stocks and bonds first, in either order, then funds, derivatives and finally alternatives. Funds make most sense once you know what they hold; derivatives make most sense once you know stocks, bonds and present value.

## References & further reading

- Zvi Bodie, Alex Kane and Alan J. Marcus, *Investments* (Intermediate). The standard university text covering all asset classes in this section.
- Burton G. Malkiel, *A Random Walk Down Wall Street* (Beginner). A readable tour of securities, valuation and the case for diversification.
- Benjamin Graham, *The Intelligent Investor* (Beginner to Intermediate). The classic on investing in stocks and bonds sensibly.
- Frank J. Fabozzi, *Bond Markets, Analysis, and Strategies* (Intermediate). The standard text on bonds.
- John C. Hull, *Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives* (Intermediate to Advanced). The standard text on derivatives.
- William J. Bernstein, *The Intelligent Asset Allocator* (Intermediate). On combining asset classes, with an emphasis on evidence.
