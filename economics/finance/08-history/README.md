# History of markets and crises

Financial theory is easier to understand once you have seen the episodes that shaped it. This section moves from ancient lending through the first joint-stock company and stock exchange, then the classic bubbles (tulips, the Mississippi and the South Sea), the growth of the New York exchange and its panics, and Canada's own market history. It continues with the 1929 crash and Depression, Bretton Woods and its breakdown, the crises of the 1980s and 1990s, the dot-com bubble, the 2008 crisis and the COVID and inflation shocks. Dates and figures are checked against standard accounts; where sources disagree, the lessons say so.

**Prerequisites:** [Foundations](../01-foundations/README.md) and [Markets](../04-markets/README.md); [Behavioural finance](../07-behavioural-finance/README.md) helps with the bubbles.

## Suggested reading order

The lessons are chronological, 01 to 12; read them in order to see how institutions respond to earlier failures. If short of time, read 02 (the first exchange), 07 (1929), 11 (2008) and 12 (the rate shock) first.

## References & further reading

- Charles P. Kindleberger and Robert Z. Aliber, *Manias, Panics, and Crashes* (Intermediate). The standard history of financial crises.
- Carmen M. Reinhart and Kenneth S. Rogoff, *This Time Is Different* (Intermediate to Advanced). Eight centuries of financial crises.
- Niall Ferguson, *The Ascent of Money* (Beginner). A readable economic history of finance from lending to crises.
- William N. Goetzmann, *Money Changes Everything* (Intermediate). Finance in history from Mesopotamia to the modern day.
- William N. Goetzmann and K. Geert Rouwenhorst (eds.), *The Origins of Value* (Advanced). Essays on the history of financial innovation.
- Anne Goldgar, *Tulipmania* (Intermediate). A modern archival study of the Dutch episode.
- Peter M. Garber, *Famous First Bubbles* (Advanced). A sceptical reading of tulips, the Mississippi and the South Sea.
- Larry Neal, *The Rise of Financial Capitalism* (Advanced). On the financial revolutions of the 17th and 18th centuries.
- John Carswell, *The South Sea Bubble* (Intermediate). A narrative history.
- John Kenneth Galbraith, *The Great Crash 1929* (Beginner). A classic short account.
- Milton Friedman and Anna J. Schwartz, *A Monetary History of the United States, 1867-1960* (Advanced). The monetary account of the Depression.
- Barry Eichengreen, *Golden Fetters* (Advanced). The gold standard and the Great Depression.
- Roger Lowenstein, *When Genius Failed* (Beginner). The story of LTCM.
- John Cassidy, *dot.con* (Beginner). The history of the internet bubble.
- Michael Lewis, *The Big Short* (Beginner). The 2008 crisis through the eyes of the people who bet against the housing market.
- Andrew Ross Sorkin, *Too Big to Fail* (Beginner). A detailed account of the 2008 crisis in the United States.
- Adam Tooze, *Crashed* (Advanced) and *Shutdown* (Advanced). Global histories of the 2008 crisis and the COVID economic shock.
- Michael Bliss, *Northern Enterprise: Five Centuries of Canadian Business* (Intermediate). A history of Canadian business and finance.
- Charles R. Geisst, *Wall Street: A History* (Intermediate). A history of American financial markets.
