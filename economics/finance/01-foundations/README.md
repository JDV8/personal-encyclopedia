# Foundations: money, time and risk

The first section of the finance course contains no stocks or bonds. It builds the two ideas everything else depends on. The first is **time**: money now is worth more than money later, which leads to interest, compounding, present value and discounting. The second is **risk**: future payments are uncertain, which leads to probability, expected value, volatility, the trade-off between risk and return, and the free lunch of diversification. A reader who masters these eleven lessons can derive most of the formulas in the later sections instead of memorising them.

**Prerequisites:** none. Comfort with percentages and exponents helps; each formula is built up from an example.

## Suggested reading order

Read in numerical order. Lessons 01 to 03 explain what money and credit are and how interest works. Lessons 04 to 06 build the time-value toolkit (future value, present value, annuities). Lesson 07 adds inflation. Lessons 08 to 11 move to uncertainty: probability, volatility, the risk–return trade-off, and diversification. Work the numeric examples yourself with a calculator or a spreadsheet; the arithmetic is the lesson.

## References & further reading

- Burton G. Malkiel, *A Random Walk Down Wall Street* (Beginner). A readable tour of risk, return and diversification, with a strong historical thread.
- Peter L. Bernstein, *Against the Gods: The Remarkable Story of Risk* (Beginner). The history of probability and risk, from Pascal and Fermat to modern portfolio theory.
- William N. Goetzmann, *Money Changes Everything: How Finance Made Civilization Possible* (Beginner). Ancient tablets, interest and the invention of financial tools, told as one story.
- Niall Ferguson, *The Ascent of Money: A Financial History of the World* (Beginner). A lively survey of credit, bonds, stocks, insurance and property.
- Charles Wheelan, *Naked Statistics* (Beginner). An accessible introduction to the probability and statistics used in this section.
- Richard A. Brealey, Stewart C. Myers and Franklin Allen, *Principles of Corporate Finance* (Intermediate). The standard textbook treatment of present value, risk and return.
- Frederic S. Mishkin, *The Economics of Money, Banking and Financial Markets* (Intermediate). Money, interest rates and banks in depth.
- Zvi Bodie, Alex Kane and Alan J. Marcus, *Investments* (Intermediate). The standard investments textbook; its early chapters cover risk, return and diversification.
- Sidney Homer and Richard Sylla, *A History of Interest Rates* (Advanced). A detailed record of interest rates across four millennia.
