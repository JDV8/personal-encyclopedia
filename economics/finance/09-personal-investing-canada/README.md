# Personal investing in Canada

This final section applies the course to a Canadian household. It begins with what to do before investing (cash flow, debt, an emergency fund), then explains the registered accounts (TFSA, RRSP and FHSA) and non-registered accounts, how investment income is taxed, how to build a simple diversified portfolio, how to compare platforms and costs, and finally scams and the common mistakes that cost investors the most. It is education, not personal advice: contribution limits and rules are given for 2026 and must be confirmed on Canada.ca (CRA) before acting.

**Prerequisites:** The earlier sections, especially [Foundations](../01-foundations/README.md), [Funds](../03-securities/03-funds/README.md), [Portfolio theory](../06-portfolio-theory/README.md) and [Behavioural finance](../07-behavioural-finance/README.md).

## Suggested reading order

Read in order. Lessons 01 to 05 are about readiness and accounts; 06 covers tax; 07 and 08 turn the portfolio theory into a plan and a platform; 09 is a checklist worth re-reading from time to time. For up-to-date limits, use the CRA pages cited in the references.

## References & further reading

- David Chilton, *The Wealthy Barber* (Beginner). The classic Canadian personal-finance book, written as a story, on saving and investing basics. Check that the edition is recent for tax details.
- Preet Banerjee, *Stop Over-Thinking Your Money!* (Beginner). A short Canadian guide to personal finance decisions.
- Andrew Hallam, *Millionaire Teacher* (Beginner). A plain-language case for low-cost indexing by a Canadian-born author and teacher.
- Jonathan Chevreau, *Findependence Day* (Beginner). A Canadian guide to financial independence and retirement planning.
- John C. Bogle, *The Little Book of Common Sense Investing* (Beginner). The case for low-cost index funds, applicable to Canadians.
- Burton G. Malkiel, *A Random Walk Down Wall Street* (Beginner). A broad introduction with a life-cycle investment guide.
- Morgan Housel, *The Psychology of Money* (Beginner). On behaviour and long-term investing.
- Canada Revenue Agency, *TFSA, RRSP and FHSA guides* on Canada.ca (Beginner to Intermediate). The authoritative, current source for limits, room and rules.
- Financial Consumer Agency of Canada and the Ontario Securities Commission's GetSmarterAboutMoney.ca (Beginner). Free, independent guides on investing, fees and fraud.
- Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (Beginner). Current information on scams and how to report them.
