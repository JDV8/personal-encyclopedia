# Markets: how trading works

Securities are only useful if they can be created and traded. This section follows the life of a trade: what an exchange does, how new securities reach the market through IPOs, the roles of brokers and dealers, and the order types you choose from. It then covers the spread and the market makers who provide liquidity, how indices summarise a market, what happens after a trade through clearing and settlement, the mechanics and dangers of margin and short selling, and finally how algorithms and high-frequency traders changed market structure.

**Prerequisites:** [Securities](../03-securities/README.md), especially [Stocks](../03-securities/01-stocks/README.md).

## Suggested reading order

Read in order. Lessons 01 to 05 give you what you need to place a sensible trade yourself. Lessons 06 to 09 explain the background machinery and can be read afterwards or when the topics come up.

## References & further reading

- Larry Harris, *Trading and Exchanges: Market Microstructure for Practitioners* (Advanced). The standard text on how markets work, from order books to market makers.
- Burton G. Malkiel, *A Random Walk Down Wall Street* (Beginner). Chapters on market structure and the performance of professional investors.
- Zvi Bodie, Alex Kane and Alan J. Marcus, *Investments* (Intermediate). Chapters on how securities are traded and on market indexes.
- Michael Lewis, *Flash Boys* (Beginner). A journalist's account of high-frequency trading and its critics.
- Scott Patterson, *Dark Pools* (Beginner). A history of the rise of electronic trading and algorithmic firms.
- Maureen O'Hara, *Market Microstructure Theory* (Advanced). The academic framework for bid-ask spreads and information in prices.
- Jay R. Ritter and Ivo Welch, "A Review of IPO Activity, Pricing, and Allocations", *Journal of Finance* (2002) (Advanced). A survey of IPO underpricing and long-run performance research.
