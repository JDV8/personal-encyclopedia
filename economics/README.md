# Economics

Economics studies how people, firms and governments allocate scarce resources and how the resulting choices add up to prices, markets and the wealth of nations. This branch currently contains one course, [finance](finance/README.md): how money, credit, securities and financial markets work. Finance is applied economics under uncertainty: it takes the ideas of scarcity, incentives and prices and asks what a claim on future money is worth and how it should be traded and held.

**Prerequisites:** none. This is the entry point for the economics branch.

## Suggested reading order

Start with the [finance course](finance/README.md) overview and read its sections in order. Natural future neighbours of finance, not yet written, are microeconomics (prices, supply, demand), macroeconomics (growth, inflation, unemployment), and the economics of banking and money.

## Contents

- [Finance](finance/README.md) — 102 lessons and a 583-entry glossary: time value, securities, markets, regulation, portfolios, behaviour, history, and personal investing in Canada.

## References & further reading

- N. Gregory Mankiw, *Principles of Economics* (Beginner). A standard first textbook covering micro and macro.
- Paul Krugman and Robin Wells, *Economics* (Beginner). A widely used alternative introduction.
- Frederic S. Mishkin, *The Economics of Money, Banking and Financial Markets* (Intermediate). The bridge from economics to finance.
- Hal R. Varian, *Intermediate Microeconomics* (Intermediate). Microeconomic foundations used in portfolio and option theory.
