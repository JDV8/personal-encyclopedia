# Religion

**Prerequisites:** none required. [Philosophy](../philosophy/README.md) helps when a religious text makes a claim about reality, knowledge, or the good life.
**Key ideas:** religion as a family of practices, stories, institutions, and claims about what is ultimate; study here is descriptive and structural first; particular traditions are nested, not dumped at the root.

"Religion" is a modern umbrella word. It groups, sometimes awkwardly, things that older cultures might have named as law, cult, wisdom, way, or simply "how things are with the gods and with us." This folder uses the word as a practical heading: traditions that organize a life around what is taken to be ultimate — usually through story, ritual, community, and some claim about obligation or salvation.

The method is the same as in the rest of the encyclopedia. The aim is not to begin with a summary of "what Christians believe" or "what the Bible says, in one page." The aim is to put first the concepts that make a later story intelligible: what kind of collection the Bible is, how a canon forms, what a gospel or a prophecy is as a genre, what world Second Temple Judaism and the Roman Empire were.

This is a study path, not a devotional manual and not a brief for unbelief. Traditional readings and historical-critical readings will both appear, marked as such. Where they differ, the difference is part of the subject.

## How this folder is organized

The only branch filled in so far is Abrahamic, and within it the Bible, because that was the first request. The nesting is deliberate. "The Bible" is not a top-level topic sitting beside "philosophy." It is a library inside a religion (Christianity's naming and arrangement of that library), inside a family of traditions (Abrahamic), inside the general heading of religion.

```
religion/
  abrahamic/
    christianity/
      bible/
        old-testament/
        new-testament/
```

Judaism's own organization of the same (or overlapping) scriptures — the Tanakh — is discussed inside the Bible foundations, because you cannot understand "Old Testament" without it. A fuller `religion/abrahamic/judaism/` branch belongs on this tree later. So does Islam. They are not omitted because they are secondary. They are not yet written.
