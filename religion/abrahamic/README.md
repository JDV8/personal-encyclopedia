# Abrahamic traditions

**Prerequisites:** [Religion](../README.md)
**Key ideas:** a family of traditions tracing themselves to Abraham; shared motifs (one God, covenant, prophecy, scripture) with real disagreements; the Bible path in this encyclopedia is entered through Christianity's collection and naming, with the Hebrew Bible kept in view.

Judaism, Christianity, and Islam are often grouped as **Abrahamic** because each, in its own way, claims a story that runs through Abraham (Avraham, Ibrahim): a person called by the one God, promised descendants and blessing, and made a kind of beginning for a people and a witness.

The grouping is useful and limited. It is useful because it flags shared structures: monotheism (won, in the biblical story, over a long historical development that scholars describe more gradually than the finished text does), the authority of revealed speech, the moral seriousness of history, and a scripture that can be read, disputed, and lived. It is limited because the three traditions are not three editions of one book, and because "Abrahamic" can hide how differently they construe law, Jesus, Muhammad, and the identity of Israel.

In the biblical narrative you will meet Abraham in [the patriarchal stories](christianity/bible/old-testament/03-abraham-isaac-jacob.md). For Jews, Abraham is the first patriarch of the covenant people. For Christians, he becomes also a figure of faith, reread through the New Testament. For Muslims, he is a hanif, a pure monotheist, closely linked to the Kaaba — a story this folder has not yet taken up in its own right.

## What is filled in, and what is not

This first pass follows Christianity's Bible: the Old Testament as Christians arrange and read it, and the New Testament. That choice follows the request for "the Old and New Testament and the stories within." It does *not* mean that Jewish reading is a mere preface to Christian reading. The foundation lessons will keep distinguishing the **Hebrew Bible (Tanakh)** from the **Christian Old Testament**, in content, order, and interpretive frame.

Still to be written, and already implied by this heading: a Judaism branch (Torah, rabbinic tradition, and Jewish ways of reading these same stories), and an Islam branch (Qur'an, prophet, and the Abrahamic claim in that key). Until they exist, be careful not to let "Bible" stand in for "Abrahamic religion."
