# Deuterocanon

The **deuterocanonical** books (from a Greek phrase meaning "second canon") are writings found in the Septuagint and in Catholic and Orthodox Bibles, but not in the Hebrew Bible or in most Protestant Old Testaments. Protestants often call them the **Apocrypha** ("hidden things"), a term that can sound dismissive; this encyclopedia uses "deuterocanon" as the more precise historic Christian word, and it still notes that Jewish and Protestant canons do not include these books as scripture.

Catholic Bibles include Tobit, Judith, Wisdom of Solomon, Sirach (Ecclesiasticus), Baruch (with the Letter of Jeremiah), 1–2 Maccabees, and the longer Greek forms of Esther and Daniel. Eastern Orthodox Bibles generally include those and also, with some variation among churches, 1 Esdras, the Prayer of Manasseh, Psalm 151, 3 Maccabees, and, in some traditions, 2 Esdras (4 Ezra) and 4 Maccabees (sometimes in an appendix). The overviews in this folder cover that wider set.

These books are Second Temple Jewish literature. They matter historically even for readers whose canon does not include them: they are the world between [Malachi](../old-testament/books/minor-prophets/malachi.md) and the [New Testament](../new-testament/README.md), the world of [Hanukkah](../glossary/terms/hanukkah.md), of wisdom in Greek, and of martyrdom theologies that [Hebrews](../new-testament/books/general-epistles/hebrews.md) and [Revelation](../new-testament/books/prophecy/revelation.md) also know. For the canonical question itself, see [canon](../02-canon-and-hebrew-bible-vs-old-testament.md) and the glossary entry [deuterocanon](../glossary/terms/deuterocanon.md).

## Suggested reading order

Start with the two Maccabees ([1 Maccabees](1-maccabees.md) for the history, [2 Maccabees](2-maccabees.md) for the theology), then [Sirach](sirach.md) and [Wisdom of Solomon](wisdom-of-solomon.md), then the tales ([Tobit](tobit.md), [Judith](judith.md), the [additions](additions-to-esther.md) and [additions to Daniel](additions-to-daniel.md)). [Baruch](baruch.md) sits well after [Jeremiah](../old-testament/books/major-prophets/jeremiah.md). The Orthodox extras can be read after the Catholic set.

## References & further reading

- David A. deSilva, *Introducing the Apocrypha* (Intermediate). The best one-volume historical introduction for these books.
- *The New Oxford Annotated Bible with Apocrypha* (Beginner). Convenient texts and short introductions.
- Daniel J. Harrington, *Invitation to the Apocrypha* (Beginner). A shorter Catholic-friendly guide.
- James H. Charlesworth, ed., *The Old Testament Pseudepigrapha* (Advanced). For 2 Esdras / 4 Ezra and related writings in a wider Second Temple frame.
