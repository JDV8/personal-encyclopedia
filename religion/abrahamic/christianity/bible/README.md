# The Bible

**Prerequisites:** [Christianity](../README.md). Helpful: [Religion](../../../README.md) and [Abrahamic traditions](../../README.md).
**Key ideas:** a library, not a single book; foundations (canon, languages, dating, genres) before stories; the Old Testament path and the New Testament path; traditional and scholarly views kept in view together.

The Bible is a collection of writings, produced over many centuries, in more than one language, by many hands, later gathered into a canon — and into more than one canon, depending on the community. To start with "what happens in the Bible" without this is like starting with Aristotle's ethics without the problem of change: you can retell content, but you will miss what kind of thing you are reading.

So this folder does not open at Genesis 1. It opens with four foundation lessons. Only then does it retell the major narrative arcs and, in the New Testament, the Gospels, Acts, Paul, and Revelation.

The stories are retold faithfully, in their own terms. Notes on history and composition are labeled as traditional or scholarly where those two regularly come apart (for example: Mosaic authorship of the Torah; dating of the Gospels; whether a prophet's book is one voice). "Scholarly" here means the mainstream of historical-critical study, which itself contains disputes. It is not a synonym for "the true view."

## References & further reading

- John Barton, *A History of the Bible: The Book and Its Faiths* (Intermediate). Composition, canon, and reception without treating "scholarly" as a single verdict.
- *The Jewish Study Bible*, 2nd ed. (Intermediate). The Tanakh in a Jewish frame, which this path keeps distinct from the Christian Old Testament.
- *The New Oxford Annotated Bible* (Beginner). A reliable annotated Christian-canon Bible for the reading path.
