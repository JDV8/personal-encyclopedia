# Law and gospel

"Law and gospel" is a later Christian pairing (especially in Lutheran theology) for the demand of God and the promise of God. It can be a useful lens, and it can flatten the Bible if "law" is made to mean only a harsh Old Testament and "gospel" only a kind New Testament.

In the Torah itself, law is a gift to a delivered people, given in a [covenant](covenant.md). The prophets accuse Israel of breaking that gift, not of having it. Jesus, in the [Sermon on the Mount](../../new-testament/stories/03-sermon-on-the-mount.md), intensifies the law and announces the kingdom. Paul opposes "works of the law" as a requirement for Gentile membership and still says the law is holy, and that love fulfills it. [James](../../new-testament/books/general-epistles/james.md) commends the "perfect law of liberty." A first-principles path is to read each of those settings first, and only then to use a systematic contrast, if it still helps.
