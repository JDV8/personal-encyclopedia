# Prophets and prophecy


![La Prophétie d'Isaïe The prophecy of Isaiah. prophecy…](images/prophets-and-prophecy.jpg)

*La Prophétie d'Isaïe The prophecy of Isaiah. prophecy… Credit: Louis-Félix Legendre. License: Public domain. Source: [File:Legendre - La Prophétie d'Isaïe.JPG](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Legendre_-_La_Prophétie_d'Isaïe.JPG).*
Prophecy in the Bible is a word from the Lord into a present, usually political and moral, situation. Prediction can be part of it; it is not the whole. The Former Prophets (Joshua through Kings) already tell Israel's story under that word; the Latter Prophets collect oracles, visions, and narratives in the names of Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and the Twelve. [Daniel](../../old-testament/books/major-prophets/daniel.md), in the Christian Old Testament a prophet, stands among the Writings in the Tanakh.

True and false prophecy is itself a biblical problem (Jeremiah and Hananiah; Deuteronomy's tests). The New Testament assumes the same world: John the Baptist, Jesus as a prophet mighty in deed and word, named prophets in Acts, and the book of [Revelation](../../new-testament/books/prophecy/revelation.md) as a Christian prophecy. "To prophesy" in Paul can also mean speech that builds up an assembly.

A first-principles reader should learn the historical settings (eighth-century Assyria, late-monarchic Babylon, Persian restoration) before treating the books as a quarry of isolated future-predictions. The lesson [The prophets](../../old-testament/08-prophets.md) and the book overviews are the path.
