# Pontius Pilate

Pontius Pilate was the Roman prefect of Judea (usually dated 26–36 CE) under whom Jesus was crucified, a fact also noted by Tacitus and, with a hostile portrait, by Josephus and Philo. The Gospels portray him as reluctant and still responsible. Historical study tends to see a harsher official than the evangelists sometimes allow, and a Jewish-Roman collaboration in a colonial legal process. He is named in the later Christian creeds as a date-stamp: this death happened in public history.
