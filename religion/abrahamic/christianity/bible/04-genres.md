# Genres

**Prerequisites:** [Languages, dating, and composition](03-languages-dating-and-composition.md)
**Key ideas:** genre is a first-principles tool for reading; narrative, law, poetry, prophecy, wisdom, apocalyptic, gospel, and letter each ask to be read differently; mixing them up produces false problems.

If you read a proverb as a law, or an apocalypse as a newspaper, or a lament as a doctrinal essay, you will misunderstand even if you are sincere. **Genre** is the name for the kind of writing in front of you, and for the expectations it invites. The Bible is unusually mixed. Learning the main kinds is part of learning what the Bible is.

## Narrative and law

Large stretches of the Torah and the Former Prophets are **narrative**: stories of origins, ancestors, exodus, conquest, judges, and kings. Narrative can be told as history, as origin story, as theological drama, or as some blend. The text itself rarely labels which. Traditional readers have often taken the main narrative as historical unless there is a reason not to. Scholarly readers ask, book by book, what kind of history is being written (royal chronicle, national epic, novella, and so on) and how close the writing stands to the events.

**Law** (or instruction) appears especially in Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, and Deuteronomy: case law, ritual rules, dietary commands, and the great covenant speeches. Ancient Near Eastern law collections (such as Hammurabi's) offer comparisons: the biblical codes both resemble and diverge from their neighbors. Law in the Bible is also embedded in story — given at Sinai, rehearsed on the edge of the land — so it is never only a statute book.

## Poetry, prayer, and wisdom

**Psalms** are prayers and hymns: praise, lament, thanksgiving, royal songs, and more. Parallelism (saying a thing twice, with a shift) is the basic technique of Hebrew poetry, not rhyme. **Wisdom literature** (Proverbs, Job, Ecclesiastes, and some Psalms; also Sirach and Wisdom in wider canons) asks how to live skillfully in the world God made. Proverbs often sounds confident: the wise are rewarded. Job and Ecclesiastes press back on that confidence. Reading "the Bible's view of suffering" from only one of these is a genre mistake.

The **Song of Songs** is love poetry, interpreted both erotically and allegorically across Jewish and Christian history. That history of reading is itself part of the book’s life.

## Prophecy and apocalyptic

**Prophecy** in the biblical sense is not primarily prediction, though it includes oracles about the future. The prophets are more often covenant prosecutors and public interpreters of their moment: they name injustice, idolatry, and false security, and they also speak hope, restoration, and a coming day of the Lord. Poetry and symbolic action are normal for them. See [the prophets](old-testament/08-prophets.md).

**Apocalyptic** writing (Daniel in part; Revelation; parts of other books) unveils hidden realities in visions, symbols, numbers, and cosmic conflict. It usually speaks to a crisis in the present under the figure of a last unveiling. It is a kind of resistance literature as well as a theology of God's final justice. Reading it as a linear timetable of next year's news is a modern habit, not a reliable guide to the genre. See [Revelation](new-testament/06-revelation.md).

## Gospel and letter

A **gospel** is a distinctive early Christian genre: a narrative proclamation of good news about Jesus, not a modern biography and not only a sayings collection. The four canonical gospels share a family resemblance and still differ in outline and emphasis. See [the four Gospels](new-testament/02-the-four-gospels.md).

A **letter** (epistle) is addressed to a situation: a church, a person, a cluster of communities. Paul's letters are earlier than the written Gospels, in the view of most scholars, and they are occasional — they answer questions and conflicts. Reading a sentence from 1 Corinthians as if it were a timeless proverb, without the occasion, is another genre mistake. See [Paul's letters](new-testament/05-pauls-letters.md).

Once these kinds are in your ear, the stories can be retold without flattening them into a single type of writing.
