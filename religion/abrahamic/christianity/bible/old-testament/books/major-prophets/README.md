# Major prophets

In Christian Bibles the "major" prophets are Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and Daniel, with Lamentations placed after Jeremiah because tradition links the poems to him. "Major" refers to the length of the scrolls, not to greater inspiration. In the Tanakh, Isaiah, Jeremiah, and Ezekiel stand among the Latter Prophets, Lamentations and Daniel among the Writings. That difference of placement, especially for Daniel, is part of how communities have heard genre and authority; see [canon](../../../02-canon-and-hebrew-bible-vs-old-testament.md).

These overviews should be read with the narrative lesson [The prophets](../../08-prophets.md) and with [Exile and return](../../10-exile-and-return.md). Each file presents traditional and scholarly views of authorship and date, an outline, and the book's afterlife in Jewish and Christian reading.

## Suggested reading order

Canonical order. Isaiah is the longest and is often best read in its three large movements (1–39, 40–55, 56–66). Jeremiah and Ezekiel are companions in the same catastrophe, one mostly in Jerusalem, one in exile. Lamentations can be read in a single sitting after Jeremiah. Daniel pairs the court tale and the vision.

## References & further reading

- Brevard S. Childs, *Isaiah* (Advanced). A canonical reading that still knows the critical debate about the book's growth.
- William L. Holladay, *Jeremiah* (Advanced), or Robert P. Carroll, *Jeremiah* (Advanced), for contrasting scholarly approaches.
- Daniel I. Block, *The Book of Ezekiel* (Intermediate/Advanced). A full, accessible scholarly commentary.
- John J. Collins, *Daniel* (Advanced). The standard critical commentary on the book as Second Temple apocalyptic, to be read alongside traditional treatments.
