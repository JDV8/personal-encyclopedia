# History

These twelve books, in the Christian Old Testament order, run from the entry into the land to Jewish life under Persia: Joshua, Judges, Ruth, 1–2 Samuel, 1–2 Kings, 1–2 Chronicles, Ezra, Nehemiah, and Esther. In the Tanakh the same writings are split between the Former Prophets (Joshua–Kings) and the Writings (Ruth, Chronicles, Ezra–Nehemiah, Esther), and they stand in a different sequence. That difference is part of [canon](../../../02-canon-and-hebrew-bible-vs-old-testament.md).

Read them as narrative theology, not as a modern textbook of Near Eastern history. Traditional reading takes them as reliable sacred history. Historical-critical study asks about sources (including a Deuteronomistic edition of Joshua–Kings), date, and the relationship to archaeology and imperial records. Both frames are noted in the overviews. The [story lessons](../../README.md) retell the main arcs; these files study each book as a book.

## Suggested reading order

Canonical order, with these companions: [Joshua and Judges](../../06-joshua-and-judges.md), [Saul, David, and Solomon](../../07-saul-david-solomon.md), [the prophets](../../08-prophets.md) alongside Kings, and [Exile and return](../../10-exile-and-return.md) alongside the end of Kings, Chronicles, Ezra, and Nehemiah. Ruth and Esther can be read in one sitting each.

## References & further reading

- Robert Alter, *The David Story* (Intermediate). A literary translation and commentary on 1–2 Samuel.
- Iain Provan, V. Philips Long, and Tremper Longman III, *A Biblical History of Israel* (Intermediate). A more conservative historical synthesis to read alongside critical introductions.
- John J. Collins, *Introduction to the Hebrew Bible* (Intermediate). Balanced critical introductions to each book in this section.
- Sara Japhet, *I and II Chronicles* (Advanced). The major modern commentary on the Chronicler's theology and sources.
