# Law / Torah

The first five books — Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy — are the Torah in Jewish tradition and the Pentateuch in scholarly and Christian usage. They move from the origin of the world and of Israel's ancestors, through deliverance from Egypt and covenant at Sinai, to Moses' farewell on the edge of the land. Traditional teaching attributes them to Moses. Historical-critical study describes a long compositional history (classically the Documentary Hypothesis of J, E, D, and P, now often restated as Priestly and non-Priestly strands and later editing). Both frames are laid out in the book files and in [languages, dating, and composition](../../../03-languages-dating-and-composition.md).

## Suggested reading order

Read in canonical order, with the story lessons as narrative companions: [Creation and Eden](../../01-creation-and-eden.md) with Genesis 1–3; [Cain, the Flood, and Babel](../../02-cain-abel-flood-babel.md) and [Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob](../../03-abraham-isaac-jacob.md) and [Joseph](../../04-joseph.md) with the rest of Genesis; [Moses, the Exodus, and Sinai](../../05-moses-exodus-and-sinai.md) with Exodus (and Numbers in the wilderness). Leviticus and Deuteronomy are best read as books, not only as background.

## References & further reading

- Nahum M. Sarna, *Exploring Exodus*, and his JPS Torah commentary on Genesis and Exodus (Intermediate). Clear, learned Jewish commentary on the narrative books.
- Jacob Milgrom, *Leviticus* (Advanced). The major modern scholarly commentary on the ritual and holiness material.
- Richard Elliott Friedman, *Who Wrote the Bible?* (Beginner). A readable presentation of the documentary approach; not the last word, but a good entry to the authorship debate.
- S. R. Driver, *A Critical and Exegetical Commentary on Deuteronomy* (Advanced). A classic historical-critical treatment of the "second law."
