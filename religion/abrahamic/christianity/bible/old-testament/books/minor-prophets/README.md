# Minor prophets

The twelve shorter prophetic books — Hosea through Malachi — were written on a single scroll in ancient Judaism and are still called the Book of the Twelve (or, in older Christian usage, the Minor Prophets). "Minor" means shorter, not less important. In the Tanakh they close the Latter Prophets; in Christian Bibles they close the Old Testament.

They span at least four centuries, from the eighth-century words of Amos, Hosea, and Micah, through the late-monarchic and exilic horizons of Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah, and Obadiah, to the Persian-period preaching of Haggai, Zechariah, and Malachi. Joel and Jonah are harder to date. The companion lesson is [The prophets](../../08-prophets.md).

## Suggested reading order

Canonical order is the default. A historical grouping is also useful: eighth century ([Hosea](hosea.md), [Amos](amos.md), [Micah](micah.md), and the setting of [Jonah](jonah.md)); late seventh and early sixth ([Nahum](nahum.md), [Habakkuk](habakkuk.md), [Zephaniah](zephaniah.md), [Obadiah](obadiah.md)); post-exilic ([Haggai](haggai.md), [Zechariah](zechariah.md), [Malachi](malachi.md)); with [Joel](joel.md) placed by theme (the day of the Lord) as much as by date.

## References & further reading

- James Limburg, *Hosea–Micah* (Beginner). A short, clear commentary on the first half of the Twelve.
- David L. Petersen, *Haggai and Zechariah 1–8* and *Zechariah 9–14 and Malachi* (Advanced). Standard critical commentaries on the restoration prophets.
- Ehud Ben Zvi and James D. Nogalski (essays collected in various "Book of the Twelve" volumes) (Advanced). For readers who want the scroll as a planned collection, not only twelve separate booklets.
- Abraham J. Heschel, *The Prophets* (Intermediate). Not a book-by-book commentary, but the classic modern account of prophetic pathos, with deep attention to Hosea, Amos, and their companions.
