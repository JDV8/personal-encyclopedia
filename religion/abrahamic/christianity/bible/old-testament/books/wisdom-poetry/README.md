# Wisdom and poetry

Five books stand here in the Christian Old Testament: Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, and the Song of Songs. In the Tanakh they belong to the Writings (*Ketuvim*), not to a separate "wisdom" division, and they are not grouped in this order (the Five Scrolls, for example, place the Song, Ruth, Lamentations, Ecclesiastes, and Esther together for festival reading). The category "wisdom literature" is nonetheless useful: Job, Proverbs, and Ecclesiastes (with [Sirach](../../../deuterocanon/sirach.md) and the [Wisdom of Solomon](../../../deuterocanon/wisdom-of-solomon.md) in the deuterocanon) share a concern with skill for living, the fear of the Lord, and the problem of how the world actually goes. Psalms is first of all prayer and hymn. The Song is love poetry.

The companion lesson is [Wisdom literature](../../09-wisdom-literature.md). These overviews study each book as a book.

## Suggested reading order

[Proverbs](proverbs.md) first, as the baseline of conventional wisdom; then [Job](job.md) and [Ecclesiastes](ecclesiastes.md), which test it; [Psalms](psalms.md) as the prayer book of the whole canon (it can also be read throughout); [Song of Songs](song-of-songs.md) last in this section.

## References & further reading

- Robert Alter, *The Wisdom Books* and *The Book of Psalms* (Intermediate). Literary translations that keep the poetry audible.
- Michael V. Fox, *Proverbs 1–9* and *Proverbs 10–31* (Advanced). The major modern scholarly commentary on Proverbs.
- C. L. Seow, *Ecclesiastes* (Advanced) and *Job 1–21* (Advanced). Careful philological and theological commentary.
- Tremper Longman III, *Song of Songs* (Intermediate). A readable treatment of the literal sense and the history of allegorical reading.
