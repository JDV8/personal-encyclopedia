# Old Testament

**Prerequisites:** the four Bible foundation lessons, starting with [What the Bible is](../01-what-the-bible-is.md). At minimum, read [canon](../02-canon-and-hebrew-bible-vs-old-testament.md) and [genres](../04-genres.md).
**Key ideas:** "Old Testament" is the Christian name and order; the path follows the main narrative from creation through exile and return, then the prophets and wisdom books as their own kinds of writing.

This folder retells the core stories and, where the tradition is less a single story than a body of speech (prophets, wisdom), explains what kind of speech it is. It uses the Christian heading "Old Testament" because this branch sits under Christianity's Bible, and it notes the Tanakh's different arrangement when that changes the sense of the whole.

The narrative path here is: origins and ancestors (Genesis), deliverance and covenant (Exodus and Sinai), life in the land (Joshua, Judges), kingship (Saul, David, Solomon, and the split that follows), the prophets' intervention, wisdom's questions, and the trauma and afterlife of exile.

Traditional Jewish and Christian reading has often taken this arc as a continuous history authored or guaranteed by God, with Moses as the central human author of the Torah. Historical-critical study typically sees a literature that reached its present form over a long period, using older traditions, and that sometimes tells the past in order to address a later audience (especially the exile and after). The retellings below try to be faithful to the received story first, then to flag where those two views regularly diverge.
