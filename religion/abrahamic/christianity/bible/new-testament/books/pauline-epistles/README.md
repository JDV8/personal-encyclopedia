# Pauline epistles

Thirteen letters in the New Testament are attributed to Paul, from [Romans](romans.md) to [Philemon](philemon.md). ( [Hebrews](../general-epistles/hebrews.md) follows Philemon in many older arrangements but is treated in this encyclopedia among the general epistles, because it is formally anonymous and is not, in modern scholarly judgment, Pauline.) The survey lesson is [Paul's letters](../../../05-pauls-letters.md); the narrative companions are [Acts](../history/acts.md) and [Paul's journeys](../../../stories/09-pauls-journeys.md).

Seven letters are almost universally accepted as written by Paul: Romans, 1–2 Corinthians, Galatians, Philippians, 1 Thessalonians, and Philemon. Ephesians, Colossians, and 2 Thessalonians are debated. The Pastoral Epistles (1–2 Timothy, Titus) are widely regarded in critical scholarship as later letters in Paul's name, and are still defended as Pauline by many traditional and conservative scholars. Each overview reports that conversation and then reads the letter as a letter.

## Suggested reading order

A historical path: [1 Thessalonians](1-thessalonians.md), [Galatians](galatians.md), [1 Corinthians](1-corinthians.md), [2 Corinthians](2-corinthians.md), [Romans](romans.md), then the prison letters ([Philippians](philippians.md), [Philemon](philemon.md), [Colossians](colossians.md), [Ephesians](ephesians.md)), then [2 Thessalonians](2-thessalonians.md) and the Pastorals. A canonical path simply follows the list below, which is ordered roughly by descending length of the churches' letters, then the Pastorals, then Philemon.

## References & further reading

- N. T. Wright, *Paul and the Faithfulness of God* (Advanced), or, more briefly, *Paul: A Biography* (Beginner). A major contemporary portrait, to be read alongside rival accounts.
- E. P. Sanders, *Paul and Palestinian Judaism* (Advanced). A founding text of the New Perspective.
- James D. G. Dunn, *The Theology of Paul the Apostle* (Intermediate/Advanced). A systematic walk through the undisputed letters.
- Luke Timothy Johnson, *The First and Second Letters to Timothy* (Intermediate). A learned defense of Pauline authorship of the Pastorals, useful as a counterpoint to the critical majority.
