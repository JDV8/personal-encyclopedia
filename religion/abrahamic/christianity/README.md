# Christianity

**Prerequisites:** [Abrahamic traditions](../README.md)
**Key ideas:** Christianity as a movement that begins inside Judaism and rereads Israel's scriptures; the Bible as the first-principles entry, before later creeds and denominations; "Old" and "New" as a Christian framing of the library.

Christianity is a family of communities that confess Jesus of Nazareth as the Messiah (Christ) and, in the forms that became historically dominant, as somehow one with the God of Israel. It begins as a Jewish movement in the first century CE, in the world described in [Second Temple Judaism and Rome](bible/new-testament/01-second-temple-judaism-and-rome.md). It becomes, over the following centuries, a predominantly Gentile religion and, eventually, an imperial one.

Later Christian theology — Trinity, creeds, church offices, the split of East and West, the Reformation — matters enormously. It is not where this encyclopedia starts. Those developments presuppose a set of writings and a set of stories. The first-principles path is therefore **the Bible**: what it is, how it was formed, and what the narratives actually say.

Even the names on the spine are already interpretive. "Old Testament" and "New Testament" are Christian titles. They imply a covenant structure and a fulfillment claim. Jewish readers have a Bible (or rather a Tanakh, and then a much larger literature of interpretation) without a "New Testament" and without calling their scriptures old. Reading honestly means seeing that framing, not pretending it is neutral.
