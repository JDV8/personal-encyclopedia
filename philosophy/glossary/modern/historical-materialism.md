# Historical materialism

Marx and Engels's theory of history, according to which the way a society produces its material life (its "forces" and "relations" of production) forms the economic base that shapes its legal, political and ideological "superstructure". History advances as developing productive forces come into conflict with existing relations of production, producing class struggle and revolution: from ancient slavery to feudalism to capitalism, and, they predicted, to communism. Critics question its determinism and predictive record; defenders like G. A. Cohen reconstructed it analytically.
