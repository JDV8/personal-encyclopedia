# Teleological suspension of the ethical

A phrase from Kierkegaard's *Fear and Trembling* (1843), written under the pseudonym Johannes de silentio. Abraham's willingness to sacrifice Isaac at God's command cannot be justified by universal ethics (which says he is a murderer); if Abraham is not simply a murderer, then there must be a higher *telos* (end) for which the ethical can be suspended: the individual's absolute relation to God. Johannes is both awed and appalled. The idea remains controversial, raising questions about religious violence and divine command ethics.
