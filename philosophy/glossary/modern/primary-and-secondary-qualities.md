# Primary and secondary qualities

A distinction, made by Galileo, Descartes, Boyle and above all Locke, between qualities that objects really have and that resemble our ideas of them (size, shape, motion, number, solidity), and qualities that are only powers in objects to produce sensations in us (colour, taste, smell, sound, warmth). The sugar is not "sweet" as it is extended; sweetness is in the perceiver. Berkeley argued that the distinction collapses: primary qualities are perceived only along with secondary ones, so both are mind-dependent.
