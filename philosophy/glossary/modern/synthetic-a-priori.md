# Synthetic a priori

Judgments that extend our knowledge (synthetic) yet are known independently of experience (a priori), such as, according to Kant, "7 + 5 = 12", the truths of geometry, and "every event has a cause". Kant argued that they are possible because they express the forms of our intuition (space and time) and the categories of our understanding, which structure all possible experience. Empiricists and logical positivists denied such judgments exist; non-Euclidean geometry and modern physics were taken by many as evidence against Kant's examples.
