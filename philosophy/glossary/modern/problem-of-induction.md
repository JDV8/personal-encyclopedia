# Problem of induction

The problem, posed sharply by David Hume, of justifying inferences from observed cases to unobserved ones: from "the sun has always risen" to "the sun will rise tomorrow". Such inferences assume that the future will resemble the past, but this cannot be proved by reason (its denial is not contradictory) or by experience (that would be circular). Hume concluded that we rely on custom. Kant, Mill, Russell, Popper, Goodman (with his "new riddle") and Bayesians have offered responses; none commands agreement.
