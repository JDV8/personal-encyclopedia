# Ockham's razor

The principle of parsimony: do not multiply entities beyond necessity, or more generally, prefer the simpler explanation when two account equally well for the facts. It is named after the fourteenth-century Franciscan William of Ockham, who used such reasoning against the realist theory of universals, though the familiar Latin formula (*entia non sunt multiplicanda praeter necessitatem*) is not found in his writings. The razor is a methodological rule, not a proof: simpler theories can be false, and what counts as "simpler" is often disputed.
