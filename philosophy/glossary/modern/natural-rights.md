# Natural rights

Rights human beings possess by nature, prior to and independent of government, such as Locke's rights to life, liberty and property. Governments exist to secure them, and lose legitimacy if they violate them. The idea shaped the American Declaration of Independence and the French Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen (1789). Bentham dismissed natural rights as "nonsense upon stilts"; Burke preferred inherited rights; Arendt noted that refugees without a state found them unenforceable. Human rights are their modern descendant.
