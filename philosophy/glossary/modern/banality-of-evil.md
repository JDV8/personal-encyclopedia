# Banality of evil

Hannah Arendt's phrase, from the subtitle of *Eichmann in Jerusalem* (1963), for the way that enormous crimes can be committed by ordinary people whose motives are shallow and whose main failing is thoughtlessness: an inability to think from the standpoint of others, a reliance on clichés and official language. Arendt did not mean that the crimes were banal or that Eichmann was innocent. Later historians argued that Eichmann was a more ideological antisemite than she believed, but the concept endures.
