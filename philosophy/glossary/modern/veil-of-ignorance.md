# Veil of ignorance

A device in Rawls's *A Theory of Justice* (1971). Parties in the original position choose principles of justice without knowing their place in society, class, natural talents, intelligence, strength, conception of the good or psychological tendencies. Since no one can tailor principles to his own advantage, the principles chosen are fair. Rawls argued that such parties would choose equal liberty and the difference principle. John Harsanyi had used a similar device to argue for average utilitarianism.
