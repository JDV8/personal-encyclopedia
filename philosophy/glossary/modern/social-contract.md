# Social contract

The idea that political authority is legitimate because it rests on an agreement, actual or hypothetical, among those subject to it. Hobbes, Locke and Rousseau gave classic versions, differing in their account of the state of nature and of the terms of the agreement; Hume objected that actual governments arose by conquest and usurpation, not consent. Kant treated the contract as an idea of reason testing laws. In the twentieth century John Rawls revived contractualism with his original position.
