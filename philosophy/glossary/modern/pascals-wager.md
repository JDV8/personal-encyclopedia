# Pascal's wager

An argument in Pascal's *Pensées* for betting on God's existence. Reason cannot decide whether God exists, but we must wager. If God exists and we believe, we gain an infinite good; if not, we lose little; disbelief risks infinite loss. So prudence favours belief, and one who cannot believe should act as believers do (taking holy water, having Masses said) until belief comes. Critics point to the "many gods" problem, doubt that belief can be chosen, and question whether God would reward calculated faith. It is an early piece of decision theory.
