# Panopticism

Foucault's name, in *Discipline and Punish* (1975), for the principle of power embodied in Jeremy Bentham's Panopticon (a design of 1791): a circular building with cells around a central watchtower, from which every inmate can be seen but cannot see whether he is being watched. Because surveillance might be happening at any moment, inmates discipline themselves. Foucault argued that this principle spread throughout modern institutions, such as schools, factories, hospitals and barracks. It is now often applied to digital surveillance.
