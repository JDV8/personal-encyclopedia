# Leap of faith

An expression associated with Kierkegaard (though he did not use this exact phrase), for the movement of faith that cannot be reached by rational argument or by gradual approximation, but only by a decisive "leap" in the face of objective uncertainty and paradox. The phrase draws on Lessing's "ugly broad ditch" between historical truths and eternal truths. For Kierkegaard faith is passionate inwardness and risk. Camus criticized the leap as "philosophical suicide", an evasion of the absurd.
