# Amour-propre

French for "self-love", used by Rousseau for the relative, comparative self-love that arises in society: the desire to be esteemed by others and to rank above them. It contrasts with *amour de soi*, natural self-preservation. In the *Discourse on Inequality* Rousseau blames inflamed amour-propre for vanity, envy, competition and the vices of civilization; later scholars (e.g., Frederick Neuhouser) stress that it can also be educated toward mutual recognition. Earlier French moralists like La Rochefoucauld and Pascal used the term for selfishness.
