# Is–ought gap

The thesis, drawn from a famous paragraph in Hume's *Treatise* (Book III), that one cannot validly derive a conclusion about what *ought* to be from premises only about what *is*. Hume observed that moralists slip imperceptibly from "is" to "ought" without explaining how. G. E. Moore's related "naturalistic fallacy" argued that goodness cannot be defined in natural terms. Philosophers continue to debate whether the gap is real, whether it can be bridged (John Searle's promising argument), and what it implies for ethics.
