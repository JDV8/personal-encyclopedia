# General will

In Rousseau's *Social Contract*, the will of the political community as a whole aimed at the common good, as distinct from the "will of all", the sum of private interests. Each citizen, by obeying the general will, obeys a law he gives himself, and so remains free; whoever refuses may be "forced to be free". Admirers see in it a theory of democratic self-rule; critics such as Benjamin Constant and Isaiah Berlin feared it could justify tyranny in the name of the people.
