# Nominalism

The view that universals (such as "redness" or "humanity") are not real things existing outside the mind or in things, but only names or concepts applied to many individuals. Only individuals exist. In the medieval debate, Roscelin and especially William of Ockham defended nominalism against realists (following Plato or, more moderately, Aristotle and Aquinas). Modern nominalists, such as Nelson Goodman and W. V. O. Quine, also reject abstract objects. The debate turns on what explains the fact that different things share a property.
