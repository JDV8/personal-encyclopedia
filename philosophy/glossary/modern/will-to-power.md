# Will to power

Nietzsche's idea (*Wille zur Macht*) that the fundamental drive of living things is not self-preservation or pleasure but the expansion and expression of power: growing, mastering, overcoming resistance, including [self-overcoming](self-overcoming.md). It appears in *Thus Spoke Zarathustra* (especially "On Self-Overcoming"), *Beyond Good and Evil* (notably §§13 and 36) and *On the Genealogy of Morality*. A book titled *The Will to Power* was compiled after his death by his sister Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche and Heinrich Köselitz from notebooks; it is not a work he completed. Scholars debate whether the idea is a metaphysics (Heidegger), a psychology of self-overcoming (Kaufmann), a naturalist hypothesis about drives, or a theory of differential force (Deleuze). The full lesson is [The will to power](../../history/modern/nineteenth-century/09-will-to-power.md).
