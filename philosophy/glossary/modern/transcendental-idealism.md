# Transcendental idealism

Kant's doctrine that space and time are forms of our sensibility and that the objects we experience are appearances, not things in themselves. It is "transcendental" because it concerns the conditions of possible experience, and differs from Berkeley's idealism: Kant held that the objects of experience are empirically real, while their spatial and temporal form comes from us. It allowed him to explain synthetic a priori knowledge and to make room for freedom and faith, while denying knowledge of the soul, the world-whole and God.
