# Falsifiability

Popper's criterion, in *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* (1934), for distinguishing empirical science from non-science: a theory is scientific if it is possible in principle to refute it by observation, because it rules out some possible observations. Einstein's general relativity, which risked refutation by the 1919 eclipse observations, was Popper's model; he argued that Freudian and Adlerian psychology and Marxism (in its later forms) were unfalsifiable. Critics note that theories are tested together with auxiliary assumptions (the Duhem–Quine thesis).
