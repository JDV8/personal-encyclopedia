# Bulverism

A fallacy named by C. S. Lewis in his 1941 essay "Bulverism", later collected in *God in the Dock*. The move is to assume that an opponent is wrong and then explain *why* the opponent holds the view — by class, sex, psychology, interest or "wishful thinking" — without first showing that the view is false. Lewis invented a character, Ezekiel Bulver, who "proves" that a person is wrong by explaining the person's motives. The term is a cousin of the *argumentum ad hominem* and of what later writers call the genetic fallacy. It remains a useful label in debates about ideology, psychoanalysis and political "unmasking."
