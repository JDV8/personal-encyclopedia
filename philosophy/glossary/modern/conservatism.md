# Conservatism

In political philosophy, the family of views that treat inherited institutions, manners and attachments as a form of social knowledge, and that therefore approach radical redesign with caution. The classic statement is Edmund Burke's, written against the French Revolution: society is a partnership between the living, the dead and the yet unborn. Twentieth-century philosophical conservatism, of which [Roger Scruton](../../history/modern/twentieth-century/thinkers/roger-scruton.md) is a leading English example, often emphasises [oikophilia](oikophilia.md) (love of home), civil association between family and state, and the role of tradition in aesthetics as well as in politics. Conservatism in this sense is not identical with a party, with nationalism, or with a preference for free markets, though it has been allied with all three. Critics argue that "tradition" can be used to defend unjust inheritances.
