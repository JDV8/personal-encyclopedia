# Civil disobedience

A public, non-violent, conscientious breach of law intended to protest injustice or bring about change, usually with acceptance of the legal penalty. Thoreau's essay "Resistance to Civil Government" (1849, later titled "Civil Disobedience"), written after he was jailed for refusing a poll tax that supported slavery and the Mexican War, is a classic statement. Tolstoy, Gandhi and Martin Luther King Jr. developed the practice; John Rawls offered a theory of its role in a nearly just democracy.
