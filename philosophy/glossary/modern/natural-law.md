# Natural law

The idea that there is a moral law grounded in human nature and knowable by reason, binding on all people prior to any human legislation. Roots lie in Stoicism and Cicero; Aquinas gave the classic Christian account: the natural law is rational creatures' participation in God's eternal law, whose first precept is that good is to be done and evil avoided. In the seventeenth century Grotius, Pufendorf, Hobbes and Locke reworked natural law into theories of natural rights and the social contract. Legal positivists deny that law's validity depends on morality. C. S. Lewis, in *The Abolition of Man*, defended a closely related idea under the name of the [Tao](the-tao.md).
