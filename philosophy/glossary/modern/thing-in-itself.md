# Thing in itself

Kant's term (*Ding an sich*) for things as they are independently of how they appear to us. We know only appearances (phenomena), structured by space, time and the categories; things in themselves (noumena) can be thought but not known. Critics asked how Kant could say they cause our sensations. German idealists like Fichte and Hegel abolished the notion; Schopenhauer identified the thing in itself with the will, which we know from the inside in our own bodies.
