# Ontological argument

An argument for God's existence from the concept of God alone, without appeal to experience. In Anselm's *Proslogion* (1078), God is "that than which nothing greater can be conceived"; such a being must exist in reality, not merely in the mind, since otherwise a greater one could be conceived. Gaunilo replied with a perfect island; Aquinas rejected the argument; Descartes and Leibniz revived versions of it; Kant objected that existence is not a real predicate. Modal versions were developed in the twentieth century by Charles Hartshorne and Alvin Plantinga.
