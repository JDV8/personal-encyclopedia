# Cosmological argument

A family of arguments that reason from the existence of the world, or of change, causation or contingent things, to a first cause or necessary being. Aristotle argued for an unmoved mover; Aquinas's first three "ways" argue from motion, efficient causation and contingency; Islamic theologians developed the *kalam* version, from the world's having a beginning in time; Leibniz argued from the principle of sufficient reason. Critics such as Hume and Kant questioned whether the chain of causes requires a first member, and whether the first cause must be God.
