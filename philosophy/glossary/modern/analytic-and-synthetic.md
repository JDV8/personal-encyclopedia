# Analytic and synthetic

Kant's distinction between judgments whose predicate is contained in the subject concept ("all bodies are extended"), true by concepts alone, and judgments that add something new ("all bodies are heavy"). Combined with the a priori/a posteriori distinction, it yields Kant's central question: how are synthetic a priori judgments possible? Logical positivists held that all a priori truths are analytic. Quine's "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" (1951) challenged whether the distinction can be drawn sharply at all.
