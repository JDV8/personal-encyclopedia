# Monad

From the Greek *monas*, "unit". In Leibniz's late metaphysics, monads are the simple, unextended, soul-like substances of which everything is ultimately composed. Each has perceptions and appetitions, mirrors the whole universe from its own point of view, and has "no windows" through which anything could enter or leave. Their apparent interaction is a pre-established harmony set by God. Bodies are aggregates of monads. The word had earlier uses in Pythagorean and Neoplatonic thought and in Giordano Bruno.
