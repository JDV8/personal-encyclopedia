# Men without chests

C. S. Lewis's name, in the first lecture of *The Abolition of Man*, for people whose trained emotions have been starved by an education that treats all value judgments as mere expressions of feeling. In a psychology he takes from Plato and the medievals, the "chest" is the seat of magnanimity and rightly ordered sentiment, joining the head (reason) to the belly (appetite). Without it, reason cannot govern desire, and calls for courage or honour become idle. The phrase is a diagnosis of a moral-psychological condition, not an insult aimed at particular people. Lewis thought the condition prepared the way for "conditioners" who would treat human beings as raw material.
