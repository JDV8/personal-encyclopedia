# Methodic doubt

Descartes's procedure of doubting, once in life, everything that can be doubted, in order to find a foundation that cannot. In the *Meditations* the doubt rises in stages: the senses sometimes deceive; I may be dreaming; an evil demon might deceive me even about mathematics. Unlike the ancient sceptics, Descartes doubted to reach certainty, not suspension of judgment. The first certainty to survive is the cogito. Critics such as Peirce argued that real doubt needs a reason and cannot be adopted at will.
