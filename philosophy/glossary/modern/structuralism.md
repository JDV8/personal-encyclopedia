# Structuralism

An approach, influential in the 1950s–60s, that studies phenomena (language, myths, kinship, literature, the unconscious) as systems of relations, in which elements have meaning only through their differences from one another. It grew from Ferdinand de Saussure's linguistics, in which a sign's value lies in its differences within a language system. Claude Lévi-Strauss applied it to kinship and myth; Roland Barthes to culture; Jacques Lacan to psychoanalysis. Post-structuralists such as Derrida and Foucault questioned the stability of structures.
