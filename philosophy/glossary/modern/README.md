# Medieval and modern terms

Vocabulary from the medieval, early-modern, nineteenth-century and twentieth-century paths: the cogito and the categorical imperative, the will to live and the will to power, Dasein, bad faith, the absurd, archetypes, paradigms and mimetic desire. Each entry is a short paragraph naming who coined or used the term and linking to the thinker, text or lesson where it is unpacked.

**Prerequisites:** the [glossary overview](../README.md), and the [medieval](../../history/medieval/README.md) or [modern](../../history/modern/README.md) history overview.

## Suggested reading order

Use this list as a lookup while reading the history lessons. A good first handful, roughly in historical order: ontological argument, nominalism, cogito, problem of induction, social contract, categorical imperative, Geist, will to live, will to power, nihilism, Dasein, existence precedes essence, the absurd, paradigm, mimetic desire, the Tao (Lewis), oikophilia.

## References & further reading

- Simon Blackburn, *The Oxford Dictionary of Philosophy* (Beginner). Reliable short definitions for almost every term here.
- Ted Honderich (ed.), *The Oxford Companion to Philosophy* (Intermediate). Longer signed entries, good on the history of each term.
- Howard Caygill, *A Kant Dictionary* (Advanced). The Kantian vocabulary (a priori, autonomy, thing in itself) traced through his works.
- Michael Inwood, *A Hegel Dictionary* and *A Heidegger Dictionary* (Advanced). Untangle Geist, Aufhebung, Dasein, thrownness and their German roots.
- The *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (plato.stanford.edu) (Intermediate–Advanced). Free, scholarly entries on nearly every concept and thinker in this folder.
