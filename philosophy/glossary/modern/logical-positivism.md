# Logical positivism

A movement of the 1920s–30s centred on the Vienna Circle (Moritz Schlick, Rudolf Carnap, Otto Neurath) and the Berlin group (Hans Reichenbach), popularized in English by A. J. Ayer's *Language, Truth and Logic* (1936). Its verification principle held that a statement is meaningful only if it is either analytic (true by logic and definition) or empirically verifiable; metaphysics, theology and ethics were thus declared cognitively meaningless. Difficulties formulating the principle (is it itself verifiable?), Popper's criticism and Quine's attack on the analytic–synthetic distinction led to its decline.
