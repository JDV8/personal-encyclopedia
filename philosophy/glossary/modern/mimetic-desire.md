# Mimetic desire

René Girard's thesis that human desire is not spontaneous or directed straight at objects, but imitates the desire of others, who serve as "models" or "mediators". Desire is triangular: subject, model, object. When model and imitator are close, they come to desire the same object and become rivals, and their rivalry can escalate into violence. Girard found this structure in novelists like Cervantes, Stendhal, Proust and Dostoevsky, and built on it a theory of culture and religion.
