# Yin-yang and correlative cosmology

**Prerequisites:** [Yin-Yang and the Five Phases](../../history/ancient/chinese/08-yin-yang-and-five-phases.md)


![The teaching Confucius. Portrait by Wu Daozi, 685-758, Tang Dynasty.](images/yin-yang-and-correlative-cosmology.jpg)

*The teaching Confucius. Portrait by Wu Daozi, 685-758, Tang Dynasty. Credit: Wu Daozi, 685-758, Tang Dynasty.. License: Public domain. Source: [File:Confucius Tang Dynasty.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Confucius_Tang_Dynasty.jpg).*
Yin and yang begin as ordinary words (shady and sunny sides of a hill) and become names for complementary aspects of process: rest and activity, winter and summer, yielding and firm. They are not, in the better early uses, a moral war of bad against good, and they are not two lumps of stuff. The [Five Phases](../../glossary/chinese/wuxing.md) (*wuxing*) — Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water — are phases of activity standing in generating and overcoming cycles, not five elements in the later popular sense.

**Correlative cosmology** is the move that then links phases to seasons, colors, organs, and dynasties. Explanation here works by placing things in a web of timed, opposed, and generating relations, rather than by reducing the many to one Milesian *arche*. [Zou Yan](../../history/ancient/chinese/thinkers/zou-yan.md) is the traditional name attached to a systematic historical use of the cycle; Han thought later folded it into Confucian and medical writing. The political use (a dynasty "is" a phase) can legitimate, warn, or over-tidy the past.

## Working the idea

*Yin* and *yang* begin as ordinary contrasts — shady and sunny slopes, the north and south faces of a hill — and become, in Warring States and early imperial thought, a way of mapping the whole: dark and light, rest and activity, female and male, winter and summer, inner and outer. They are not two warring gods, and they are not, in the better texts, a lazy synonym for "opposites." They are complementary, cyclical, and nested. Each term already contains the turn toward the other. The *Yijing* (*I Ching*) and the later correlative systems (including the five phases: wood, fire, earth, metal, water) extend this habit into government, medicine, music, and the calendar: things of the same "kind" resonate; a disorder in one register can be read in another.

Zou Yan is the name traditionally attached to a grand correlative program, though the sources are later and partial. The important philosophical point does not depend on settling his biography. It is that explanation, in this style, often proceeds by placing a thing in a *pattern of correspondences* rather than by isolating a single efficient cause. A ruler's virtue, a season, a color, and an organ can belong on one table. That can look, to a reader trained only on Greek *aitiai*, like loose analogy. Inside the tradition it is a disciplined (if often over-extended) attempt to think a connected cosmos.

Work the idea by taking one concrete table (a five-phase correspondence, or a *Yijing* hexagram's imagery) and asking what it helps you notice, and where it would over-explain. Then contrast it with a Greek four-cause account of the same domain, for example a body or a political change, without declaring one style the winner on first contact. The point is to see correlative thinking as a method, not as decoration.

## Key ideas

- Yin and yang are complementary processes; *wuxing* are phases in relation, not five stuffs.
- Correlative cosmology explains by correspondence and cycle.
- Political and medical uses are historical, not optional extras.
