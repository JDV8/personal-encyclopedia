# Philosophy concepts

Granular first-principles lessons, organized by the five branches named in the [philosophy overview](../README.md). These files split ideas that the historical surveys only introduce. Each lesson includes a mermaid diagram of the main structure.

**Prerequisites:** [Philosophy](../README.md). For historical context, use the [ancient](../history/ancient/README.md) paths in parallel.

## Suggested reading order

A natural path is metaphysical problems first, then knowledge, then ethics, then logic and politics — or you can follow a tradition (Greek or Chinese) across the folders. Suggested starting files:

1. [The problem of change](metaphysics/the-problem-of-change.md)
2. [Knowledge and opinion](epistemology/knowledge-and-opinion.md)
3. [Virtue and arete](ethics/virtue-and-arete.md) and [ren and li](ethics/ren-and-li.md)
4. [Syllogism and the Organon](logic/syllogism-and-the-organon.md)
5. [The polis](political/the-polis-and-political-animal.md) and [government by virtue](political/government-by-virtue.md)

## References & further reading

- Julia Annas, *Ancient Philosophy: A Very Short Introduction* (Beginner). A short map of the same five-branch terrain.
- Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* and *Metaphysics* (Intermediate). The densest single source for many of the Greek concept lessons.
- Bryan W. Van Norden, *Introduction to Classical Chinese Philosophy* (Beginner). The clearest first map for the Chinese concept lessons.
- A. C. Graham, *Disputers of the Tao* (Advanced). Especially for names, Mohism, and Daoist critique.
