# Imperial Chinese thinkers (Song–Ming bridges)

This folder holds brief overviews of later Confucian thinkers who reshaped how the ancient books were read. They are not Warring States figures. They belong here, after the ancient path, as bridges: [Zhu Xi](zhu-xi.md) built the Four Books curriculum and a metaphysics of *li* and *qi*; [Wang Yangming](wang-yangming.md) answered him with innate knowing and the unity of knowledge and action.

**Prerequisites:** [Confucius](../../../ancient/chinese/thinkers/confucius.md), [Mencius](../../../ancient/chinese/thinkers/mencius.md), and preferably the [Great Learning](../../../ancient/chinese/texts/great-learning.md) and [Doctrine of the Mean](../../../ancient/chinese/texts/doctrine-of-the-mean.md).

## Suggested reading order

1. [Zhu Xi](zhu-xi.md)
2. [Wang Yangming](wang-yangming.md)

## References & further reading

- Wing-tsit Chan, *A Source Book in Chinese Philosophy* (Intermediate). Includes substantial, standard selections from both thinkers.
- Daniel K. Gardner, trans., *Learning to Be a Sage* (Intermediate). Zhu Xi's conversations on reading and self-cultivation, a good first primary text.
- Wing-tsit Chan, trans., *Instructions for Practical Living and Other Neo-Confucian Writings by Wang Yang-ming* (Intermediate). The main English Wang Yangming.
- Julia Ching, *To Acquire Wisdom: The Way of Wang Yang-ming* (Advanced). A careful study of his life and thought.
