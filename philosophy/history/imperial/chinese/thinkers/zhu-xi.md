# Zhu Xi

**Prerequisites:** [Confucius](../../../ancient/chinese/thinkers/confucius.md), [Mencius](../../../ancient/chinese/thinkers/mencius.md), and the [Great Learning](../../../ancient/chinese/texts/great-learning.md) and [Doctrine of the Mean](../../../ancient/chinese/texts/doctrine-of-the-mean.md)

## Name and dates

Zhu Xi (1130–1200), also written Chu Hsi, was a Song-dynasty scholar, official, and the most influential systematizer of what is often called Neo-Confucianism (*daoxue*, the Learning of the Way). He lived centuries after the Warring States classics. He belongs in this encyclopedia as a **later bridge**: the person who most shaped how those classics were later read, taught, and examined.

## Sources

His output is huge: commentaries on the Four Books, conversations recorded by students (*Zhuzi yulei*), anthologies, and official writings. In English, a starting point is the translated conversations in *Learning to Be a Sage* (Gardner) and the commentary tradition gathered in *Sources of Chinese Tradition* and Wing-tsit Chan's *A Source Book in Chinese Philosophy*. The Four Books as a curriculum — [*Analects*](../../../ancient/chinese/texts/analects.md), [*Mencius*](../../../ancient/chinese/texts/mencius.md), [*Great Learning*](../../../ancient/chinese/texts/great-learning.md), [*Doctrine of the Mean*](../../../ancient/chinese/texts/doctrine-of-the-mean.md) — are in large part his pedagogical decision, later adopted for the civil service examinations.

## Thought

Zhu Xi organizes the world through two principles. **[*Li*](../../../../glossary/chinese/li-principle.md)** (pattern, principle — a different word from ritual *li*) is the intelligible order in things, ultimately one *li* variously present. **[*Qi*](../../../../glossary/chinese/qi.md)** is the psycho-physical stuff, the vital energy, through which pattern is instantiated, sometimes clearly and sometimes turbidly. This is not ancient Zhou cosmology restated; it is a Song metaphysics that also answers Buddhism and Daoism with a Confucian account of why the world and the moral law are one.

Self-cultivation, in his program, includes **[investigating things](../../../../concepts/epistemology/investigation-of-things.md)** (*gewu*) and extending knowledge, together with reverence, reading, and the discipline of the heart-mind. He reads the *Great Learning* as a sequence from inner rectification to family, state, and peace under Heaven. He takes [Mencius](../../../ancient/chinese/thinkers/mencius.md) as the orthodox interpreter of [Confucius](../../../ancient/chinese/thinkers/confucius.md) on nature's goodness, and he is relatively hard on [Xunzi](../../../ancient/chinese/thinkers/xunzi.md). The commentaries often decide, for later centuries, what a terse Analect is "really" saying.

## Reception

From the later Song and especially the Yuan and Ming, his curriculum and many of his interpretations were official orthodoxy, which made him both a teacher of a civilization and a target. [Wang Yangming](wang-yangming.md) would reject the outward-leaning investigation of things as he understood it, in favor of innate knowing. Modern reformers sometimes blamed "Zhu Xi Confucianism" for rigidity. For this path, he is the reason the Four Books exist as a set, and the thinker through whom *li* and *qi* became the default metaphysical vocabulary of later Confucianism.

## Key ideas

- A Song synthesis: *li* (principle) and *qi* (vital stuff); Mencian nature; a Four Books curriculum.
- Cultivation includes investigating things and extending knowledge.
- He is a later reader, not a Warring States master; his power is over the received tradition.
