# Modern philosophy

"Modern" philosophy here runs from the Renaissance to the late twentieth century: from Machiavelli and Montaigne to Rawls, Foucault, Girard, Lewis and Scruton. It is where most of the names people mean by "the great philosophers" live, among them Descartes, Spinoza, Hume, Kant, Hegel, Schopenhauer, Kierkegaard, Nietzsche, Wittgenstein, Heidegger, Sartre and Camus. It also includes a few writers who were not professional philosophers but changed philosophy: the novelists Dostoevsky and Tolstoy, the psychologists Freud and Jung, the literary critic and anthropologist René Girard, and the literary scholar and Christian apologist C. S. Lewis. Roger Scruton, who was a professional philosopher, extends the path into conservatism and aesthetics. Each of those pages says so plainly.

The path has three eras, each with numbered lessons (the narrative), a thinkers/ folder (one page per person) and a texts/ folder (one page per key book):

- **Early modern (c. 1500–1800):** humanism, the new science, rationalism and empiricism, the social contract, the Enlightenment, Kant.
- **Nineteenth century:** German idealism, Schopenhauer's pessimism, utilitarianism, Marx, transcendentalism, Kierkegaard, the Russian philosophical novel, Nietzsche and the will to power.
- **Twentieth century:** phenomenology, the analytic turn, existentialism, the absurd, psychoanalysis, philosophy of science, political philosophy after totalitarianism, structuralism and Foucault, mimetic theory, then conservatism, tradition and beauty (Lewis, Scruton).

**Prerequisites:** [Medieval philosophy](../medieval/README.md) (recommended) and [Ancient Greek philosophy](../ancient/greek/README.md)

## References & further reading

- Bertrand Russell, *A History of Western Philosophy* (Beginner). Opinionated and witty; strongest on the early moderns, unfair to some (notably Nietzsche), still a great read.
- Anthony Kenny, *A New History of Western Philosophy*, vols. 3–4 (*The Rise of Modern Philosophy*; *Philosophy in the Modern World*) (Intermediate). Balanced narrative from Descartes to the late twentieth century.
- Frederick Copleston, *A History of Philosophy*, vols. 4–11 (Intermediate–Advanced). Detailed and fair, thinker by thinker.
- Roger Scruton, *A Short History of Modern Philosophy: From Descartes to Wittgenstein* (Intermediate). A compact, argument-centred survey.
- Robert C. Solomon, *Continental Philosophy since 1750: The Rise and Fall of the Self* (Intermediate). Kant to Foucault through one theme.
