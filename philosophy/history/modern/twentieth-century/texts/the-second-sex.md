## Dating and form

*Le Deuxième Sexe* was published by Gallimard in 1949 in two volumes, after parts had appeared in the journal *Les Temps modernes*. [De Beauvoir](../thinkers/simone-de-beauvoir.md) began it after asking herself what being a woman had meant for her and finding that the question opened onto the history and situation of women in general. The book was a scandal: the novelist François Mauriac publicly attacked it, and the Vatican placed it on the Index of Forbidden Books. The first English translation, by the zoologist H. M. Parshley (1953), was abridged and philosophically unreliable; a complete translation by Constance Borde and Sheila Malovany-Chevallier appeared in 2009.

## Outline

- **Introduction.** "What is a woman?" Man defines himself as the Subject, the Absolute; "she is the Other". Unlike other oppressed groups, women are dispersed among men and lack a shared history and solidarity; many are complicit because the role offers protection and escapes the risks of freedom.
- **Volume I, "Facts and Myths."** *Destiny*: biology, psychoanalysis and historical materialism each explain something but none accounts for woman's subordination; biology is not destiny. *History*: from nomadic societies to the modern era, women's situation was shaped by property, inheritance, work and religion. *Myths*: men project onto woman contradictory myths (mother, virgin, nature, temptress); de Beauvoir analyses them in Montherlant, D. H. Lawrence, Claudel, Breton and Stendhal.
- **Volume II, "Lived Experience."** It opens: "One is not born, but rather becomes, a woman." *Formative years*: childhood, the girl, sexual initiation, the lesbian. *Situation*: the married woman, the mother, social life, prostitution, old age. *Justifications*: the narcissist, the woman in love and the mystic, attempts to find meaning within the role of Other. *Toward liberation*: the independent woman, whose freedom requires economic independence and social change.

## Key ideas

- Woman has been defined as the Other relative to man.
- Femininity is a situation produced by history and upbringing, not a natural essence.
- Liberation requires economic independence and relations of reciprocity between free subjects.

## Reception

*The Second Sex* became a founding text of second-wave feminism, influencing Betty Friedan, Kate Millett and Shulamith Firestone, and later gender theory, notably Judith Butler's reading of "becoming a woman". Critics have objected to its negative descriptions of pregnancy and housework, its reliance on Sartrean categories, its focus on middle-class Western women, and its parallels between sexism, racism and antisemitism. Scholars such as Toril Moi and Nancy Bauer have defended it as serious philosophy.
