# Early modern philosophy

From roughly 1500 to 1800, European philosophy was remade by the recovery of ancient texts, the Reformation and its wars, the new mathematical science of Galileo and Newton, and the rise of the modern state. Philosophers turned from commentary on authorities to method: how can I know anything with certainty, and what can I know? The answers split into rationalism (Descartes, Spinoza, Leibniz) and empiricism (Locke, Berkeley, Hume), until Kant tried to unite them. Alongside, Machiavelli, Hobbes, Locke and Rousseau reinvented political philosophy around power, consent and rights.

## References & further reading

- Roger Scruton, *A Short History of Modern Philosophy* (Beginner–Intermediate). Clear chapters on each of the major early moderns.
- Anthony Kenny, *The Rise of Modern Philosophy* (Intermediate). Narrative plus thematic chapters on knowledge, mind, ethics and God.
- John Cottingham, *The Rationalists* (Intermediate). Descartes, Spinoza and Leibniz compared.
- R. S. Woolhouse, *The Empiricists* (Intermediate). Locke, Berkeley and Hume compared.
- Ritchie Robertson, *The Enlightenment: The Pursuit of Happiness, 1680–1790* (Intermediate). A broad, readable history of the period's ideas.
