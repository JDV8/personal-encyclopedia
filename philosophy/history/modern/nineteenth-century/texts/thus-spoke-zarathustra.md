# Nietzsche, *Thus Spoke Zarathustra*

**Prerequisites:** [[friedrich-nietzsche|Friedrich Nietzsche]]; [[nietzsche-and-the-death-of-god|Nietzsche and the death of God]]

@@IMG@@

## Dating and form

*Also sprach Zarathustra: Ein Buch für Alle und Keinen* ("A Book for All and None") appeared in four parts between 1883 and 1885; [[friedrich-nietzsche|Nietzsche]] wrote the first three parts in bursts of about ten days each, in Rapallo, Sils Maria and Nice. The fourth part was privately printed in about forty copies. The idea of eternal recurrence came to him, he said, in August 1881 by a pyramidal rock near Lake Silvaplana, "6,000 feet beyond man and time". The book is a philosophical prose poem in the style of Luther's Bible, with parables, speeches, songs and dreams. Its hero is named after Zarathustra (Zoroaster), the Persian prophet who, Nietzsche says in *Ecce Homo*, first made morality a cosmic force and so must be the first to recognize his error.

## Outline

- **Prologue.** After ten years in the mountains, Zarathustra descends like the setting sun to share his wisdom. In a marketplace he announces the **Übermensch**: "Man is something that shall be overcome." Man is a rope stretched between animal and overman, over an abyss. The crowd prefers the **last man**, who has invented happiness and blinks. A tightrope walker falls and dies; Zarathustra carries the corpse away and decides to seek companions, not crowds.
- **Part I.** Speeches: "On the Three Metamorphoses" (camel, lion, child: bearing values, destroying them, creating new ones); "On the Despisers of the Body"; "On the New Idol" (the state); "On the Gift-Giving Virtue". "Dead are all gods: now we want the overman to live."
- **Part II.** "On the Tarantulas" (preachers of equality motivated by revenge); "On Self-Overcoming" (will to power as the essence of life); "On Redemption" (the will cannot will backwards; "it was" is its torment).
- **Part III.** "On the Vision and the Riddle" and "The Convalescent": the thought of eternal recurrence, which Zarathustra struggles to bear; "The Seven Seals (or: The Yes and Amen Song)".
- **Part IV.** Zarathustra meets the "higher men" (two kings, a pope out of service, the ugliest man who killed God, and others); their "Ass Festival" parodies worship; the book ends with a sign that his hour has come.

## Key ideas

- After the death of God, humans must create values; the Übermensch is the meaning of the earth.
- The last man, who wants only comfort, is the danger.
- Will to power is the essence of life as self-overcoming.
- Eternal recurrence is the heaviest thought and the highest formula of affirmation.

## Reception

Nietzsche considered it his most important book. Richard Strauss composed a tone poem with its title (1896). Soldiers in the First World War carried it; the Nazis exploited its rhetoric. Its poetic form invites wildly different readings. Translations: Walter Kaufmann (in *The Portable Nietzsche*); R. J. Hollingdale (Penguin); Graham Parkes (Oxford World's Classics); Adrian Del Caro (Cambridge).
