# Nineteenth-century texts

Overviews of fifteen nineteenth-century books and essays: dating and form, outline, key ideas, reception and recommended editions. Three are novels or stories (Dostoevsky's two and Tolstoy's *Ivan Ilyich*) included for their philosophical weight.

**Prerequisites:** [Nineteenth-century thinkers](../thinkers/README.md)

## Suggested reading order

Short ones first if you want to read the originals: "Self-Reliance", *The Communist Manifesto*, *On Liberty*, *Notes from Underground*, *The Death of Ivan Ilyich*, *A Confession*. Then the long books: *Walden*, *Fear and Trembling*, *Beyond Good and Evil*, the *Genealogy*, *Zarathustra*, *The Brothers Karamazov*, *The World as Will and Representation*, and Hegel's *Phenomenology* last.

## References & further reading

- Friedrich Nietzsche, *Basic Writings of Nietzsche*, trans. Walter Kaufmann (Modern Library) (Intermediate). Five complete books, including *Beyond Good and Evil* and the *Genealogy*.
- Arthur Schopenhauer, *Essays and Aphorisms*, trans. R. J. Hollingdale (Penguin) (Beginner). The gentlest way in before *The World as Will and Representation*.
- Fyodor Dostoevsky, *The Brothers Karamazov*, trans. Richard Pevear and Larissa Volokhonsky (Intermediate).
- Søren Kierkegaard, *Fear and Trembling*, trans. Alastair Hannay (Penguin) (Intermediate).
