# Medieval philosophy

Medieval philosophy runs roughly from Augustine (died 430) to William of Ockham (died c. 1347): about a thousand years in which Christian, Islamic and Jewish thinkers asked how the philosophy they inherited from Plato, Aristotle and Plotinus could be reconciled with revealed scripture. The questions were not only theological. How does reason relate to faith? Can God's existence be proved? Are universals real? How can God foreknow what we freely do? Is the world eternal? The answers shaped the vocabulary of every later century.

This folder is a bridge between the [ancient](../ancient/README.md) and [modern](../modern/README.md) paths: four lessons, eight thinkers and six texts, chosen because the modern philosophers you came for (Descartes, Spinoza, Leibniz, Locke, Kant, Kierkegaard) were all arguing with them.

**Prerequisites:** [Ancient Greek philosophy](../ancient/greek/README.md), especially [Plato](../ancient/greek/thinkers/plato.md), [Aristotle](../ancient/greek/thinkers/aristotle.md) and [Plotinus](../ancient/greek/thinkers/plotinus.md)

## References & further reading

- Anthony Kenny, *Medieval Philosophy* (*A New History of Western Philosophy*, vol. 2) (Beginner). A lucid narrative from Augustine to the late scholastics, organized by period and by topic.
- Frederick Copleston, *A History of Philosophy*, vols. 2–3 (Intermediate). The classic detailed survey, sympathetic and thorough.
- Peter Adamson, *Medieval Philosophy* and *Philosophy in the Islamic World* (*A History of Philosophy without Any Gaps*, vols. 3–4) (Beginner–Intermediate). Lively short chapters covering many figures beyond this folder.
- John Marenbon, *Medieval Philosophy: A Very Short Introduction* (Beginner). A short map that questions the usual boundaries of the period.
- A. S. McGrade (ed.), *The Cambridge Companion to Medieval Philosophy* (Advanced). Thematic essays by specialists.
