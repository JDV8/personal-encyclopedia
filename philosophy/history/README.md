# History of philosophy

**Prerequisites:** [What philosophy is, and its core branches](../README.md)
**Key ideas:** history as a path into the questions, not a replacement for them; schools and periods are useful handles; ideas travel, get translated, and change meaning.

A history of philosophy can turn into a parade of names. That is not the aim here. The aim is to watch how a problem gets set, how an answer generates a new problem, and how a later thinker inherits both.

"Ancient," "medieval," and "modern" are European period labels. They are convenient for the Greek-to-European line, and misleading if they are treated as a universal clock. Early Chinese philosophy has its own periodization (Spring and Autumn, Warring States, Qin-Han synthesis, and later commentary traditions). This folder uses "ancient" as a rough contemporary window: first-millennium BCE thought, plus the Hellenistic centuries that follow in the Greek world and the early imperial synthesis in China.

When you read historically, keep the branches in mind. Ask: is this a claim about what there is, about what we can know, about how to live, about valid inference, or about political order? The same page of Aristotle or of Xunzi may do more than one of these at once.
