# History of philosophy

A history of philosophy can turn into a parade of names. That is not the aim here. The aim is to watch how a problem gets set, how an answer generates a new problem, and how a later thinker inherits both.

"Ancient," "medieval," and "modern" are European period labels. They are convenient for the Greek-to-European line, and misleading if they are treated as a universal clock. Early Chinese philosophy has its own periodization (Spring and Autumn, Warring States, Qin-Han synthesis, and later commentary traditions). This folder uses "ancient" as a rough contemporary window, and "imperial" for later Chinese bridges (Song–Ming Confucianism) that explain how the ancient books were later read.

When you read historically, keep the [branches](../README.md) in mind. Ask: is this a claim about what there is, about what we can know, about how to live, about valid inference, or about political order?

**Prerequisites:** [What philosophy is, and its core branches](../README.md)

## References & further reading

- Anthony Kenny, *A New History of Western Philosophy*, vol. 1, *Ancient Philosophy* (Intermediate). A clear narrative of the Greek and Roman line.
- A. C. Graham, *Disputers of the Tao* (Advanced). The matching depth for early China.
- Pierre Hadot, *What Is Ancient Philosophy?* (Intermediate). History as the story of schools and ways of life.
- Anthony Kenny, *A New History of Western Philosophy*, vols. 2–4 (Intermediate). The same clear narrative carried through the medieval and modern periods.
- Bertrand Russell, *A History of Western Philosophy* (Beginner). Opinionated and very readable, from the Presocratics to the early twentieth century.
