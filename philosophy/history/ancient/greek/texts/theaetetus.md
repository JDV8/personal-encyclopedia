# *Theaetetus*

**Prerequisites:** [Plato](../thinkers/plato.md); helpful: [*Meno*](meno.md)

## Dating and authorship

A late-middle dialogue on the question **what is knowledge?** It is often grouped with the *Sophist* and *Statesman* as a more technical, less mythic Plato, still using Socrates as the main speaker. A frame presents the conversation as a remembered discussion with the mathematician Theaetetus, later a wounded war hero.

## Outline and key ideas

Three definitions of knowledge are tested and found wanting. Knowledge is not **perception**, even if one develops [Protagoras](../thinkers/protagoras-and-the-sophists.md)'s "man is the measure" in a sophisticated way; the world of sheer appearing cannot account for judgment about being, difference, and number, or for the possibility of expert disagreement. Knowledge is not simply **true opinion**, because a jury can be persuaded of the truth without knowing, and because an account of false opinion proves surprisingly hard to give. Knowledge is not **true opinion with an account** (*logos*), at least not on the accounts of "account" tried here (a statement, a list of parts, a distinguishing mark). The dialogue ends in *aporia*, but it is an *aporia* rich in tools: midwifery as a metaphor for Socratic method, a critique of Heraclitean flux as a theory of perception, and a mapping of the problems any later epistemology must face.

Notably, the Theory of Forms is almost offstage. Some readers take this as a new, more "analytic" Plato; others think the failure of the three definitions *points* toward Forms as the missing object of knowledge. The text itself does not close that debate.

## Themes and reception

The *Theaetetus* is the founding work of epistemology as a self-conscious subject in the Greek line. Later skeptics, empiricists, and rationalists all find ancestors or targets in it. Read it after the *Meno* (knowledge versus true opinion) and beside the *Sophist* (the problem of not-being and false statement).

## Key ideas

- Three failed definitions: perception, true opinion, true opinion plus an account.
- A deep problem about how false judgment is possible.
- An *aporia* that still sets the agenda for "what is knowledge?"
