# Epictetus, *Discourses*

**Prerequisites:** [*Enchiridion*](enchiridion.md) or [Epictetus](../thinkers/epictetus.md)

## Dating and authorship

Four surviving books (originally more) of informal lectures, written up by Arrian from Epictetus's school at Nicopolis. The voice is more expansive, funny, and severe than the *Handbook*, and it is the fuller document of the teaching.

## Outline and key ideas

Themes of the *Handbook* are here in their native length: *prohairesis* as the true self; the use of impressions; the roles of a son, a citizen, a guest; the kinship of humans under Zeus; the shame of fearing exile, office, or death if those things were never yours. Epictetus baits students, imagines excuses, and keeps returning to freedom as a state of the will, spoken by a man who had been enslaved. The *Discourses* also show more of the school's theology and of its running fight with Epicureans, Academics, and lazy would-be philosophers.

## Reception

Marcus Aurelius knew this work. It is the main long Stoic text in Greek that a modern reader can still inhabit at length, now that Chrysippus is lost.

## Key ideas

- The living lecture record behind the *Handbook*.
- Freedom, roles, and the use of impressions, at full length.
- A formerly enslaved teacher's theology of the inner *logos*.
