# Sextus Empiricus

**Prerequisites:** [Pyrrho](pyrrho.md)

## Name and dates

Sextus Empiricus (probably late second century CE, dates uncertain) was a Pyrrhonian skeptic and, by his name and parts of his work, a physician in the "Empiric" tradition of medicine. We know almost nothing of his life. We have his books, which is rare enough in this school to make him the main voice of ancient Pyrrhonism.

## Sources

The surviving works are the *Outlines of Pyrrhonism* (*Pyrrhoneiae Hypotyposes*) and the large set often called *Against the Learned* (or *Against the Mathematicians*), which attacks the claims of grammarians, rhetoricians, geometers, theologians, and dogmatic philosophers. The *Outlines* are the place to begin: a systematic handbook of the skeptical way.

## Thought

Sextus carefully distinguishes Pyrrhonian skepticism from two failures, as he sees them: **dogmatism** (claiming to have found the truth) and **Academic skepticism** (in his picture, claiming that truth cannot be found — itself a dogma). The Pyrrhonist is still investigating. For any non-evident claim, an opposing claim can be set against it with equal force. The result is [*epoché*](../../../../glossary/greek/epoche.md), suspension of judgment, and *ataraxia* follows "like a shadow," not as a goal grimly targeted. The skeptic lives, meanwhile, by appearances, custom, the promptings of nature, and the arts — including medicine — without asserting a theory about the hidden reality behind them.

The **modes** (or tropes) are tools for producing that equipollence: arguments from differences among animals, among humans, among senses, among circumstances, positions, and mixtures; from relativity; from the dependency of impressions on quantity and culture. A later set of modes attacks causal and inferential proof itself. The writing is often repetitive by design. It is a manual, not a climax.

Sextus is also a historian of philosophy in spite of himself: to refute the schools he must report them, and so he is a major source for Stoic, Epicurean, and Aristotelian positions we would otherwise have only in fragments. Read him twice — once for the skeptical method, once as a window on everyone else.

## Reception

His works were little discussed in the medieval Latin West, then explosively important after their Renaissance recovery. Montaigne's *Apology for Raymond Sebond* is steeped in them; early modern "skepticism" is unthinkable without that revival, even when the questions have changed. For this path, Sextus is the fullest surviving account of philosophy as a disciplined refusal to conclude — and a reminder that *ataraxia* was a shared Hellenistic prize, claimed by dogmatists and skeptics alike.

## Key ideas

- Pyrrhonism, in Sextus's definition, keeps investigating; it does not assert that knowledge is impossible.
- Opposing arguments lead to *epoché*; tranquility follows as a result, while one lives by appearances.
- His handbooks are also a treasury of reports about the dogmatic schools.
