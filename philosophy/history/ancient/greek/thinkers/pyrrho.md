# Pyrrho

**Prerequisites:** the [Hellenistic schools survey](../06-hellenistic-schools.md)

## Name and dates

Pyrrho of Elis (c. 365–270 BCE) is the traditional fountainhead of Greek skepticism as a way of life. He is said to have traveled with Alexander's army to India, to have met gymnosophists (ascetic "naked philosophers"), and to have returned to live in a startling calm. Almost every biographical detail is late and may be emblem. The afterlife of his name — Pyrrhonism — is more solid than any single anecdote.

## Sources

He wrote nothing that survives. Our earliest substantial witness is his student Timon of Phlius, himself fragmentary. Later, [Sextus Empiricus](sextus-empiricus.md) (second century CE or so) wrote the handbooks that define "Pyrrhonian" skepticism for us, at a long distance. A crucial and debated report, from Aristocles via Eusebius, summarizes Pyrrho's program in three questions: what things are like, what attitude we should adopt, and what the result will be for those who adopt it. How much of later Pyrrhonism is already in Pyrrho is one of the hardest problems in Hellenistic scholarship.

## Thought

On the Aristocles report, things are equally indifferent, unmeasurable, and undecidable; we should be without beliefs (*adoxastous*) and without inclination one way or the other; the result is first speechlessness, then [*ataraxia*](../../../../glossary/greek/ataraxia.md). Other reports show a man who did not dodge carts or dogs, so indifferent was he to appearances — stories that later Pyrrhonists would reject, because they do live by appearances, they just do not claim knowledge about the hidden nature of things.

A cautious first picture: Pyrrho offered *ataraxia* not by discovering the true nature of the world (as Stoics and Epicureans claimed to) and not by denying that anything can be known as a positive dogma, but by a practice of not committing to one account over its opposite. Later Pyrrhonism will formalize this as [*epoché*](../../../../glossary/greek/epoche.md), the suspension of judgment, produced by the equal force of opposing arguments (*isosthenia*). Whether Pyrrho himself was more a radical "life without belief" or an early version of that disciplined suspension is disputed. What is clear is the contrast with the dogmatic schools: tranquility does not come from a finished physics, but from letting the demand for a finished physics go.

## Reception

The Academic skeptics ([Arcesilaus](../../../../glossary/greek/academic-skepticism.md), Carneades) developed a related, more argumentative skepticism inside Plato's Academy, often aimed at the Stoics. A later revival, known through Sextus, claimed Pyrrho as ancestor. Modern philosophy's uses of "skepticism" (Descartes, Hume) are another story again, often more about the justification of science than about a way of life. Start with Pyrrho as a Hellenistic therapist of belief, not as a modern epistemologist in a cloak.

## Key ideas

- A founder known only through later, sometimes conflicting, reports.
- Tranquility is sought by not deciding between opposed accounts of how things are.
- Later Pyrrhonism (Sextus) is a developed school; do not simply identify it with the historical Pyrrho.
