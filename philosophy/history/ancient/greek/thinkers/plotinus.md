# Plotinus

**Prerequisites:** [Plato](plato.md); helpful: [Aristotle](aristotle.md)

## Name and dates

Plotinus (204/5–270 CE) was born in Egypt, studied at Alexandria under Ammonius Saccas, joined a failed imperial expedition east, and from about 245 taught in Rome. He is the central figure of what we now call **Neoplatonism**, a late-antique systematizing and spiritualizing of Plato (and of much Aristotle) that would shape pagan, Christian, Jewish, and Islamic philosophy for centuries. He is later than the Hellenistic schools; he belongs on this path as the last great ancient Greek metaphysical voice, not as a "Presocratic" or a classical Athenian.

## Sources

His student Porphyry edited the treatises into six groups of nine, the [*Enneads*](../texts/enneads.md), and wrote a *Life of Plotinus*. The treatises began as seminar discussions, later written out, and they are difficult, repetitious, and sometimes sublime. Porphyry's arrangement is thematic, not chronological. The *Life* also records a failed attempt to found a "Platonopolis" and a famous remark that Plotinus "seemed ashamed of being in a body."

## Thought

Reality, for Plotinus, is a graded procession from a first principle, **the One** (or the Good), beyond being and beyond description. From the One, without any diminishing of it, proceeds **[Intellect](../../../../glossary/greek/nous.md)** (*Nous*), which is at once a mind and the realm of Forms — Plato's intelligibles now thought as the inner life of a single Intellect. From Intellect proceeds **Soul**, which can look up toward Intellect or out toward the sensible world, which Soul helps to order. Matter stands at the far edge, as a kind of impoverishment or darkness, not a rival god. This is [emanation](../../../../glossary/general/emanation.md) (or procession) and return: everything comes from the One and can turn back toward it. The movement is not a story in time, like a creation date, but a structure of dependence that is always so.

The ethical and spiritual point is union, or at least likeness, with the source. The soul must purify itself of excessive entanglement with the body and ascend, by virtue, by dialectic, and by a turning inward that Plotinus describes with extraordinary care. He reports, in Porphyry's life, a few experiences of union with the One. Mysticism here is not the abandonment of philosophy; it is what the metaphysics says a soul is for.

He argues with [materialists](democritus-and-leucippus.md), with [Aristotle](aristotle.md) (while using him), with Gnostic contempt for the sensible world (the world, for Plotinus, is still beautiful, an image of Intellect), and with any view that would make the first principle a mind that thinks, for thinking already implies a duality of thinker and thought. The One is prior even to that.

## Reception

Through Porphyry, Iamblichus, Proclus, and the later Academy, Neoplatonism became the common high philosophy of late antiquity. Augustine read Plotinus (in Latin translation) at a decisive point in his own thought. Islamic and medieval Latin philosophers inherited versions of this hierarchy. For this encyclopedia, Plotinus is the great late ancient attempt to make [Plato](plato.md)'s Good, Forms, and soul into one structured picture of reality and of the soul's return.

## Key ideas

- The One, Intellect, and Soul are a hierarchy of procession and return.
- Forms live in Intellect; the sensible world is an image, not a waste.
- Philosophy is also an ascent of the soul, not only a theory of what there is.
