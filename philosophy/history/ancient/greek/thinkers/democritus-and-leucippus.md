# Democritus and Leucippus

**Prerequisites:** [Parmenides](parmenides.md) and [Zeno of Elea](zeno-of-elea.md)

## Names and dates

Leucippus (fifth century BCE), perhaps of Abdera or Miletus, is a shadowy founder. Democritus of Abdera (c. 460–370 BCE), a younger contemporary of [Socrates](socrates.md), is the figure we can say more about: a prolific writer, a traveler in later stories, nicknamed "the laughing philosopher" in a tradition that may be more emblem than fact. Ancient authors sometimes treat the two as a single atomic school; modern scholars often cannot cleanly divide which doctrines belong to whom. This file therefore treats them together.

## Sources

Their many books are lost. We depend on fragments (more ethical maxims than physical arguments, oddly) and on reports from Aristotle, who took atomism seriously enough to argue with it at length, from later Epicureans, and from doxographers. [Epicurus](epicurus.md) inherited and revised the system; much of what later antiquity knew of atoms came through that channel.

## Thought

The package is a direct reply to Eleatic pressure. **What-is** (atoms, *atomoi*, "uncuttables") is eternal, solid, ungenerated, and indivisible. **What-is-not** (the void) is real enough to allow motion and plurality. Without empty space, Parmenides and Zeno would be right that motion is unthinkable. With it, the many can move, collide, hook, and rebound. Macroscopic change is rearrangement, not coming-to-be from nothing. Worlds form when atoms, moving in the void, gather into a swirl; they also dissolve. There may be infinitely many worlds.

Qualities such as color, taste, and temperature are, in a famous report, matters of convention or of effects on us; atoms themselves have shape, size, arrangement, and motion. This is an ancestor of the later distinction between primary and secondary qualities. The soul, too, is atomic — fine, mobile atoms — so thought and sensation have a physical basis, and death is the dispersal of that configuration, not a journey.

Ethics in the surviving Democritean maxims prizes cheerfulness (*euthymia*), measure, and a mind not thrown about by fear and luck. The tightest ethical use of the physics will come with Epicurus: if the soul disperses, death is nothing to us, and fear of the gods' meddling is a mistake.

## Reception

Aristotle rejected a void and actual indivisibles, and he thought atomism could not explain form, purpose, or the real structure of living things. Plato barely mentions the school by name, though some readers see a shadow of it in later dialogues. Epicureanism made atomism a complete way of life. In the early modern period, a revived atomism (or corpuscularianism) would help shape the new science, at a long historical distance and with many changes. For this path, the first-principles achievement is already clear: a way to grant Parmenides stable being *and* save a world of motion, at the cost of admitting the void and reducing quality to arrangement.

## Key ideas

- Atoms and void: unchanging beings plus empty space make motion possible.
- Change is rearrangement; sensible qualities are effects, not features of atoms themselves.
- The soul is atomic, which later supports an ethics that removes the fear of death.
