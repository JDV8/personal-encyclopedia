# Epicurus

**Prerequisites:** [Democritus and Leucippus](democritus-and-leucippus.md) and the [Hellenistic schools survey](../06-hellenistic-schools.md)

## Name and dates

Epicurus (341–270 BCE) was born on Samos to Athenian parents, taught in several cities, and around 307 founded the Garden in Athens, a philosophical community that included women and enslaved persons among its members — unusual in the landscape of the schools. He wrote voluminously. Almost all of it is lost. What remains are three letters (to Herodotus, Pythocles, and Menoeceus), a collection of *Principal Doctrines*, the *Vatican Sayings*, and a mass of later testimony, especially from Lucretius's Latin poem *De rerum natura* and from critics such as Cicero.

## Thought

The physics is a revised [atomism](../../../../concepts/metaphysics/atoms-and-void.md). Reality is atoms and void; the gods, if they exist, are not administrators of human life and are not to be feared; the soul is atomic and dissolves at death, so death is "nothing to us." Worlds come to be and pass away without providence. A distinctive Epicurean addition, in later reports, is the slight **swerve** (*[clinamen](../../../../glossary/greek/clinamen.md)*) of atoms, introduced to avoid a closed determinism and to make room for freedom — a doctrine we know more from Lucretius than from Epicurus's own surviving words, and which remains interpretively contested.

The point of the physics is ethical. The good is **pleasure**, but not the luxury or frenzy of the caricature. The highest pleasure is the absence of bodily pain (*aponia*) and of mental disturbance (*[ataraxia](../../../../glossary/greek/ataraxia.md)*). Desires are to be sorted: natural and necessary ones (food, shelter, friendship) can be satisfied simply; many social desires (status, extravagant wealth) cannot be satisfied at all and should be dropped. Friendship is central to the Garden. Justice is a useful contract not to harm or be harmed, not a Form and not a cosmic law.

Where the Stoic says virtue is the only good, Epicurus says virtue is valuable because it helps produce a tranquil life. The argument is over what is for the sake of what. His letter to Menoeceus is the best short primary text: a handbook for not postponing the care of one's life, and for treating philosophy as therapy rather than display.

## Reception

The school lasted for centuries and was persistently slandered as coarse hedonism — a charge already answered in the surviving letters. Lucretius gave the physics its greatest poem. Early Christian writers often treated Epicureanism as the enemy (no providence, no immortal soul). Modern readers have returned to him as a philosopher of mortality, friendship, and the disciplined reduction of fear. For this path, he is the most complete ethical use of atomism, and a rival to the Stoa on the question of what a good life is for.

## Key ideas

- Atoms and void, without providence; death is dispersal, hence nothing to us.
- Pleasure as freedom from pain and disturbance, not as endless stimulation.
- Philosophy is a therapy of fear; friendship and simple desires do most of the work.
