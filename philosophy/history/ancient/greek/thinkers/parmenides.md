# Parmenides

**Prerequisites:** [Foundational concepts](../01-foundational-concepts.md) and [Heraclitus](heraclitus.md)

## Name and dates

Parmenides of Elea (in southern Italy, early fifth century BCE) wrote a poem in hexameter in which a unnamed youth is taken by chariot to a goddess, who instructs him in the way of truth and warns him off the way of mortal opinion. The form matters. This is philosophy in the clothing of a religious revelation, and it is the most consequential metaphysical argument of early Greek thought.

## Sources

Substantial fragments of the poem survive, especially of the "Way of Truth," quoted by later authors (Simplicius above all, who copied long stretches). The "Way of Opinion," which seems to have offered a cosmology of light and night, is more damaged. We can reconstruct the core argument better than we can reconstruct his life. Plato's dialogue [*Parmenides*](../texts/parmenides.md) is a later, searching tribute, not a biography.

## Thought

The goddess requires a choice. One way says that **it is, and it cannot not be**. The other says that it is not, or that it both is and is not. The second way is closed: you cannot know or point out what is not. From "it is" a series of signs follows. What-is is ungenerated and imperishable (if it came to be, it would have come from what is not). It is whole, one, continuous, and unmoving. It is complete, like a well-rounded ball, equal in every way, not more here and less there. Coming-to-be, passing-away, motion, and genuine plurality are, on this strict path, unthinkable.

This is a shock to the Milesian project and to ordinary experience. If the argument holds, the changing world of the senses cannot be the object of knowledge. At best it is the content of *doxa*, opinion, which the poem's second part may describe without endorsing as truth.

Two cautions. First, what "is" (*to eon*, *einai*) means here is itself a problem: existence, identity, predicative being, or a massy "being" as a subject? Different readings of Parmenides turn on that question. Second, the poem may be a training in what follows from a certain path, not a simple denial that the everyday world is there. Even so, later Greek philosophy has to answer him, sidestep him, or split reality into levels.

## Reception

His student [Zeno of Elea](zeno-of-elea.md) designed paradoxes meant to show the cost of denying the Eleatic one. [Atomists](democritus-and-leucippus.md) will save change by admitting a real void (what-is-not, in one sense) and unchanging atoms (what-is). [Plato](plato.md) will keep a stable object of knowledge (the Forms) and a changing sensible world that participates in them. [Aristotle](aristotle.md) will relocate stability in substances and in [potentiality and actuality](../../../../concepts/metaphysics/potentiality-and-actuality.md). Almost every later metaphysics in this tradition is, among other things, a reply to Parmenides.

## Key ideas

- What-is is, and cannot not be; what-is-not cannot be thought or said.
- On the way of truth, being is one, ungenerated, unchanging, and complete.
- The changing world is, at most, the way of opinion — and later philosophy must explain how that is possible.
