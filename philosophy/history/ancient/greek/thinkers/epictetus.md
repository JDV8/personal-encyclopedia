# Epictetus

**Prerequisites:** [Zeno of Citium](zeno-of-citium.md)

## Name and dates

Epictetus (c. 50–135 CE) was born into slavery in Hierapolis (in today's Turkey), brought to Rome, and later freed. He studied Stoicism under Musonius Rufus, was expelled from Rome with other philosophers under Domitian, and taught at Nicopolis in Epirus. His own body, including a lame leg, is part of the tradition of his teaching: philosophy is training under conditions you did not choose. He wrote nothing for publication.

## Sources

What we have is owed to his student Arrian, who composed the *Discourses* (*Diatribai*) from lecture notes and extracted the short [*Enchiridion*](../texts/enchiridion.md) (the "Handbook"). How close the Greek is to Epictetus's spoken words is a standing question; the voice is consistent and severe enough to treat as a single teacher. The *Enchiridion* is the best first text; the *Discourses* are the fuller room.

## Thought

The opening move of the *Handbook* is the whole method: some things are up to us, and some are not. Up to us are judgment, impulse, desire and aversion — the uses of impressions. Not up to us are body, property, reputation, and office. Happiness is not arranged by changing the second list. It is arranged by placing our good only in the first. This is the Roman Stoic discipline of [*prohairesis*](../../../../glossary/greek/prohairesis.md), the moral purpose or faculty of choice, as the true self.

An impression appears; you need not assent. "It is not the thing that disturbs us, but the view we take of it" is the popular wording of a stricter claim: disturbance is in the judgment that an external is truly good or evil. *Apatheia* is freedom from those false judgments, not the absence of all feeling. Roles — son, citizen, dinner guest — still have a "what is fitting," but they do not rewrite what the good is.

The theology is Stoic and often intimate: Zeus, the god within, the kinship of humans as fragments of the same *logos*. Epictetus can sound like a preacher. He is also a technician of attention, closer to a trainer than to a metaphysician. Slavery and freedom, in his mouth, are first of all names for the condition of the *prohairesis*. That does not make legal slavery just, and it should not be used as if it did. It does explain why a formerly enslaved teacher became the most exacting voice of inner independence in the school.

## Reception

The *Handbook* was adopted, adapted, and even Christianized (a later *Enchiridion* with God replacing Zeus). It has had a continuous life as a short book of exercises, from late antiquity through early modern Europe to contemporary "practical Stoicism." The risk of the afterlife is to keep the exercises and drop the claim about what is truly good. Read him first as a Stoic, not as a life-hack.

## Key ideas

- Some things are up to us; the good lives only there.
- Training is the withholding of assent from impressions that smuggle in false value.
- The *Enchiridion* and *Discourses* are lecture-born texts, recorded by Arrian.
