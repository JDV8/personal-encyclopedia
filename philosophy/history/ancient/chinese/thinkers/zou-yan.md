## Name and dates

Zou Yan (also Tsou Yen, probably late fourth to early third century BCE) was a thinker from the state of Qi, associated in later memory with the Jixia academy, and the name most often attached to a systematic early use of **yin-yang** and the **Five Phases** (*[wuxing](../../../../glossary/chinese/wuxing.md)*) as a way to organize history and the cosmos. He is more a hinge than a man we can really know.

## Sources

No book of his survives. We depend on reports in Sima Qian's *Shiji* and in later encyclopedic and cosmological writing. The reports credit him with a grand scheme of the world (a China surrounded by seas and other continents, far larger than the usual map) and with a theory that dynasties rise and fall according to a cycle of phases, each with its color, number, and emblem. How much of later Han correlative cosmology was already in Zou Yan, and how much was projected back onto him, is uncertain. Treat "Zou Yan" as the traditional founder-name of a toolkit, not as a recovered author.

## Thought

The toolkit is **[correlative cosmology](../../../../concepts/metaphysics/yin-yang-and-correlative-cosmology.md)**. Yin and yang are complementary processes, not a moral war of dark against light. The Five Phases — Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water — are phases of activity, not five lumps of matter, and they stand in generating and overcoming relations. Once you have those cycles, you can correlate them with seasons, directions, colors, organs, and, politically, with dynasties.

The political use is the charged one. If a dynasty is associated with a phase, then ritual color, calendar, and even the story of why the last house fell can be told as a cosmic succession rather than as mere force. That can be a language of legitimation, of omen, or of reform (the next phase must be prepared). Han thinkers such as Dong Zhongshu would later fold this apparatus together with Confucian *ren* and the [Mandate of Heaven](../../../../glossary/chinese/tianming.md). Medical and technical texts would use it without settling the Mencius–Xunzi debate.

Zou Yan, as reported, is the person who tried to make this a comprehensive, almost geographic and historical science of pattern, rather than a set of local correspondences.

## Reception

The "Yin-Yang school" is a bibliographic category as much as a movement. Sima Qian already treats Zou Yan as famous, slightly extravagant, and widely imitated. For this path, he is the named bridge between Warring States cosmology and the Han imperial synthesis — and a reminder that [Yin-Yang and the Five Phases](../08-yin-yang-and-five-phases.md) have a history, not only an atmosphere.

## Key ideas

- Traditional founder-name for a systematic Five-Phase and yin-yang historiography.
- No authentic book survives; later cosmology may have enlarged his role.
- The politically charged move is to cycle dynasties through cosmic phases.
