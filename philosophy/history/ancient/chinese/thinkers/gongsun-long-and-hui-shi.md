# Gongsun Long and Hui Shi (the School of Names)

**Prerequisites:** [Foundational concepts](../01-foundational-concepts.md); helpful: [Zhuangzi](zhuangzi.md) and [Mozi](mozi.md)

## Names and dates

The "School of Names" (*mingjia*) is a later label for a cluster of Warring States thinkers who specialized in disputation about names, kinds, sameness and difference, and paradox. The two outstanding figures are Hui Shi (Hui Shih, fourth century BCE), a friend and foil of [Zhuangzi](zhuangzi.md), and Gongsun Long (late fourth to early third century BCE), author of the notorious claim that "a white horse is not a horse." They are not a school with a founder and a church. They are the early Chinese specialists in what this encyclopedia would put under [logic](../../../../concepts/logic/paradox-and-the-school-of-names.md) and the theory of [names and actuality](../../../../concepts/epistemology/names-and-actuality.md).

## Sources

Hui Shi's own writings are lost. We know his "ten theses" (the earth is so small and so large; the sun is at noon and declining; a thing is both shortly after being born already dying; and so on) mainly from the *Zhuangzi*, which buries him with affection and mockery. Gongsun Long is represented by the short, difficult [*Gongsun Longzi*](../texts/gongsun-longzi.md), of which the "White Horse" and "Pointing and Things" (or "Zhiwu") chapters are the most discussed; some chapters may be later. The later [Mohist canons](../../../../concepts/logic/later-mohist-canons.md) are a related, more systematic attempt at the same terrain.

## Thought

Hui Shi seems to have pressed **relativity and the instability of distinctions**: large and small, now and then, sameness and difference depend on standpoint and scale. If you push hard enough, "the ten thousand things are one." Zhuangzi is close to this and also wary of it — wary of winning at disputation, and of letting a thesis become a new trap.

Gongsun Long's **white horse is not a horse** is not (if we read him generously) a joke about painted horses. It is a claim about how names work: "horse" names a shape, "white" a color, "white horse" a combination that is not identical with the name "horse." To use names carefully is to refuse a slide that ordinary speech makes all the time. Other arguments (a "hard white" stone, the pointing at things) similarly try to prize apart what is mixed in perception and in language.

Whether this is serious logic, verbal trickery, or a political-adjacent training in standards (names that do not match realities are a governmental problem, as [Confucius](confucius.md) already knew) is debated. The first-principles yield is clear: early China has a tradition that makes **the fit between names and stuff** into an object of technical argument, not only of moral exhortation.

## Reception

They were often dismissed as sophists, a reception that resembles the fate of the Greek [Sophists](../../greek/thinkers/protagoras-and-the-sophists.md) without being the same movement. [Xunzi](xunzi.md) attacks name-trickery while offering his own theory of names. Modern philosophy of language has returned to the white-horse debate with sometimes anachronistic tools and sometimes real light. Read them next to the Mohist canons and next to Zhuangzi's warnings about disputation.

## Key ideas

- Hui Shi: paradoxes of scale and relativity; the many as one.
- Gongsun Long: careful analysis of compound names, especially "white horse is not a horse."
- The School of Names is a later label for a real early interest in language and kinds.
