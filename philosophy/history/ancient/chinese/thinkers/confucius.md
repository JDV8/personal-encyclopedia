# Confucius

**Prerequisites:** [Foundational concepts](../01-foundational-concepts.md) and the survey [Confucius and the *Analects*](../02-confucius-and-the-analects.md)

## Name and dates

Kongzi (Master Kong, 551–479 BCE), latinized as Confucius, was a teacher and sometime minor official from the state of Lu, in a world where the Zhou ritual order was already fraying. Like [Socrates](../../greek/thinkers/socrates.md), he left no systematic treatise in his own hand. The person we meet is already a tradition: a transmitter of older ways who, in transmitting them, remade what a human life and a government could be.

## Sources

The [*Analects*](../texts/analects.md) (*Lunyu*) is a compiled record of sayings and conversations, assembled by later disciples and their disciples. Traditional reverence treats it as a faithful window on the master. Modern scholarship sees layers and editorial shaping across the Warring States and into the early empire. Other texts later associated with him (parts of the *Spring and Autumn Annals* tradition, or ritual books) are even more complicated in authorship. This overview takes the received *Analects* as the core, without pretending we have a stenographic record.

## Thought

Two terms carry the ethics. **[*Ren*](../../../../glossary/chinese/ren.md)** (humaneness, a full human-heartedness) is the central virtue: a cultivated concern for others, grown from family feeling and extended outward, expressed in the rule of not imposing on others what you do not want for yourself. Confucius is stingy with the word; it is an achievement, not a mood. **[*Li*](../../../../glossary/chinese/li-ritual.md)** is ritual, ceremony, and the inherited forms of appropriate conduct. Form trains feeling. Without *ren*, ritual is empty; without *li*, humaneness has no reliable shape. Filial piety (*[xiao](../../../../glossary/chinese/xiao.md)*) is the first school of this structure.

The **[*junzi*](../../../../glossary/chinese/junzi.md)**, once a noble by birth, becomes an ethical title: the exemplary person, as against the small person who calculates advantage. Education — study, reflection, the correction of friends — is the path. Asked what he would do first in office, he says he would **[rectify names](../../../../concepts/epistemology/names-and-actuality.md)** (*zhengming*): if roles and words come apart, government cannot hold.

Government at its best works by *De*, moral power, not by punishments that make people merely evasive. He presents himself as a transmitter, not an innovator, looking back to early Zhou sage-kings. That conservatism is also a reform program: the present is judged by a moral past, and almost no actual ruler measures up.

## Reception

After a period of rivalry with [Mohists](mozi.md), [Daoist](laozi.md) texts, and [Legalists](han-feizi.md), the *Ru* tradition became, from the Han onward, the main official philosophy of imperial China, reread in every dynasty. [Mencius](mencius.md) and [Xunzi](xunzi.md) split over human nature while claiming him. [Zhu Xi](../../../imperial/chinese/thinkers/zhu-xi.md) later fixed the *Analects* among the Four Books. He is not "Chinese thought" by himself; he is the most consequential teacher in one argumentative family.

## Key ideas

- *Ren* and *li* are paired: feeling and form, humaneness and ritual.
- The *junzi* is made by learning; names and roles must match.
- The *Analects* is a layered record, not a single-authored book.
