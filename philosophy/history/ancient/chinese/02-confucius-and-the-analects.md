# Confucius and the *Analects*

**Prerequisites:** [Foundational concepts](01-foundational-concepts.md)
**Key ideas:** a teacher known through recorded sayings; *ren* (humaneness) and *li* (ritual propriety) as paired first principles; the *junzi* as an ethical ideal; rectification of names; government by moral power rather than force.

Kongzi (551–479 BCE), latinized as Confucius, was a teacher and sometime minor official from the state of Lu. Like [Socrates](../greek/03-socrates.md), he left no systematic treatise in his own hand. The *Analects* (*Lunyu*) is a compiled record of sayings and conversations, assembled by later disciples and disciples of disciples. Traditional reverence treats it as a faithful window on the master. Modern scholarship sees layers and editorial shaping. Either way, this is the book that made "Confucian" a tradition.

## *Ren* and *li*

Two terms carry most of the ethics.

***Ren*** is often translated "humaneness," "benevolence," or "goodness." In the *Analects* it is the central virtue of a fully human life: a committed concern for others, rooted in family feeling and extended outward, expressed in the "negative golden rule" — do not impose on others what you do not want for yourself. *Ren* is not a mood. It is a cultivated disposition, hard enough that Confucius is stingy about awarding the word to anyone, including himself.

***Li*** is ritual, ceremony, and, more broadly, the inherited forms of appropriate conduct: how one mourns, how one greets, how a ruler receives ministers, how a child serves parents. It is easy for modern readers to dismiss *li* as etiquette. That misses the claim. For Confucius, form trains feeling. Ritual is a technology of the self and of social harmony. Without *ren*, ritual is empty performance; without *li*, humaneness has no reliable shape.

Filial piety (*xiao*) sits at the base of this structure. Learning to be a son or daughter is the first school of *ren*. Critics (especially Mozi) will later say this starts too close to home and never becomes truly impartial. Confucians reply that love must be grown from where it actually starts, or it will be a slogan.

## The *junzi*

The *junzi*, once a term for a person of noble birth, becomes in the *Analects* an ethical title: the exemplary person, as opposed to the "small person" (*xiaoren*) who calculates advantage. The *junzi* is learned but not pedantic, firm but not rigid, concerned with rightness (*yi*) more than profit (*li*, a different word from ritual). Education is the path: study, reflection, and the company of others who correct you.

This is a meritocratic opening inside a hierarchical world. Birth still matters in practice; the ideal says that nobility is a way of being, not a bloodline. Much later imperial examination culture takes that hint and institutionalizes it, for better and worse.

## Names, government, and *De*

Asked what he would do first if given office, Confucius says he would **rectify names** (*zhengming*). If names and roles are disordered — if a "ruler" does not rule as a ruler should, if a "father" is not a father — language and reality come apart, and policy cannot hold. This is political philosophy in an epistemological key: standards of naming are standards of responsibility.

Government, at its best, works by *De*, moral power. "If you lead them with virtue and keep them in line with ritual, they will have a sense of shame and correct themselves." Law and punishment have a place, but they are second-best; they make people evasive rather than good. The model is the ruler who faces south, as the saying goes, and the world is in order — not because he does nothing in the lazy sense, but because his cultivated person sets a pattern others can inhabit.

Confucius presents himself as a transmitter, not an innovator, looking back to early Zhou ideals and to sage-kings. That conservatism is real. It is also a reform program: the present is judged by a moral past, and almost no actual ruler measures up. The *Analects* is less a constitution than a portrait of a kind of person, and a claim that states are only as good as those persons.
