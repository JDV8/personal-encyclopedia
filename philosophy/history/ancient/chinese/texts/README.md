# Chinese texts

One overview file for each major primary work on the ancient Chinese path, plus the *Changes* and the two short ritual chapters later canonized as Four Books. These are maps, not substitutes for the books.

**Prerequisites:** [Ancient Chinese philosophy](../README.md) and the matching [thinkers](../thinkers/README.md).

## Suggested reading order

1. Confucian core: [Analects](analects.md), [Mencius](mencius.md), [Xunzi](xunzi.md)
2. Daoist counterparts: [Daodejing](daodejing.md), [Zhuangzi](zhuangzi.md)
3. Rivals: [Mozi](mozi.md), [Gongsun Longzi](gongsun-longzi.md), [Book of Lord Shang](book-of-lord-shang.md), [Han Feizi](han-feizi.md)
4. Cosmology and later curriculum: [I Ching](i-ching.md), [Great Learning](great-learning.md), [Doctrine of the Mean](doctrine-of-the-mean.md)

Then the later readers: [Zhu Xi](../../../imperial/chinese/thinkers/zhu-xi.md) and [Wang Yangming](../../../imperial/chinese/thinkers/wang-yangming.md).

## References & further reading

- Philip J. Ivanhoe and Bryan W. Van Norden, eds., *Readings in Classical Chinese Philosophy* (Beginner). The best first anthology beside these overviews.
- Edward Slingerland, trans., *Confucius: Analects* (Beginner). A translation with traditional commentary, useful for first reading.
- Burton Watson, trans., *The Complete Works of Zhuangzi* and his selections from *Xunzi* and *Han Feizi* (Beginner). Readable, widely used English versions.
- D. C. Lau, trans., *Tao Te Ching* and *Mencius* (Beginner). Careful, compact translations.
- Richard John Lynn, trans., *The Classic of Changes* (Intermediate). The *Yijing* with Wang Bi's commentary.
- Daniel K. Gardner, trans., *The Four Books* (Beginner). Shows the *Great Learning* and *Doctrine of the Mean* in their later curriculum form.
- A. C. Graham, *Disputers of the Tao* (Advanced). Historical and philosophical context for the whole set.
