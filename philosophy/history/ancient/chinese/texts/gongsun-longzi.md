# *Gongsun Longzi*

**Prerequisites:** [Gongsun Long and Hui Shi](../thinkers/gongsun-long-and-hui-shi.md)

## Dating and authorship

A short, difficult collection attributed to [Gongsun Long](../thinkers/gongsun-long-and-hui-shi.md). The "White Horse" chapter is the most famous and the most likely to be early; other chapters (including "Pointing and Things") are harder and in some cases suspected of later composition or corruption. There is no long, easy narrative here. The book is a set of disputation texts.

## Outline and key ideas

"A white horse is not a horse" is the emblem. The argument, in generous readings, is about how names work: "horse" picks out a shape, "white" a color, "white horse" a combination that is not identical with the name "horse." Ordinary speech slides these together; the disputant refuses the slide. Other chapters pry apart "hard" and "white" in a stone, or worry about the relation of pointing (*zhi*) to things. Whether this is a serious logic of predication, a political-adjacent training in the "rectification of names," or a mix of insight and trick is still argued. Read it next to the later [Mohist canons](../../../../concepts/logic/later-mohist-canons.md) and next to [Zhuangzi](zhuangzi.md)'s satire of disputation.

## Reception

Often dismissed as sophistry, then recovered by modern philosophy of language, sometimes with anachronistic tools. It is a small book with a large afterlife in the problem of [names and actuality](../../../../concepts/epistemology/names-and-actuality.md).

## Key ideas

- A short, uneven corpus; "White Horse" is the center.
- Compound names are not simply identical with their parts or with a more general name.
- Best read with the Mohist canons and with Zhuangzi, not in isolation.
