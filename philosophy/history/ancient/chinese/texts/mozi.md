# *Mozi*

**Prerequisites:** [Mozi](../thinkers/mozi.md) and [*Analects*](analects.md)

## Dating and authorship

A corpus in the name of [Mozi](../thinkers/mozi.md) and his school: ethical-political essays, later technical "canons" on language and inference, and chapters on defensive warfare and fortification. It was compiled over more than one generation. The essays on impartial care, Heaven's will, and the campaigns against aggression, fatalism, and lavish ritual and music are the first reading. The canons are harder and later. There is no complete, easy English *Mozi*; Ian Johnston's translation is the fullest recent attempt, and earlier selections (Watson, Ivanhoe and Van Norden's reader) are better for a first pass through the ethical chapters.

## Outline and key ideas

The ethical essays argue, often in a repeating, almost oral structure, that doctrines should be tested by their benefit to the world and by the model of Heaven and the sage-kings. [*Jian ai*](../../../../concepts/ethics/impartial-care.md) (impartial care) is the signature claim: partiality produces war and robbery; caring for others' states as one's own is the remedy. Heaven's will endorses this standard. Aggressive war is condemned as a scaled-up crime; defensive war is another matter, and the military chapters are practical. Lavish funerals and music waste the people's wealth. Fatalism saps effort. The later canons, by contrast, are terse, technical, and damaged; they classify names, kinds, and ways of agreeing and inferring, and they are a main exhibit for early Chinese [logic](../../../../concepts/logic/later-mohist-canons.md).

## Reception

The organized Mohist movement faded after the early empire, and the book was neglected relative to the Confucian and Daoist classics. Modern interest in impartiality, just war, and the canons has brought it back. Read it as the *Analects*' most organized early rival, not as a footnote.

## Key ideas

- A school corpus: ethical essays first, canons and defense chapters later.
- Impartial care, Heaven's will, and tests by benefit organize the ethics.
- The later canons are a technical logic of names and inference.
