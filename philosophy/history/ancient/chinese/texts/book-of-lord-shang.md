# *Book of Lord Shang* (*Shangjunshu*)

**Prerequisites:** [Shang Yang](../thinkers/shang-yang.md)

## Dating and authorship

A collection of essays in the name of [Shang Yang](../thinkers/shang-yang.md) (d. 338 BCE), the Qin reformer. Some chapters may be close to his milieu; others are later Legalist writing under his prestige. Yuri Pines's recent translation and study is the best scholarly English entry. The book should be read as a school text as well as a window on Qin policy, not as a signed autobiography.

## Outline and key ideas

The essays return to a few harsh, clear claims. Agriculture and war are the two handles of a strong state. Law must be public, uniform, and believed. Rank should follow meritorious service, not hereditary privilege. The wandering talkers of the Hundred Schools are a drain. Institutions must **change with the times**; imitating the former kings is a mistake when conditions have changed — an explicit anti-traditionalism aimed at the Confucian love of antiquity. The tone is unapologetic about using the people as a resource of grain and soldiers.

## Reception

[Han Feizi](../thinkers/han-feizi.md) treats Shang Yang as a source for *fa*. Later memory made the book a symbol of cruel efficiency, and Qin's brief empire after unification gave that memory a historical hook. It is one of the hardest political texts in this encyclopedia, and one of the most important for understanding how Qin was imagined to have worked.

## Key ideas

- A Legalist school text around Shang Yang's reforms, of mixed date.
- Farm, war, public law, and institutional change against traditionalism.
- A theory of state strength, not of the people's flourishing as an end.
