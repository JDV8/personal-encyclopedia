# Ancient philosophy

By the middle of the first millennium BCE, two of the world's most durable philosophical traditions were already underway, largely out of contact with each other.

In the Greek-speaking world, inquiry shifted from mythic theogony toward arguments about nature, being, knowledge, and the good life. That shift is not a clean break with religion or poetry — Parmenides wrote in verse, and Plato never stopped using myth — but it does introduce a style of giving reasons that later European philosophy treats as ancestral.

In China, the late Spring and Autumn and Warring States periods (roughly the sixth to third centuries BCE) produced the "Hundred Schools": rival answers to the breakdown of Zhou political and ritual order. The leading questions were often practical and political first, but they carried metaphysics and epistemology with them.

These are not the only ancient traditions (India alone would require its own branch). They are the two requested as a first pass, and they repay being read side by side.
