# 3. Study designs and the evidence hierarchy

**Prerequisites:** [Statistics for readers](../02-statistics/README.md), at least lessons 01–08.

The main kinds of study, from case reports to randomised trials and meta-analyses, what each can and cannot show, and how evidence is graded. Includes designs used to monitor safety, such as self-controlled case series and passive surveillance.

## Contents

1. [The evidence hierarchy](01-the-evidence-hierarchy.md): The evidence hierarchy ranks designs by protection against bias for causal questions.
2. [Case reports and case series](02-case-reports-and-case-series.md): Case reports and series describe patients without a comparison group.
3. [Ecological studies](03-ecological-studies.md): Ecological studies compare groups, not individuals.
4. [Cross-sectional studies](04-cross-sectional-studies.md): Cross-sectional studies are snapshots measuring exposure and outcome at the same time.
5. [Case-control studies](05-case-control-studies.md): Case-control studies start from the outcome and look back at exposures.
6. [Cohort studies](06-cohort-studies.md): Cohort studies follow people from exposure to outcome.
7. [Randomised controlled trials](07-randomised-controlled-trials.md): RCTs assign treatments by chance, balancing known and unknown factors.
8. [Blinding, placebos and allocation concealment](08-blinding-placebos-and-allocation-concealment.md): Allocation concealment prevents anyone steering patients at enrolment; it is always possible.
9. [Intention to treat and per-protocol analysis](09-intention-to-treat-and-per-protocol.md): Intention to treat analyses everyone as randomised and preserves the benefits of randomisation.
10. [Crossover trials](10-crossover-trials.md): In a crossover trial, each participant receives both treatments in random order.
11. [The self-controlled case series](11-self-controlled-case-series.md): The SCCS compares event rates in risk windows and control periods within the same people.
12. [Natural experiments and quasi-experiments](12-natural-experiments-and-quasi-experiments.md): Natural experiments exploit situations where exposure is assigned almost at random.
13. [Passive surveillance and pharmacovigilance](13-passive-surveillance-and-pharmacovigilance.md): Pharmacovigilance detects harms too rare for trials to find.
14. [Systematic reviews](14-systematic-reviews.md): Systematic reviews use explicit, reproducible methods to find and appraise all relevant studies.
15. [Meta-analysis](15-meta-analysis.md): Meta-analysis pools studies into a weighted average, giving more weight to precise studies.
16. [Cochrane and GRADE](16-cochrane-and-grade.md): Cochrane, founded in 1993, produces protocol-driven systematic reviews without commercial sponsorship.

## Suggested reading order

Start with 01 (the hierarchy). Then 02–06 (observational designs), 07–11 (trials and related designs), 12–13 (quasi-experiments and surveillance) and 14–16 (evidence synthesis).
