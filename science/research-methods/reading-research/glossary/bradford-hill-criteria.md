# Bradford Hill criteria

Nine considerations proposed by Austin Bradford Hill in 1965 for judging whether an observed association is likely to be causal: strength, consistency, specificity, temporality, biological gradient, plausibility, coherence, experiment and analogy. Hill stressed they were not rigid rules or a checklist, and that only temporality is strictly necessary for causation.
