# Immortal time bias

A bias in cohort studies in which a period of follow-up during which the outcome cannot occur, often the time before people start an exposure, is wrongly counted as exposed time. It makes exposures look protective. Classic examples include studies of Oscar winners' longevity and some drug studies.
