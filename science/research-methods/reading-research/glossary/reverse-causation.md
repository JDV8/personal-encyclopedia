# Reverse causation

When the supposed outcome actually causes the supposed exposure, rather than the other way round. For example, people who become ill may stop drinking alcohol, making non-drinkers look less healthy. Cohort designs that measure exposure before outcome, lag periods and natural experiments help address it.
