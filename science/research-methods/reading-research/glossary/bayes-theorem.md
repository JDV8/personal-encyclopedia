# Bayes' theorem

A rule for updating the probability of a hypothesis in light of new evidence. The posterior probability is proportional to the prior probability multiplied by the likelihood of the evidence under the hypothesis. In practice, it explains why a positive test for a rare disease is often a false positive, and why surprising findings need strong evidence.
