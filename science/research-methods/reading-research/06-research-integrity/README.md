# 6. Research integrity and the replication crisis

**Prerequisites:** [Statistics for readers](../02-statistics/README.md) and [Anatomy of a paper](../05-anatomy-of-a-paper/README.md).

How the research record gets distorted, and what is being done about it: preregistration, trial registries, outcome switching, p-hacking, HARKing, publication bias, the replication crisis, fraud and misconduct, and open science.

## Contents

1. [Preregistration](01-preregistration.md): Preregistration records hypotheses, outcomes and analysis plans before data are seen.
2. [Trial registries](02-trial-registries.md): Trial registries record trials before enrolment, making unpublished trials visible.
3. [Outcome switching](03-outcome-switching.md): Outcome switching reports outcomes different from those pre-specified, without acknowledgement.
4. [P-hacking and researcher degrees of freedom](04-p-hacking.md): P-hacking is selectively reporting analyses that produce significant results.
5. [HARKing](05-harking.md): HARKing presents a hypothesis formed after seeing the results as if it were planned.
6. [Publication bias](06-publication-bias.md): Publication bias makes the published literature unrepresentative of all research done.
7. [The replication crisis](07-the-replication-crisis.md): Large projects have found that many published findings, especially in psychology and preclinical biology, do not replicate.
8. [Fraud and misconduct](08-fraud-and-misconduct.md): Misconduct is fabrication, falsification or plagiarism; questionable practices are a larger grey zone.
9. [Open science](09-open-science.md): Open science makes papers, data, code, materials and plans available for scrutiny.

## Suggested reading order

01–03 (registration and outcomes), 04–06 (flexible analysis and selective publication), 07 (the replication crisis), 08 (fraud) and 09 (reforms).

## Key studies & sources

- Turner EH, Matthews AM, Linardatos E, et al. Selective publication of antidepressant trials and its influence on apparent efficacy. *NEJM* 2008;358:252–260. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsa065779). 94% of published trials looked positive versus 51% by FDA analysis.
- Goldacre B, Drysdale H, Dale A, et al. COMPare: a prospective cohort study correcting and monitoring 58 misreported trials in real time. *Trials* 2019;20:118. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13063-019-3173-2).
- Kaplan RM, Irvin VL. Likelihood of null effects of large NHLBI clinical trials has increased over time. *PLoS ONE* 2015;10:e0132382. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0132382). Positive results fell from 57% to 8% after mandatory registration.
- Open Science Collaboration. *Science* 2015;349:aac4716. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aac4716).
- Begley CG, Ellis LM. Raise standards for preclinical cancer research. *Nature* 2012;483:531–533. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1038/483531a). Amgen scientists confirmed 6 of 53 landmark findings; data not released.
- Fanelli D. How many scientists fabricate and falsify research? *PLoS ONE* 2009;4:e5738. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0005738). About 2% admitted fabrication or falsification.
- Kerr NL. HARKing: hypothesizing after the results are known. *Personality and Social Psychology Review* 1998;2:196–217. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327957pspr0203_4).
- Nosek BA, Ebersole CR, DeHaven AC, Mellor DT. The preregistration revolution. *PNAS* 2018;115:2600–2606. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1708274114).
