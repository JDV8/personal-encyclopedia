# 4. Causation and bias

**Prerequisites:** [Study designs](../03-study-designs/README.md).

How to get from association to causation: confounding, reverse causation, selection and survivorship bias, healthy-user effects, measurement and recall bias, time-related biases, the Bradford Hill considerations and causal diagrams.

## Contents

1. [Correlation and causation](01-correlation-and-causation.md): An association can arise from chance, bias, confounding, reverse causation or causation.
2. [Confounding](02-confounding.md): A confounder is associated with the exposure, affects the outcome and is not on the causal pathway.
3. [Reverse causation](03-reverse-causation.md): Reverse causation is when the outcome, or its early stages, causes the exposure.
4. [Selection bias](04-selection-bias.md): Selection bias arises from how people enter or leave a study or analysis.
5. [Survivorship bias](05-survivorship-bias.md): Survivorship bias comes from studying only cases that passed a selection process.
6. [The healthy user effect](06-healthy-user-bias.md): People who use preventive interventions differ systematically from those who do not.
7. [Measurement and recall bias](07-measurement-and-recall-bias.md): Measurement error (information bias) affects exposures and outcomes.
8. [Time-related biases](08-time-related-biases.md): Immortal time bias credits treated people with survival time they had to have to be treated.
9. [The Bradford Hill criteria](09-bradford-hill-criteria.md): Hill's 1965 address set out nine viewpoints for judging causation from associations.
10. [Causal diagrams](10-causal-diagrams.md): Causal diagrams (DAGs) draw causal assumptions as arrows between variables.

## Suggested reading order

01 and 02 first; 03–08 are a catalogue of biases that can be read in any order; finish with 09 and 10.

## Key studies & sources

- Hill AB. The environment and disease: association or causation? *Proceedings of the Royal Society of Medicine* 1965;58:295–300. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1177/003591576505800503). The source of the Bradford Hill considerations.
- Redelmeier DA, Singh SM. Survival in Academy Award-winning actors and actresses. *Annals of Internal Medicine* 2001;134:955–962. [doi](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-134-10-200105150-00009). Reported a 3.9-year survival advantage; later reanalysis by Sylvestre and colleagues (2006) accounting for immortal time found a much smaller, non-significant difference.
- Hernán MA, Alonso A, Logan R, et al. Observational studies analyzed like randomized experiments. *Epidemiology* 2008;19:766–779. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0b013e3181875e61). Healthy-user and prevalent-user bias in HRT studies.
- Honda H, Shimizu Y, Rutter M. No effect of MMR withdrawal on the incidence of autism: a total population study. *Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry* 2005;46:572–579. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7610.2005.01425.x). Autism diagnoses kept rising after MMR use stopped in Yokohama's Kohoku Ward.
