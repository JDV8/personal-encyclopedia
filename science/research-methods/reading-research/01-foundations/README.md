# 1. Foundations

**Prerequisites:** None. Start here.

What science is, why it works, and why reading research is harder than it looks. These lessons set up the ideas (falsifiability, measurement, the social norms of science and how claims become accepted knowledge) that the rest of the course relies on.

## Contents

1. [What science is](01-what-science-is.md): Science is a method for catching errors, not a list of facts.
2. [Falsifiability, hypotheses and theories](02-falsifiability-and-theories.md): A hypothesis is a testable proposal; a theory is a well-tested explanatory framework; a law describes a regularity.
3. [Observation, measurement and experiment](03-observation-measurement-and-experiment.md): Measurement must be valid and reliable; always check how the outcome was measured.
4. [The norms of science](04-norms-of-science.md): Merton's norms: share findings, judge claims impersonally, serve the enterprise, scrutinise everything.
5. [How a claim becomes knowledge](05-how-a-claim-becomes-knowledge.md): Claims travel a pipeline: lab, animals, phased human trials, regulation, surveillance, synthesis.
6. [Why reading research is hard](06-why-reading-research-is-hard.md): Confirmation bias and motivated reasoning make us scrutinise unwelcome studies harder than welcome ones.

## Key studies & sources

- Ioannidis JPA. Why most published research findings are false. *PLoS Medicine* 2005;2(8):e124. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.0020124). A modelling essay arguing that small studies, small effects and flexible analyses make many positive findings false; influential, though its assumptions have been debated.
- Open Science Collaboration. Estimating the reproducibility of psychological science. *Science* 2015;349:aac4716. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aac4716). Replicated 100 psychology studies: 97% of originals but 36% of replications were significant.
- Smith GCS, Pell JP. Parachute use to prevent death and major trauma related to gravitational challenge: systematic review of randomised controlled trials. *BMJ* 2003;327:1459. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.327.7429.1459). A satirical review making the point that some effects are obvious without trials.
- Hróbjartsson A, Gøtzsche PC. Is the placebo powerless? *New England Journal of Medicine* 2001;344:1594–1602. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM200105243442106). Systematic review of trials with no-treatment groups, finding little evidence of large placebo effects except on subjective outcomes such as pain.
