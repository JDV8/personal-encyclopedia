# 9. From lab to headline

**Prerequisites:** [Causation and bias](../04-causation/README.md) and [Funding and conflicts](../08-funding-and-conflicts/README.md).

How findings change on the way from the laboratory to the news: press releases and science journalism, surrogate endpoints, animal and cell studies, subgroups and secondary analyses, consensus and dissent, and how much weight a single study deserves.

## Contents

1. [Press releases and science journalism](01-press-releases-and-science-journalism.md): Most people encounter research through press releases and news, which often exaggerate.
2. [Surrogate endpoints](02-surrogate-endpoints.md): Surrogate endpoints are measures expected to predict clinical outcomes; they make trials faster and smaller.
3. [Animal and in vitro studies](03-animal-and-in-vitro-studies.md): In vitro and animal studies are essential for mechanisms, screening and safety but often fail to translate.
4. [Subgroups and secondary analyses](04-subgroups-and-secondary-analyses.md): Subgroup analyses ask whether treatment effects differ between groups of participants.
5. [Consensus, dissent and experts](05-consensus-dissent-and-experts.md): Scientific consensus is the collective judgement of experts based on converging evidence.
6. [Single studies and the weight of evidence](06-single-studies-and-the-weight-of-evidence.md): A single study is one data point; chance, bias and specificity make it fragile.

## Suggested reading order

Any order; 06 (weight of evidence) works well last.
